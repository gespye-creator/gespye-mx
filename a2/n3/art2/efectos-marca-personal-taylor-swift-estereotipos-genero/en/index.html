Effects of Taylor Swift's personal brand on gender stereotypes

Efectos de la marca personal de Taylor Swift en los estereotipos de género

Abstract.

Gender stereotypes in the sports field play a determining role in the thinking and acting of female audiences interested in sports. The objective of this research is to explain the effect that Taylor Swift's presence at Super Bowl LVIII had, with respect to gender stereotypes associated with American football audiences. A qualitative analysis is carried out through focus groups with undergraduate students who are fans of the artist, but not of this sport. The situations that motivated them to continue the transmission were identified. The results reflect that her interest in this event was a result of the singer's presence as well as the halftime show and not the event itself.

Keywords:

Gender stereotypes; female audiences; public figure; football.

Introduction

In recent years Taylor Swift has been a topic of discussion in different spheres; however, since 2020, the reach of her personal image has drawn the interest of marketing and communication research. Some of this work relates to her songwriting style and lyrics (Sloan, 2021), the role of her music in advertising (Ruth and Spangardt, 2017), the social influence of her fan base, called swifties (Prego Nieto, 2018), and, more recently, the artist's role within the National Football League (NFL) (Morgan, 2023).

Taylor Swift's presence and popularity in traditional and digital media have allowed her to establish herself as a globally recognized artist. Research conducted by Morning Consult established that Swift's fandom is currently one of the most intense and galvanizing social forces in the United States (Briggs, 2024), giving rise to what is known as the Taylor Swift effect, which refers to the full extent of mass influence Taylor has over her fans. Valles (2024) illustrates this fact, noting that wherever the artist performs or visits, the number of tourists to that destination increases, and, together with her rebranding, she manages to break with traditional feminine stereotypes, such as, for example, her image change (Becerril, 2023).

Recently, the artist's presence in the world's most important American football league, the NFL, has become a focus of attention because this sport is considered masculine and it is noted that few women enjoy this activity (Esmonde et al., 2018); moreover, it is recognized that, by nature, it is intense and a territory where masculine physical supremacy and superiority predominate, in which women appear to have no place on the field. The Super Bowl, the final game of the NFL season, has established itself as the sporting event with the largest audience worldwide; however, this past Super Bowl, with Taylor Swift's involvement due to her relationship with Travis Kelce, one of the players for the Kansas City Chiefs — one of the finalist teams — broadened the reach and follower profile of this sporting event (Honeyshah, 2023), which further increased anticipation and therefore motivated viewership of the game.

The event's rise in popularity was reflected across various digital and traditional media outlets. According to PALCO 23 (2024), during 2023, the Super Bowl broadcast recorded 123.7 million viewers, making it the highest in NFL history; these figures reveal that 58.8 million of those viewers were women, representing a 9% increase over the previous year. Additionally, it was identified, through advertising content and social-media effects, that this event brought daughters and fathers closer together, fostering better family bonds, creating an experience that combined both interests — on one hand, Taylor Swift's fans, and on the other, American football fans.

These circumstances suggest that Taylor Swift, as a personal brand, is capable of modifying the behaviors, perceptions, tastes, and interests of her fan base, which, even though they are not followers of this high-contact sport, brought women in particular closer to an event traditionally followed by men. In this way, the influence an artist exerts within advertising-campaign settings creates an appeal to the audience, through which one can observe the physical attributes and behaviors aspired to both by those who follow her and by those who generate content about her (Gutiérrez, 2023), such that the Taylor Swift brand can elevate social prestige and even modify cultural aspects due to the process of adoption of, or influence by, the brand itself (Briggs, 2024).

In this sense, this research project seeks to identify how Taylor Swift's presence — recognized as a personal brand — at a masculinized event modifies a group of women's perception of this sport, and to reveal the role a personal brand plays in breaking down gender stereotypes, in this case, among the female sports audience. For the empirical analysis of the research, the scope was limited to female undergraduate students in Public Relations and Communication (LRPC) at the Centro Universitario de Ciencias Económico Administrativas (CUCEA). This profile was chosen because their academic training allows them to recognize media trends, the presence of public figures, and their relationship to audience measurement and content management. Specifically, the actions, practices, and interactions of Taylor Swift that motivated these students to follow the broadcast of the sporting event are discussed in a group setting, as well as the traditional feminine stereotypes related to American football among these students.

Theoretical Framework

Based on the definition of the problem and the scope of the research, the theoretical framework was outlined, discussing media and sports, as well as the media influence of public figures and gender stereotypes among sports audiences.

Media and Sports

The media have taken on an important role in massive sporting events, such that Lallana and Sagarzazu (2012) define sports media as social constructions developed by people who have established ideologies that are easily adopted by audiences, creating representations in which power relations and the control these media hold over the majority who consume them are implicit, making it possible to recognize the close relationship and dependence between media and sports; Coakley (2003, cited in Lallana and Sagarzazu, 2012) highlights the dependence between media and sports, since one without the other would have neither programming nor coverage, and sports would therefore lack sponsorships.

Today, digital media have created various avenues that encourage the active participation of all users (Cruz and Miranda, 2021). Nonetheless, the coverage and social models of a sporting context as represented by the media depend on how sport is experienced in daily life, as well as on the acceptance and recognition that sporting activities have among women (Lallana and Sagarzazu, 2012).

Public Figures and Media Influence

A public figure is a person who, due to their achievements, lifestyle, or fame, attracts the legitimate interest of the public (Covarrubias, 2005), that is, when a person, for whatever reason or activity, becomes openly known, and intrinsically accepts that they are losing their privacy and thereby becoming a public matter. Some of these figures are identified because they give detailed access to their personal life and are exposed to social scrutiny; however, only some figures manage to transcend and influence people (Morataya, 2019).

For Gordon (n.d., cited in Gutiérrez, 2023), social influence is understood as individuals' understanding in the face of the real, imagined, or implicit presence of others. This perception leads to constructing an explanation of what these individuals are like and how they relate to them through their behaviors, ways of thinking, or self-image; media influence is thus understood as the imitation people make of the figures they aspire to become, falling for the promotions spread by public figures that encourage viewers to consume; as Gutiérrez (2023) explains, young people aspire to be like a celebrity, and in that aspiration they consume and behave as their idols do. Celebrities employ multiple strategies involving advertising and marketing that generate sympathy and admiration among their audiences. Taylor Swift, with her extensive career and popularity, is no exception, as she generates this desire among fans to be like her.

Public figures play an important role in influencing and/or shaping the consumption behavior of receiving audiences. They are even capable of persuading large numbers of people through their actions and messages, thereby shaping opinion and behavior. For Romero et al. (2015), the consumption of audiovisual programs is not carried out in an educational manner, since their sole purpose is to capture audiences through the private information of famous figures, where the female figure in particular tends to be represented through stereotypes insofar as women are the primary symbol of "glamour, romantic stories, fashion, or recent motherhood" (Romero et al., 2015:41), which appears to generate greater interest among audiences; for Gutiérrez (2023), this is a characteristic of young people, who consume according to what is trending, in advertising campaigns, and according to whoever sponsors or promotes this repetitive pattern, since they are the ones with the greatest access to consumption and close contact with celebrities through digital platforms.

Through their presence in the media, social networks, and public spaces, artists manage to shape audiences' perception and opinion within the space in which the artist operates, as Taylor Swift has done, generating what can be called an anthropomorphization, that is, she has developed the ability to influence her followers' decisions, since she generates emotional and cognitive responses in her fans ranging from empathy to identification with her as a brand (Chapa, 2023), achieving a bond in which her followers absorb the identity constructed by the artist, the messages she conveys, her lifestyle, and even the products she promotes (Díaz, 2013).

In summary, public figures become a marketing strategy aimed at creating an emotional bond between the human and a product or brand, which ultimately increases their profitability and credibility among audiences, even fostering the social construction of gender-associated connotations, producing an influence on the way men and women are viewed, shaped by consumer interests.

Traditional Gender Stereotypes

Gender stereotypes are defined as those beliefs that are taken for granted and that, based on gender ideology, differentiate between men and women, translating into discriminatory behaviors (Barberá, 1998; Moya and Puertas, 2003, cited in Castillo-Mayén and Montes-Berges, 2014). They are characterized by attributions, traits, attitudes, behaviors, and patterns for each gender; some are repeated across people and, by being standardized, become included among the stereotypes marked by a given society (Cambre, 2020), where the family is recognized as the origin of these stereotypes, being the primary setting in which a woman learns her role and a man learns his, just as the media convey, through their messages, advertising, or content, the models and behaviors to imitate, which preserve gender inequality (Cambre, 2020).

Gender Stereotypes Among Sports Audiences

Sports have been perpetuated as an area exclusive to the male gender, and therefore women will not understand it and will not be spectators of the games, since within this field a perception is generated that their participation only matters as an object that helps generate a larger advertising audience (Cambre, 2020). Each culture establishes the roles that must be followed, giving rise to a list of gender stereotypes in which women are attributed characteristics defined by Eckes (1994, cited in Lallana and Sargarzazu, 2012) as: submissive/passive; sensitive/delicate; fragile/weak, which is why women who decide to participate in sporting activities are categorized under a social construction known as masculinized women, implying the idea that women who show a high level of physical fitness are stripped of their femininity and considered masculine, based on their musculature, even their perceived sexual orientation.

For Roche (2015), these stereotypes are clear; she notes that the male figure is referred to as bold, intelligent, and a leader, in contrast to women, who are portrayed as sensitive, kind, and associated with physical beauty, which is why the media have sought to keep viewers in a passive role; however, over time, technological and economic changes have produced a shift in audience behavior, diversifying channels and content according to viewer preferences (Romero et al., 2015).

In the case of Mexico, over the decades men have been stereotyped as the population segment that should consume sports programming. A study conducted by de Arroyabe et al. (2020) states that any sport broadcast on television is a purely masculine space within televised reality; however, Guerrero (2021) notes that television programs have the potential to influence people but are not exclusively confined to one gender, since consuming certain television programs generates positive or negative moods in viewers.

Female Audiences in the NFL

It is recognized that the Super Bowl is the most-watched televised event worldwide; Guerrero (2021), citing data provided by CBS, notes there has been an increase in broadcast audiences, and, with the pandemic, an increase was also recorded in the number of media outlets through which this event is broadcast, such that streaming platforms have now given the opportunity to reach more people.

With respect to the female audience in sports, this has been catalogued with many devalued stereotypes; Hess (1996, cited in Wedgwood, 2008) refers to the Victorian era, when football allowed women to admire male bodies in a socially acceptable way, and Lindley (2002, cited in Wedgwood, 2008) notes that some female fans today still feel sexually attracted to footballers, above the game itself; some even claim that the female audience is linked to pursuing players for reasons of money, fame, sex, love, marriage, among others (Gmelch and San Antonio, 1998; Gauthier and Forsyth, 2000, cited in Wedgwood, 2008).

Meanwhile, Gantz (1981, cited by Clark et al., 2009) suggests that women consume sports as a last resort, or from an exclusion of heterosexual femininity associated with hypermasculinized sports (Esmonde et al., 2018), or even as a result of marketing and women's consumerism, or of social motivations for togetherness (Clark et al., 2009), as could be assumed in the case of the Super Bowl.

Methodology

Studies pointing to the persuasion generated by artists, celebrities, and influencers in breaking down traditional stereotypes serve as the starting point for research into gender stereotypes among sports audiences. This research has an exploratory scope, given that its purpose is to describe how a personal brand contributes to modifying stereotypes regarding female sports audiences.

A qualitative research design is used, since the aim is to delve into information that allows for an understanding of the attitudes, motivations, and thoughts of the participants (Pérez, 2011, cited in Rojas and Sierra, 2023); a qualitative methodological strategy associated with interpretive epistemology (intersubjective dimension) is used, centered on the individual subject and the discovery of the meaning, motives, and intentions behind their actions (CeaD'Ancona, 1999, cited by Gutiérrez, 2023), in order to recognize the perception LRPC students have of Taylor Swift's appearance at the Super Bowl and the role she plays as part of the gender stereotypes present among sports audiences.

The research participants, in addition to being women and studying the Public Relations and Communication degree program, are Taylor Swift fans and are aware of the existence of the Super Bowl. This specific group was chosen given the importance, for students in this field, of learning about influence, image, and media events as part of their particular academic training.

Data collection began through the use of a brief survey with closed-ended questions, which made it possible to confirm that study participants met the defined characteristics. 50 responses were obtained from the initial survey, of which only 13 met the established requirements, and only 8 agreed to participate in the next stage.

The participants were divided into 2 focus groups; this technique is used given that, according to Krueger and Casey (2000, cited in Rojas and Sierra, 2023), it is a qualitative technique that allows people with specific characteristics to be brought together to discuss a topic in depth. These were held on Monday, April 25 and Tuesday, April 26, 2024, virtually, using the Google Meet tool. The average meeting length was 56 minutes, with a total of 14 open-ended questions aimed at obtaining a deeper understanding of the students' opinions regarding the traditional stereotypes present in American football, specifically at the time of the Super Bowl.

Once both focus groups had concluded, the contributions were transcribed and the in-vivo codes were categorized through thematic content analysis; category definitions were exclusive, meaning that an element could only be present in one category, and categories had to align with the research objectives and guiding questions.

Results

This section is structured into 2 main parts to present the findings obtained with greater clarity and coherence; first, gender stereotypes among sports audiences are addressed, followed by the influence of celebrities on feminine stereotypes. Excerpts from the participants' contributions are used to better illustrate the analysis presented, using the following coding: Focus Group 1 = FG1; Focus Group 2 = FG2; Participant 1 = P1; Participant 2 = P2; Participant 3 = P3; Participant 4 = P4, in order to contrast the data obtained from the participants of the two focus groups.

Gender Stereotypes Among Sports Audiences

A liking for American football is not something present in the participants' lives; they note that they find it a complicated sport to understand, but acknowledge that they began watching it as a result of Taylor Swift's relationship with a player from this sport, which sparked their interest in it. When asked whether they knew of any NFL team, they said they did not know or did not identify with one; nonetheless, they do have some male point of reference in this sport, such as a father, or through the relationship between female public figures and players of this sport:

Obviously I'm rooting for the Kansas [City] Chiefs, because Travis plays there, that's the only reason… I'm also rooting for the Miami Dolphins because Braxton Barrios plays there, who's Alex Earl's boyfriend… (FG2, P2).

For Chapa (2023), this is explained by the ability public figures have to influence people's decision-making or preferences, even through triggers of emotional and cognitive responses such as the role of a father figure; but it also validates what Gosling (2007) and Mewett and Toffoletti (2008, cited in Pope and Williams, 2011) note, namely that women are not usually modeled as sports fans; in this regard, Free and Hughson (2003, cited by Mewett and Toffoletti, 2012) note that, for women, being a sports fan means attending, watching, and cheering, as long as it is done in the company of family and friends.

In this sense, when asked about their motivation for watching the Super Bowl, it was confirmed that the participants usually do so for family or social togetherness:

I also started watching it because of my dad, because he would get together with his friends and I started getting into it, but I'm more drawn to the halftime show, not so much the game… (FG1, P4).

However, an additional motivation was also recognized, associated with the influence a public figure has on their decisions:

Well, I only watch it when I like the artist performing at halftime, and I literally only watch the halftime show, but if I don't know them or don't really like them, honestly I don't watch it. (FG2, P4).

When asked for their opinion on the presence of gender stereotypes in sports, most of the students agreed that American football is a sport focused on the male gender because of its rough nature; they pointed out that, despite the existence of women's leagues, the sport's strength and physicality is dominated by men, both in terms of who plays it and who watches it:

There are already women's leagues and all that now, but there's still like this pull between masculine and feminine, and masculinity still dominates in these kinds of games (FG2, P1).

Like, my cousins and people like that assume women are only interested in watching the halftime show for the music part, and that only men understand how the game actually works, and that's exactly it, like the dynamic of them getting together with their friends to drink beer (FG1, P2).

They even pointed to the lack of women's presence in the sport, whether as players, coaches, or team management:

Even when I've watched the halftime show and then the game itself starts, every shot they show is of men, like their coaches, whatever they are, at least I've never seen a woman, so yeah, I see it as very, very focused on men (FG2, P4).

It was confirmed that all the participants had watched the Super Bowl broadcast, and when asked about the possibility of watching another future broadcast of the Super Bowl, they all agreed that the condition would be that Taylor Swift or some other public figure be present:

There's always some artist at halftime, something to talk about. So at that point you have to know what's going on that week and keep yourself informed (FG2, P1).

I would only watch it if Travis and Taylor show up again, otherwise, honestly, no, I wouldn't watch it again (FG1, P4).

Some participants even said they had the impression that Taylor Swift adopted masculinized attitudes in order to be accepted as part of the event:

There was a lot of controversy because right when they showed her yelling in some shots she was cursing, and it was like, oh, how is she yelling curse words, and Taylor Swift… being a woman, how is she going to be drinking? (FG1, P2).

Meanwhile, other participants said they disagreed with this view, considering that Taylor Swift fell into the role of the submissive girlfriend by doing everything possible to be present on Super Bowl day to support her boyfriend, romanticizing the idea that women are the ones who must always make the greater sacrifices:

I saw it more like the part where the woman is there for the man. She's someone's girlfriend who's playing, and she did all this just to be there for him, to support him, you know? (FG2, P4).

These discussions invite reflection on how gender stereotypes among sports audiences have not changed; on one hand, there is a perceived need for women to become masculinized in order to be part of male interaction spaces, while, on the other, women's presence and recognition within high-contact sports occurs only through the concept of belonging entailed by being a male player's partner, or is even tacitly assumed to mean that women do not understand the sport and that their interest lies in the triviality of a spectacle or social gathering.

The Influence of Celebrities on Female Sports Stereotypes

At this point it is necessary to recognize that Taylor Swift has built such a consolidated personal brand that her fans follow every facet of the artist's public and personal life; various media outlets, as well as the NFL itself, acknowledged that her presence at the event changed the conversation and interaction on social media, attracted more viewers, sponsorships, and advertising, but above all changed the composition of the audience by bringing in a greater number of women, making it possible to infer that, despite how deeply rooted gender stereotypes are in sports, a public figure can modify or reconstruct interaction between men and women, even in high-contact activities involving roughness and strength, such as American football.

For the research participants, Taylor Swift's presence at the Super Bowl was viewed positively; some considered it a successful moment for her personal brand, even believing it benefited traditional and historic organizations such as the NFL:

Well, honestly I saw it more as literally a marketing strategy, publicity, promotion, and the fact that she was there (FG2, P4).

I think it was a win-win. I mean, I did see a lot of comments on social media and I get that some fans were kind of annoyed… like why are they giving her so much of the spotlight, blah blah blah, but I think it was a win for the Super Bowl's ratings as such (FG1, P2).

For some of the participants, Taylor Swift's presence at the Super Bowl did not change their perception of the sport, and their interest remained focused only on the public figure:

Honestly, for me it really was 100% about Taylor — I had watched the Super Bowl in previous years, but not all the way through like I watched this one, and I liked that I got to see not just Taylor but Lana del Rey too, and Ice Spice was there too, I think, so honestly it really was more because of Taylor that I started watching, and to follow the relationship… she has with Travis, that was basically it, and I wanted to see the ending, whether they'd show them or not (FG1, P1).

Meanwhile, for other participants, Taylor Swift's presence in American football sparked interest in the sport:

Yeah, I do feel like this Super Bowl kind of pushed me more toward getting into the sport because… I don't even follow Mexican sports, so I feel like that's going to get me to pay more attention… to the sport (FG2, P2).

As Gutiérrez (2023) notes, the influence an artist exerts across various settings creates an appeal to the audience and sparks anticipation both among those who follow her and those who generate content about her, such that her attitudes, behaviors, messages, and actions have the power to modify her fans' perception, even influencing how they conceive of themselves and their role within social settings. In this sense, and given the power public figures have to modify society's status quo, they are capable of modifying gender stereotypes constructed and normalized by society, which is why it is important to understand which characteristics most draw audiences to follow this particular artist's life, since her fans' response has solidified over the years:

I think I've grown up alongside her, I've followed her for a long time, so in a way you feel like she's your close friend, so to speak, because of the way she talks to you about her life, the whole metaphor thing, the music, how, well yes, in a way being a public figure means she reveals a lot of details or parts of her life that fans also find out about and, well, adopt, make part of their own lives, and then on top of that there's the music thing, her charisma, her imagination, etc… (F1, P3)

She is seen as a role model for her strong representation of female empowerment expressed through storytelling in all her song lyrics and the promotion of her music in an original and distinctive way compared to other music artists:

Also, she's a woman who keeps moving forward, she doesn't just stand still, if it's not with her music project, she keeps going through other, other ventures, in her personal case, her studies, I mean, she keeps developing despite everything she has going on right now (F2, P1)

The participants perceive her as a woman capable of breaking with traditional gender stereotypes, contrary to the expectations placed on passive feminine behavior:

Well, I think lately the Super Bowl had been… really on a downswing, it wasn't giving people much to talk about, so… this year was like the most worthwhile one and… they took the whole Taylor phenomenon,… basically her year from the start of this one and the last one…, she gained a lot of momentum, so… she basically caused a revolution between two worlds like we were just saying and that's really interesting… because right now women's presence is coming in, we're already seeing it in presidencies and… in everything, women are already gaining value and this is… really interesting because… we weren't seeing the female figure like this before (F2, P1).

For some, Taylor Swift manages to play a persuasive role in shaping collective thoughts and decisions, and even manages to bring more women into new social causes:

That's clearly what we're living through, and whatever she's involved in, her followers are going to want to get involved because they're watching her and want more of her and… in some way this… helps her followers discover new worlds and see what they like to try and… go ahead and give it a shot, and so new interests just keep being created (F2, P1).

As has been analyzed, a public figure like Taylor Swift, who has global media reach, plays a very important role in audiences' perspective; it has therefore been noted that male-dominated sports have used entertainment, such as concerts, to generate interest among female audiences, although fans' motivations for watching sports differ from the habits of non-fans (Clark et al., 2009); although Gantz and Wenger (1991, cited by Clark et al., 2009) note that, regardless of gender, sports fans have similar viewing experiences, and that women only consume sports for social reasons, this work makes it possible to affirm that a public figure can be a reason for women to become part of the audience for a sport considered masculine.

Conclusions

With respect to traditional gender stereotypes regarding women's role within American football, it was possible to identify that they are associated with exclusively feminine roles, for example, being a cheerleader, showing interest only in the Super Bowl halftime show; there is even the perception that it is a purely masculine sport, which is why female audiences for this sport are limited.

It was also possible to recognize the power a public figure has to modify gender stereotypes, especially because famous people or celebrities are seen as opinion leaders and display attitudes that have a great influence on their followers, and even though Taylor Swift did not participate in the Super Bowl, her mere presence opened the door for her followers to modify their preferences simply by identifying with or enjoying the same things she does.

Finally, it is necessary to note that it is not possible to generalize the results of this research, since it is an exploratory study and the empirical test was carried out with only a small group of participants; it is therefore necessary to continue this line of research, in both qualitative and quantitative approaches, to demonstrate the power public figures have to modify gender stereotypes, not only among sports audiences but in other contexts and dimensions that allow for the elimination of discrimination and violence resulting from them.
