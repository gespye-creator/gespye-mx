Gender stereotypes present on the Twitch platform

Estereotipos de género presentes en la plataforma Twitch

Abstract.

Gender stereotypes are an existing problem in every social sphere, they are also present in the gamer world. This research lists stereotypes found on Twitch, because the lack of visibility of this problem was recognized. The main objective is to describe the differences between men and women who use Twitch, in order to demonstrate the existing gaps in terms of opportunities, such as sponsorships and preferences. A documentary methodology was used, having as starting point research works that describe gender stereotypes in the gamer field. The data collection and analysis carried out showed that on the Twitch platform, men continue to have more work and social opportunities, in addition to having more facilities and support from followers, compared to women.

Keywords:

gender stereotypes; streamers; videogames; Twitch.

Introduction

When talking about digital platforms for videogame-related content, Twitch is the first answer given by young people, given its current reach; however, for previous generations, this may not be so obvious. In this sense, it is necessary to clarify the scope of this platform. For Sevilla Durán (2021), Twitch is more than a video platform for distributing content; it is a social network for the creation of digital content, focusing both on videogames and on conversation sessions with followers, while also broadcasting videogame competitions (eSports); meanwhile, for Lozano and Cuartero (2020), Twitch was created as a variation of the streaming platform Justin.tv, which allowed live videogame streaming, and was an alternative to reach a market niche that, at the time, Google had not yet captured.

Certainly, 20 years ago, platforms with this focus did not exist anywhere. The only service that advertised a similar variety of content was Facebook, with the major difference that it did not have a live-broadcast format. Twitch revolutionized the current market with its way of offering audiovisual content through a digital medium (the internet), offering the whole world an easily accessible, free alternative, with the distinctive feature of a diversity of content that keeps users interested.

Since videogames began to be considered an economically fruitful field, there has been a clear preference among men for consuming this product. Both content creators and consumers of this content are, for the most part, male, while women have always been a significantly minority sector. However, Sevilla Durán (2021) conducted a survey of students at the University of Seville regarding the platform and who consumed content from it, finding that 57.8% of consumers were men and 42.2% were women, showing that content generated by and for gamers is, for the most part, of greater interest to men than to women.

As of July 2023, 80% of visitors to the Twitch platform were men (Orús, 2023); however, there is a growing trend among women to create content that is increasingly consumed. Women as a group are beginning to gain traction, having, for the first time since the service was created, numbers sufficient to rival the statistics generated by men on the site, and even though the content they generate is comparable in quality and quantity to that of men, the opportunities they receive to stand out, as well as the recognition they obtain, is markedly lower than that of male creators.

In this sense, the gap that exists between men and women on the Twitch platform is present both among consumers and content creators, and can be identified not only through the statistics collected by the platform itself, but is also present in economic and social aspects, that is, women receive less financial compensation for their creations and are victims of violence and harassment, even though the platform is supposed to be a shared virtual space in which both sexes coexist in the same environment, create similar content, and could even be assumed to share demographic characteristics.

In this regard, Rosas Reatiga et al. (2024) explain that most videogame-related content platforms tend to prioritize their most loyal consumers: men, who not only predominate in number, but also tend to generate greater financial return, both due to the amount of content generated and consumed by them. Male users, for their part, say that their main motivation for generating content is the enjoyment of playing, of feeling powerful and experiencing pleasure while developing new skills in action, fighting, or shooting games, among others, whereas women prefer more varied games such as adventure and RPGs (Role Playing Games), where the content can be more thought-provoking for the audience.

Although the content generated by both men and women on Twitch falls under the same category — gameplays or similar videogame content — there are notable differences between what men and women create. Statistically, while the content generated by men that is consumed depends on the videogames being played, the production quality of their videos, and even the wit and ingenuity aimed at entertaining, the reasons women are followed are different. Often mistaken for CamGirls, who perform various sexual acts in exchange for money or tips from viewers (Martínez, 2020), women on Twitch seek to stand out through their videogame-related content, although they have learned that, for their content to be appealing, they must also produce their physical image. A woman, then, is primarily preferred for how she looks, while her content takes a back seat.

In such a way, the economic and social inequalities that occur in the professional and working world between men and women are being replicated in the gamer sphere, a situation that sparks interest in investigating and making known the differences present as a result of gender stereotypes between men and women within the Twitch platform. This is why this work seeks to recognize what differences exist between men and women on the Twitch platform, as a consequence of gender stereotypes, in order to present evidence of how what happens in the real world is transferred to new spaces of social interaction, which perpetuate these gender differences.

As already stated, Twitch is a platform that does not distinguish or restrict its content creators by gender; however, the differences become evident, which is why this platform becomes part of the object of study of this research, given the amount of statistics and data that can be obtained from it. Additionally, there is an awareness of the gender stereotypes documented in areas such as work, family, and social life, so this paper seeks to find how these stereotypes have carried over into this streaming platform.

There is recognition of some prior research that serves as background for this work; however, it was not possible to identify a study that addresses the gender stereotypes present within the Twitch platform, and as it is a recent space of social interaction, some of the scope of social interaction and communication generated within this space has not been studied, which makes gathering information on this research topic accessible, given that it will require working with information obtainable from previous academic works, as well as from the company itself and its official sources.

This research sets out, as objectives, first, to describe the existing differences between men and women within the Twitch platform, caused by the gender stereotypes present in that environment, in order to demonstrate the sex-based gaps that exist in terms of opportunities, as well as treatment and preferences, taking the last four years as a reference, by selecting, as the object of study, the leading male and female content creators who remained active during the period of this work, as well as retrieving these creators' statistics in terms of content-creation rate and number of followers, along with the sex of the latter, in order to recognize the differences between content generated by male and female creators, as well as the physical image they generally present while generating content on the platform, and to find the relationship between the content generated and the creators' image with the treatment given by their followers, as well as the compensation assigned to each sex.

State of the art

In today's digital age there are differences, or as experts on the subject call them: gaps. García et al. (2006), addressing the well-known first digital divide, explain that it is nothing more than a distancing between people due to different reasons, whether economic, cultural, or simply a lack of education regarding the use of technologies, while for Fuentes Zurita (2010), the second of these digital divides is that of gender, due to the very different roles that men and women take on (or have imposed on them) in the use of technological tools.

In this regard, Guevara-Ayón (2020) found the presence of gender stereotypes in the use of Information Technologies (IT); indeed, women's participation in conducting the interviews was scarce, which fed into the idea that they do not approach technology because it is difficult for them to understand, however, the few women interviewed acknowledged that there are indeed female colleagues with technological skills, although they are not as recognized as their male counterparts, who are considered to have an aptitude for IT. However, the difference in vocational orientation and use of IT does not arise spontaneously; Suárez and Álvarez (2023) found gender differences in social-media use among preadolescents, in that while boys prefer to consume sports- and videogame-related content via YouTube and Twitch, their female counterparts opt for music and beauty content on platforms such as TikTok or Pinterest.

Regarding time spent on videogame streaming platforms, Bastos de los Santos (2021) found that there is a difference between the sexes in the total number of hours invested per week: while a man devotes on average 14 hours a week to the platform, a woman invests only around 8 hours. The results of this analysis revealed that, to a large extent, men predominate in terms of reach, influence, and rapport with their consumers.

With respect to the perspective of game developers, this remains in line with the above; a videogame-industry executive notes that there are more left-handed players than female players, and this considering that videogames are not made for left-handed people, only to then question why videogames should be made for women — which leads Jackson (2000) to argue that this is a problem that goes beyond simple inclusion, since encouraging only men to play videogames will help them develop more computer-related skills, meaning a competitive advantage over women in their future; however, since this affects the bottom line of large companies, the situation is not taken into account. For González-Sancho and Reyes (2020), videogame development companies have tried to be inclusive through the creation of female protagonists in their titles, but have given up making these projects a priority, since in terms of sales these decisions are not reflected in the desired financial return. It is considered that the target audience (certainly mostly male) does not seek out these videogames, which pushes developers back toward conventional offerings.

On the other hand, Zimbrón and Esperanza (2019) sought to shed light on how the representation of male and female characters in videogames influences the content generated by creators; they found that, even though women today represent nearly half of gamers worldwide, videogame products continue to be aimed mostly at a male audience, and even though nearly forty years have passed since the rise of consoles, this phenomenon has not been resolved, since it was identified that 28% of female characters are hypersexualized, while male characters are presented with courage and strong character; meanwhile, for Mico Luengo (2017), women face inequalities when interacting with a female character in a given videogame, or even when using their own avatar; she describes how the simple act of playing with a female protagonist can cause a creator's content on digital platforms to stand out less than if they use male characters, and even found that the clothing worn by these female characters is, for the most part, sexualized for no apparent reason other than to attract a specific audience.

For Torres González (2021), this may be because the key to good communication with one's followers, and therefore success, involves dynamism, a well-designed set, and, above all, presenting a friendly appearance both physically and virtually, which also entails innovation in one's content, motivating the audience to keep following the creator and to relate to them. Nonetheless, for Alfonso and Aguilera (2021), there are gender inequalities in the industry; they found that both men and women feel undervalued, but while men feel this way when compared to other male content creators, women attribute it to sexist attitudes.

Ortega Muñoz and Ortega Muñoz (2022) note that the perception of female gamers on Twitch is misconstrued. The example used is that of the streamer Ari Gameplays, who was banned (expelled) from the platform for wearing low-cut blouses that did not conform to the standards of the platform's followers, even though female game characters are shown in hypersexualized attire. Gutiérrez Guerrero (2022) also documented how this female content creator, AriGameplays, is called into question, with her fame attributed to Garnizo, who is her partner; he also notes that users criticize the image of female streamers without discussing their gaming skills, while a male player, even without gaming skills, receives supportive comments without ever being judged physically.

Additionally, discrimination in sponsorships has been documented; Serra Pallarés and Yalan (2023) found that brands such as Reebok, Aorus, Intel, and Americanet are present in the gamer world and that while male gamers are supported based on the content they generate, in the case of women it is their physical appearance that determines the support they receive, since what sponsors look for are the streamers with the largest number of followers.

Finally, the work of Moisés Toro (2023) documents the voices of female content creators; they consider it a very difficult task to stand out as a gamer while being a woman, in addition to noting that sexism on the Twitch platform is very present and that they feel watched and observed; however, they note that they have found support through major female game directors such as Jade Raymond and Amy Henning, and even through organizations such as Women in Games.

Theoretical framework

This research seeks to describe gender differences based on the stereotypes present within the Twitch platform, since these contribute to generating inequalities that affect equity between men and women by making visible the differences between male and female users, in order to promote fair opportunities and treatment in every aspect of the platform, including participation, visibility, and monetization opportunities, as discussed by Gómez and Martínez (2000).

To discuss the gender stereotypes present in the videogame-streaming field, it is first necessary to define what a stereotype is. For Castillo and Montes (2014), a gender stereotype is defined as beliefs about the characteristics paired with men and women regarding gender itself, related to the tasks and behaviors a man or woman must perform and adopt simply by virtue of being one, which brings with it political, social, cultural, and economic inequalities. In this sense, gender stereotypes within the field of videogame streaming are recognized through the wage gap, the number of followers, and streamers' physical image.

With respect to the wage gap, for Paredes Zañartu (2021), this is present due to the existence of differences in earnings received between men and women; although there are a variety of factors that can affect a streamer's income on Twitch, such as number of followers, stream duration, and type of content, it has been shown that female streamers earn significantly less than men.

Regarding the number of followers, the starting point is statistics showing the comparison between the number of followers held by top streamers worldwide. It is worth noting that, for both female and male content creators, followers are mostly men, who have distinct preferences and reasons: while a male streamer will be followed for his content and personality, a female streamer will be followed mainly for her physical appearance.

The physical image and visual framing of streams varies by streamer, although the difference is even more pronounced between a male creator and a female one. Visual framing in a stream refers to the visual and auditory representation of the live broadcast, which includes the streamer's image, their surroundings, the game or activity, graphics and overlays, voice, in-game sounds, music, and sound effects (Arteaga Verde and Pozo Villanueva, 2022). Streamers create appealing images using techniques such as good lighting, correct framing, and interesting backgrounds, in addition to graphics and overlays to add personality and professionalism.

Methodology

To describe and understand the differences that manifest between men and women on the Twitch platform as a consequence of gender stereotypes, a non-experimental, exploratory design is used. For Ávila et al. (2019), non-experimental research is that which is carried out without deliberately altering the variables, based mainly on observing phenomena in their natural context and then analyzing them. This design makes it possible to examine and analyze the behaviors and dynamics present on Twitch, as well as user demographics, their interactions and behaviors with streamers, in addition to evaluating metrics and engagement in broadcasts. These same authors note that exploratory research is used when the problem has not been fully addressed, since its function is to recognize and identify the problem as such, which is the objective of this research.

For the purposes of this work, a documentary approach is used, which Peña and Pirela (2007), paraphrasing what Fox (2005) noted, define as the analysis of already-existing information, and its transfer into a new format so that it is easier for new readers to understand. The documentary method is considered the one best suited to the characteristics and needs of this research, given that already-recorded statistical results are collected, such as the difference in percentages of male versus female users on Twitch and how these totals have changed over the years. Likewise, other data are explored, such as the wage gaps between male and female streamers and differences in image (both physical and in framing during their live broadcast) between them, which are likewise quantifiable.

To delimit the documentary study sample, the first hundred streamers were defined, given that within the first fifty — the initial value considered — only two female creators appeared among those with the most followers worldwide. The period was set from 2021 to the current year, 2024. Twitch Tracker, the application that collects statistics for all existing accounts on the platform, is used as the data source. For the sample, both follower totals (and their sexes) and time invested in streaming by each creator are extracted, in order to then draw a comparison between men and women based on the resulting figures.

For data collection, index cards were used; following Rodríguez (2011), relevant data were compiled, accompanied by notes on the sources found. Data sources ranged from websites dedicated to tabulating statistics from Twitch, to previous research that had begun recording how gender stereotypes were becoming present in the videogame field, in order to later compare the results with contemporary works. Different sources of information were analyzed, such as Twitch's official page on the social network X, from which data such as top streamers by year were obtained, as well as Twitch Tracker, to obtain total follower counts for each sampled streamer, as well as their global rankings.

To process the information gathered, documentary analysis is used. As noted by Dulzaidez and Molina (2004), documentary analysis presents cited documents in a way that is easy to understand, since important data for the research are extracted and compiled into a single document. Data were collected through the use of bibliographic index cards, to later be presented in the form of research that answers what stereotypes are present in the videogame field, through the streaming activity carried out on the Twitch platform. This exercise is carried out following the guidelines of documentary analysis, presenting only those results from other works that support the existence of said stereotypes and their presence on the aforementioned platform.

Results

Through the data collection carried out by means of documentary analysis, it was found, as a first result, that the gender stereotypes present in videogames have their origin both in literature and in the fiction of past eras, where the female figure had to be rescued by the hero or main male character in the story. In the context of videogames, research carried out by Provenzo (n.d., cited in the work of Díez Gutiérrez, 2004) details that women have to be rescued after a kidnapping in 30% of videogames. Other figures found were those of Moncada and Chacón (2012, cited by Gutiérrez Guerrero in 2022), who note that, within the videogame field, female characters that appear are, in 30% of cases, mere sexual and erotic objects, in 10% "aggressor" women (who display masculine behaviors and roles), and in 60% of cases appear as "passive" women, that is, the character who must be rescued, which even borders on objectification and sexualization.

These data confirm the existence of the stereotype of the weak woman who must be rescued by a strong character, usually the archetype of the masculine, manly man, and that, for the rescue act to be appealing, she must be attractive through an emphasis on her sexuality.

Additionally, according to Moreno (2020, cited in Ortega Muñoz, 2022), the explanation for why the percentage of women playing videogames is still low compared to men is due to the widespread perception of a lack of femininity in this activity, which is why it is not looked upon favorably for women to engage in these activities; in fact, Toro (2017, cited by Gutiérrez Guerrero, 2022) notes that there is an idea that women who play videogames do so to attract attention, and even that they may be sexually harassed or insulted for not engaging in traditional activities such as washing or cooking.

They are advised that, if they are going to enter the gamer world, they should try softer titles more suited to them (Mico Luengo, 2017), which reaffirms that, due to the type of content videogames contain, these are aimed at a male audience, and that the little content available for women is that which has a more innocent or childlike nature, preserving the femininity expected of them. It is therefore possible to consider the presence of stereotypes here, in which the issue is not that videogames are only for men, but rather that, since they do not represent femininity, they are considered unsuitable for women.

Nonetheless, in recent years women's participation in videogames has changed; the number of women who have entered the gamer world through Twitch has grown. Santiago (2023) notes that by 2017, men represented 80% of total users, while by 2021, 65% of the platform's users were men (Valero, 2021), so that, if this trend continues, it is possible the percentages will even out in a few years, given that women are adopting increasingly masculinized practices (Ortega Muñoz, 2022).

However, this incorporation of women into the gamer world of the Twitch platform has not occurred on equitable terms, since Twitch's own format, which requires that, when streaming, the content creator project their image through a webcam, fosters the objectification of the women who participate, with some women recognizing that if their physical image is well leveraged it will generate subscribers they would not otherwise have, since, for them, their fans choose them only if they consider them attractive; nonetheless, they acknowledge that there are those who go so far as to harass them to the point of sending sexual messages that, if not responded to, generate an attack both on them personally and on their gaming skills (Moisés Toro, 2023); this forces them to wear eye-catching, provocative, and sensual clothing that highlights their physical attributes, which are widely recognized by their followers (Ortega Muñoz and Ortega Muñoz, 2022).

Twitch does indeed state that it has guidelines to support and protect its content creators; however, the solutions coming from the platform are not considered effective. For Cullen and Ruberg (2019, cited by Moisés Toro, 2023), community-guideline policies reflect discriminatory attitudes toward women, leading them to block users who bother them when they want to run their streams; such is the case of the content creator AriGameplays, who, in 2019, was banned for two years (expelled) from Twitch, for having been considered to have violated the previously mentioned rules — meaning that, far from protecting women, these rules are mainly used to report streamers who do not comply with image or content guidelines, rather than to punish or penalize those users who harass or otherwise abuse them.

Continuing with the example of AriGameplays, during a stream she carried out together with her husband, and fellow streamer, Garnizo, Gutiérrez Guerrero (2022) identified that 52.9% of comments directed at her were about her physical attributes, using highly sexual and even offensive words and expressions, a truly notable difference compared to the 17.1% he received, mostly compliments about his gaming skills, which again demonstrates, on one hand, the lack of protective policies from the platform, and on the other, the perpetuation of traditional gender roles within it.

Regarding followers, the requirements for following a man versus a woman on Twitch are different; the motivation for following a male gamer centers on the content he generates, the games or activities he performs, while for women it is their physical appearance and how they look in their clothing (Moisés Toro, 2023), which is reflected in the platform's statistics — for example, streamer Rivers was the most-viewed female creator worldwide in March of that year, with 2.16 million recorded hours, compared to xQc, who was watched for a total of 12.93 million hours (Babenko, 2023).

As of May 2024, Twitch Tracker shows that male streamer Jynxzi is ranked number 1 worldwide on Twitch, with 5,664,409 followers, while the first woman to appear in the ranking was Emiru, in position 81, with 1,487,010 followers. The data found, both on global rankings and on follower counts, demonstrate the trend that male creators are the ones who generate the largest number of followers, due to the fact that the gamer world continues to be an environment predominantly frequented by men, which perpetuates the belief that this medium is made by and for men.

Regarding the wage gap between men and women, already marked in any professional field, this is also present on Twitch. The magazine Merca2.0 (2021) compiled data from a study carried out by MrQ, which found that the male channel that generated the most money was CriticalRole, with 8.2 million euros (an estimated $147 million MXN), while female streamer Sintica would earn 786,264 euros annually (an estimated $14 million MXN). For 2024, using the earnings of both Jynxzi and Emiru as a current reference (top male and female streamers, respectively), the male streamer generates $270K USD ($4,582,548 MXN), while the female creator earns around $176.3K USD ($2,992,234.12 MXN) (Social Blade, 2024) — a situation that persists even though Twitch states it provides equitable income opportunities for its content creators, so the difference in earnings may be due both to donations from users as well as to brands seeking sponsorships with content creators.

Regarding the analysis of brands that sponsor streamers, Serra Pallarés and Yalan Dongo (2023) found a strong focus on women's physical attributes; these authors note that a product advertised by a female streamer will only be remembered by her followers if the creator interacts directly with it, whether by wearing it or handling it for a certain amount of time, while brands associated with male streamers are remembered without the need for interaction or handling; it is thus taken for granted, within the gamer community, that women lack knowledge about videogames and gamer brands, so that some content creators, faced with limited and reduced income from failing to reach desired metrics — both in audience and donations — as well as the lack or minimal support from their followers, consider stopping the career they set out to build on Twitch, since, more than finding their own voice as content creators, they have to deal with a masculinized platform that has no room for them (Moisés Toro, 2023), or, at best, they must take on masculine stereotypes of success and of not losing (Gutiérrez Guerrero, 2022), implying that it must be women themselves who close the distances that gender stereotypes create between them and their male counterparts.

Conclusions

The results of this research make it possible to identify that men continue to lead the videogame field, and therefore hold supremacy on the Twitch platform, and that even though the incorporation of women into the gamer world is recorded, the gap caused by gender stereotypes persists; even though the study focused on the analysis of recent years, it is important to recognize that these disparities have existed for decades, but have become more visible in the current social context, which means that social demands and the increased participation of women in traditionally masculinized fields have caused new audiences to emerge.

The analysis of the leading followers, of both male and female creators, played a crucial role in this research, making it possible to recognize the reach content creators have toward their followers, as well as the role these followers play for content creators. It also revealed that male streamers are the ones with the greatest number of followers and, generally, a more positive experience in the practice of streaming. Likewise, economic inequality between men and women was made evident, with men earning up to ten times more than female creators, as well as the causes of this large wage gap.

Likewise, it was possible to recognize how the content generated by streamers shows considerable differences. Although men and women strive to offer quality, various factors make it difficult for that effort to be equitably rewarded, such as consumer preference for a man or a woman, which comes down to the way content is generated — for men, the main reason is their content itself, while for women, it depends largely on their physical image. For this reason, the visual image of female streamers is considered a particularly controversial aspect, since criticism of women's physical appearance is common and appears to be the only reason they are valued, given that their content is even ignored — a situation that can translate into harassment, threats, and even manipulation directed at them, underscoring the importance of platform intervention through real policies that foster equity between the sexes and that recognize the challenges female streamers face in this regard.

At this point it is necessary to note that this work, in addition to the time required for its preparation, faced some limitations, such as not being able to carry out fieldwork with male and female content creators on the Twitch platform, and not including all streaming platforms, such as the emerging platform Kick, which also uses a similar entertainment system with the collaboration of digital content creators. This research topic therefore leaves open lines of research for conducting interviews or surveys with both streamers and users of these platforms, to learn firsthand how they face gender stereotypes within their content-generation process, their interaction with followers and with the platform itself, as well as the analysis of Twitch's policies and guidelines on gender equity. Comparative studies among users from different countries are also warranted, in order to detect similarities and cultural and socioeconomic differences in gender participation and representation on Twitch.
