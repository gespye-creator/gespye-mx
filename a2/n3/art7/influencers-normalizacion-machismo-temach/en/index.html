The role of influencers in normalizing sexism: "El Temach"

Abstract.

This research article focuses on analyzing the normalization of machismo through the content generated on YouTube by El Temach between 2021 and 2024. Through the analysis of six of his most popular videos, we explore how his humorous language and narratives contribute to the dissemination and acceptance of gender stereotypes and sexist attitudes among his audience, which is why we employ a qualitative methodology based on discourse analysis, such analysis aims to identify macho language through sexist humor that reinforces gender inequality. Temach uses humor that, although apparently harmless, spreads sexist attitudes, which could be normalized. This analysis confirms how content creators of digital platforms have a great impact on the normalization of machismo in society.

Keywords:

Machismo, Normalization, Humor, Influencers, social networks.

Introduction

This research aims to analyze how the content generated by the influencer known as El Temach on the social network YouTube contributes to the normalization of machismo. It is important to recognize the impact of social media content on people, since the mass adoption of digital platforms has completely transformed the way people interact, share information, and consume content. According to Inarejos (2019), as far back as 2013, platforms such as Facebook, WhatsApp, and YouTube already had a very significant reach among audiences.

The relevance of this study lies in the fact that, although machismo has grown over the years and has spread beyond the family and workplace settings where it was traditionally found, its presence in various media outlets, including television and radio broadcasters and newspapers, has expanded significantly on the internet, especially on social media. All these platforms directly expose users' personalities, and therefore tend to disqualify, criticize, blackmail, and make harsh accusations, mostly against women (Inarejos, 2019). As Chirino (2020) notes, the media have played a very important role in the rapid growth and development of gender-based violence, presenting it as something "normal" rather than a serious problem for society, especially for women.

In this sense, it is of the utmost importance to recognize the impact influencers have on social media, since they have wide reach and significant influence on their audience's day-to-day interpretation of events. An example of this is the influencer Luis Castilleja, better known as "El Temach," who stands out on digital platforms where consumption of his content is massive and constantly changing. This type of content, which in particular uses humor as its entertainment language, can contribute to the normalization of macho attitudes. Although humor is considered a medium of communication, it is also related to a negative side in which it conveys ideas that, when repeated and accepted without any kind of questioning, reinforce gender-based violence and contribute to the normalization of machismo. As Van Zoonen (1994) notes in her book Feminist Media Studies, content not only informs or disseminates information, but also affects how society interprets reality based on that information and normalizes it in everyday life.

For this reason, this research regains relevance, because, although studies such as that of Calvopiña and Tello (2024) exist, no analysis has yet been carried out of the content generated by El Temach. This research gap makes it of the utmost importance to analyze the content of his videos, given his reach and the massive, constant interaction he has with his followers, which makes this type of content an important medium for the normalization of behaviors that incite gender-based violence.

This research seeks to answer: In what way does the content with the greatest reach and views generated over time by the influencer known as El Temach on the YouTube platform contribute to the normalization of machismo?, by analyzing the themes, narratives, and discursive representations in El Temach's most-viewed YouTube videos, in which he promotes machismo, in order to recognize the normalization and promotion of gender-based violence in his content.

The work is structured into five sections. First, the state of the art and background are presented, addressing how machismo has been normalized in the media, especially on social media, where the easy access these platforms offer helps spread these attitudes. The impact on social media is discussed, noting how influencers have contributed to the normalization of macho attitudes. Additionally, previous studies evidencing this phenomenon are mentioned.

Second, the theoretical framework presents how gender-based violence is currently manifesting on digital platforms, with a main focus on the normalization of machismo on YouTube and in the content of creators such as El Temach. Key concepts are addressed, such as patriarchy, hegemonic masculinity, and machismo, explaining how these terms sustain discrimination against women.

Third, a discourse-analysis methodology with a descriptive approach is used, following the model set out by Vila (2021). This discourse analysis focuses on the 6 most-viewed videos by influencer El Temach. Fourth, the results are presented, shown in three categories: Machismo, normalization of machismo, and minimization of women through words. Finally, the conclusions present the main findings of the research, highlighting how El Temach's content contributes to the normalization of machismo, fulfilling the research objective, while also analyzing the implications this has for society, the platform's lack of regulation, and possible directions for future research.

State of the Art

Today it is possible to find machismo reflected in different media outlets, both television and print, and now also through the internet. For García (2017), micro-machismo and misogyny are present in society, and the internet only acts as a reflection of this; nonetheless, the fact that machismo has migrated to this medium, even though the internet has a lower number of women than men online, has contributed to generating impunity for digital aggressors, since it allows them anonymity, in addition to hiding behind freedom of expression, even when the content generated infringes on human rights such as equality, honor, and the dignity of the person who becomes the target of reactions and comments of this kind, which becomes a problem ranging from the normalization and reinforcement of stigmas around machismo to a strong impact on the mental health of viewers of both sexes (Vegal, 2019), especially considering that social media are agents that shape the construction of gender and tend to impact macho actions among audiences (Gil et al., 2024).

This lack of control turns social media into an ideal setting for content creators who promote gender-based violence, presenting themselves as a reflection of society. The media through which this content is spread take on minimal social responsibility toward consumers, which gives creators the freedom to replicate a sexist and macho culture (Bolaños, 2019). Such is the case of YouTube, which, according to Vila (2021), has, since the platform's early days, featured machismo, misogyny, and sexism as a humor genre when publishing a video on the platform. Some of the examples mentioned by the author are the YouTubers Germán Garmendia, Rubius, and Wismichu, who foster highly stereotyped treatment of women along gender lines. Nonetheless, these content creators are not the only ones promoting macho content; another figure considered an influencer who, since his beginnings, has generated controversial videos by promoting gender-differentiated behaviors, in addition to promoting stereotypes and machismo, is Luis Castilleja, better known as "El Temach," who, according to Calvopiña and Tello (2024), has managed to influence and instill behaviors through his narrative, guiding his audience on a more personal level, so that his content carries over into followers' real lives, thereby inciting the generation of gender-based violence and the normalization of machismo among his followers.

This is compounded when considering what Hens (2019) notes, namely that YouTubers, like other people with reach on social media, can affect what people think, so that macho comments risk spreading, while this same possibility is also open to those with a pro-equality message; nonetheless, once this door is opened, comments can go one way or the other, though the negative risk is greater, since, as Light (2013) highlights, men seek to build and reaffirm their masculine attitudes through social media, where they find macho messages that incite violence, generated mostly by anonymous people.

For Tortajada and Vera (2021), using the internet to express inequality and offend women anonymously is a hateful response to the progress feminism has made in recent times; they stress that, although there has been legal progress to stop these attacks, more research is needed on the topic to arrive at an effective solution proposal, to which Baños (2019) adds that, to achieve a more respectful digital sphere for both sexes, trust needs to be built with the female gender, making women feel fairly represented by social media users, thereby better promoting gender perspectives; he also notes that there is currently a debate over whether legal measures should be used in this medium, since they could affect other rights such as freedom of expression.

For his part, Lopes (2023) concludes that, to eradicate offensive comments toward women on social media, it is necessary to implement norms and principles to guide people's behavior, systematize responses to offensive comments, and suggests a joint action whenever behavior of this kind is observed. He also suggests that freedom of expression should be analyzed more deeply to prevent abusive and offensive behavior toward women on social media.

Nonetheless, as highlighted by the Observatorio Nacional de Tecnología y Sociedad (ONTSI) (2022), the problem lies in the fact that offensive comments generated on social media toward women are considered an invisible form of violence despite containing clear insults, threats, and harassment; hence it is suggested that the problem be made visible through a detailed case study to identify the different forms of online aggression against women; Pedroza (2019) adds that gender-based violence across various platforms causes women to exclude themselves from making comments or posts on them, which may be because women do not usually know how to defend themselves from an aggressor on social media, making it necessary to regulate behavioral norms and make reporting mechanisms more accessible, since organized hatred is present through a group of people or brands that attack a specific group of individuals who share a common characteristic, but whom they consider inferior (Gil et al., 2022).

Indeed, in recent times there has been growing interest in studying the content generated by YouTubers, among which the analysis of the differences between the imagery of men and women presented in the videos stands out, where it becomes evident that sexist humor is a tool that allows them to go viral on the YouTube platform, also demonstrating how it influences adolescents today (Vila, 2021).

For Vila (2021), the success of certain YouTubers' content is based on addressing light topics, but with offensive, sexist, and even misogynistic humor; these tend to focus or target mainly the female gender; nonetheless, despite criticism of this type of content, the popularity of these figures continues to grow, since they are admired by their audience, who are mostly adolescents and young people, which can become a risk, since YouTubers use language that connects mainly with the millennial generation, and this is based on violence; changes in tone of voice are even detected when they use this language, mainly to capture the public's attention and generate a bond (Rego and Romero, 2016).

Given this scenario, the content generated by influencers represents a risk in the normalization of machismo and, therefore, in inciting gender-based violence, especially considering that followers feel identified with the content they generate, and even perceive them as friends, which creates a bond that contributes to the growth of their social media reach, as is the case of El Temach (Calvopiña and Tello, 2024), which is why this YouTuber is part of the object of study of this research.

Theoretical framework

The research focuses on the gender-based violence that manifests across digital platforms, mainly focusing on the normalization of machismo on YouTube, specifically through the content of creators such as El Temach. In this sense, it is considered necessary to conceptualize patriarchy as a system in which the inequalities between men and women underpin discriminatory behavior, in which power has historically been associated with the male figure (Pérez, 2022). Within patriarchy, hegemonic masculinity is the dominant form of gender that reinforces the system, since its practice maintains male domination over women (Taylor et al., 2013); therefore, machismo is a cultural expression of hegemonic masculinity, since it promotes and justifies men's superiority over women, highlighting masculine characteristics such as aggressiveness, independence, and dominance, while imposing feminine concepts such as weakness, dependence, and submission (Moral and Ramos, 2016).

In this sense, the phenomenon of the normalization of machismo refers to people becoming so accustomed to this type of behavior that they stop perceiving these violent actions and accept them as characteristics of the environment they are in (Martínez et al., 2024). Thus, these attitudes are seen as "normal" or socially accepted, which facilitates their creation through social media content that spreads them massively and constantly.

In turn, according to Olivera and Rojas (2022), machismo manifests symbolically, since it is often not easily perceptible; it tends to disguise itself as protection and becomes normalized through values that, over time, become socially accepted norms.

With respect to the YouTube platform, Martínez et al. (2022) note how YouTubers, as producers of audiovisual content, use a format that teaches their young audience to separate themselves from different social groups, drawing on humor and differentiating words such as "us" and "them," referring to minorities such as women tend to be, which calls for social responsibility on the part of content creators and for pushing young people to interact with this content more consciously, offering them workshops to train them.

On the other hand, YouTube's acceptance of the spread of gender-based violence refers to how the platform, through its interaction dynamics, can amplify and perpetuate violent attitudes and behaviors toward women (Crosas, 2016). Even though digital platforms, in this case YouTube, offer a space for free expression, they have also become an environment where gender-based violence manifests in different ways and constantly, affecting women's well-being and contributing to the normalization of these attitudes and behaviors.

For this research, the concepts previously addressed, such as machismo and the normalization of machismo through the YouTube platform, will be applied through a discourse analysis of El Temach's video posts on YouTube. Thus, machismo will be observed through the discourses, attitudes, and symbolic elements, such as the themes and visual elements that represent these attitudes as "acceptable" or "normal" among the audience of these platforms, highlighting how the content contributes to the normalization of machismo.

Methodology

This research will focus on analyzing the content of the six most viral videos published on the YouTube platform by influencer "El Temach" between 2021 and 2024, which were selected because they have a view count ranging between 400,000 and 2.1 million, placing them among the most-viewed videos on his channel compared to others. The videos selected are: "10 errores que comete un bato cuando le gusta a una morra" ("10 mistakes a guy makes when he likes a girl"), "10 maneras de enamorar/ enclochar a una morra" ("10 ways to make a girl fall for you / get her hooked"), "Los primeros 10 pasos del Alfa" ("The first 10 steps of the Alpha"), "10 pasos pa´subir su valor social" ("10 steps to raise your social value"), "¿Cómo reconstruir tu vida después de una relación?" ("How to rebuild your life after a relationship?") and "10 cosas que las mujeres dicen que quieren, pero les c*ga" ("10 things women say they want, but actually piss them off"). This period is examined because El Temach gained popularity quickly during those years and his videos began to go viral, attracting an audience beyond his usual community. These videos were chosen due to their high view count and the content they cover, since they focus on repetitive topics about interpersonal relationships, masculinity, and distorted views of women; through these videos, the aim is to show how the discourses and representations of both genders blend with humor and the normalization of macho behaviors.

To achieve the objective of this research, a discourse-analysis method will be applied at a descriptive level and application, allowing all discursive and narrative elements present in the most-viewed videos to be analyzed. As Vila (2021) does in her research, Misoginia youtuber; conseguir audiencia con humor sexista [YouTuber misogyny: gaining an audience through sexist humor], where she notes that it is necessary to analyze how macho humor and the normalization of gender stereotypes play an important role in attracting a new audience. The analysis will be carried out through a matrix of previously defined categories that will include elements such as nicknames, mockery, sexist comments, and displays of superiority over the female gender.

The scope of this research is basic, since its main purpose is to solve a problem through the search for and collection of data. As means used for obtaining it, documentary methods are used, given that the existing content about El Temach on YouTube is analyzed to study the themes and narratives present in his most-viewed videos that support the normalization of machismo, thereby obtaining the information.

The data-collection method will be centered on a frequency table, showing the number of times the categories appear in the videos; this table will also be accompanied by visual representations of screenshots of video excerpts showing patterns that contribute to the normalization of machismo, such as the language used within the video (nicknames, mockery made consciously or unconsciously, humor, among others), the central themes, and how they are expressed.

Additionally, the online tool Wordcloud is used as a visual tool to create a word cloud to clearly and concisely visualize the terms, phrases, and concepts most used in El Temach's most popular videos. The purpose of using this tool is to highlight the most frequent language patterns and gender discourses, to facilitate observation of the normalization of certain macho stereotypes and attitudes present in his texts.

It is important to note that, although the analysis will focus on a limited number of videos, the high number of views and the constant interaction they receive provide only a representative sample of El Temach's most influential content. The word cloud will complement the discourse analysis, as it will provide a visual representation of the vocabulary most used; this technique will make it possible to graphically identify the most recurrent terms and phrases in the videos selected.

To implement the word cloud, transcripts of the selected videos were extracted, after which unnecessary words, connectors, prepositions, and articles that add no value to the analysis were reduced, making it possible to highlight key terms and concepts directly related to the concepts of machismo, denigration of the female gender, among other expressions linked to the content of his videos; this text was entered into Wordcloud to generate the word cloud, where the largest words indicate the most recurrent ones; once the word cloud was created, a qualitative analysis of the most prominent words was carried out, to look for patterns related to the normalization of macho behaviors and how these are adopted and put into practice by his audience.

As the first part of the results, the word cloud generated from the transcripts of the six previously mentioned videos is presented. This visual tool makes it possible to more clearly identify the frequency with which certain terms appear in the videos. The word cloud is presented as a very important starting point that facilitates the interpretation and makes sense of the results.

The most frequent terms shown in the word cloud reflect different aspects of the main narrative El Temach tells in his videos. Terms such as: relationship, love, work, "compa" ("buddy"), and "morra" ("girl") make it possible to recognize that the videos' main themes are related to social and romantic interactions. On the other hand, words such as war, insecurity, and action point to issues that tend to be more closely tied to contexts of conflict or power, which are generally associated with male representation. Likewise, the repetitive use of the word "woman" indicates that, in the videos, the female gender is an important base given the frequency with which it comes up, which may be related to the representation and stereotyping of gender; in addition, terms such as "morra" and "alfa" ("alpha") not only represent the ideology present in the videos, but also highlight how El Temach uses language that, although it may seem offensive or funny, actually sustains machismo and gender inequality.

Based on the analysis of the videos published on YouTuber El Temach's channel, and using the table as part of the instrument designed to determine the frequency of machismo in his videos, to recognize the normalization and promotion of violence against the female gender through the content on his platform, the results for each of the categories worked with are presented below.

1. Machismo

Number 1 is machismo, and, as it is presented in the videos analyzed, it is possible to observe how the YouTuber constantly reinforces the idea that masculinity is built through control over women. This type of narrative not only normalizes macho behaviors, but also presents these behaviors as acceptable, or even admirable, norms to recreate.

1.1 Describes men as "the stronger sex"

One of the clearest examples of how El Temach reinforces machismo is the constant description of men as "the stronger sex." This is reflected in the frequency table, which shows how many times the videos mention or represent men as the stronger sex. In this case, the total is 39 mentions across the six videos (see Table 1).

Regarding the description of the stronger sex, the YouTuber constantly uses phrases and words during the recording of his videos that allude to this sense of power, as in the case of the video titled "10 steps to raise your social value," in which, throughout much of the video, he describes how to raise a man's social value in order to appeal to a woman, specifically stating in point 9 that exercising with discipline is key, but sets aside healthy training, replacing it with what he calls "war mode" training, through which he shares with his viewers the idea that they will be able to gain control, appear more attractive, and attract more women. Throughout this description, he reinforces the normalization of machismo by linking the notion that a man has to be strong in order to appeal to a woman.

Another case in which men are represented as "the stronger sex" in El Temach's videos is in the video titled "The first 10 steps of the Alpha," where El Temach presents men as "primitive" and states that: "His body is an animal, designed for hunting" (El Temach, 2023). Here, the idea that men must be strong, aggressive, and competitive is reinforced, constructing masculinity as power and dominance.

This aligns with what Olavarria (2001) states, noting that dominant masculinity grants power to men solely on the basis of their gender, allowing them to establish relationships in which they devalue women and make them dependent on them, a dynamic that is socially normalized.

1.2 Mocks non-traditional male roles, such as crying or showing their feelings.

The data obtained for this frequency shows similarity with point 1.1, placing it as one of the most used devices in his videos (see Table 1). The video titled "10 things women say they want, but actually piss them off" repeatedly shows how he mockingly imitates behaviors by men who tend to treat their partners or love interests in a gentlemanly, attentive way, saying that there are many men like this and that women don't like them, which is why you have to treat women differently (make them "battle" for you).

These attitudes reflect what Olavarria (2001) proposes, who argues that the attributes associated with dominant masculinity grant men different levels of power. He notes that those who show a fragile attitude or engage in activities considered "feminine" are seen as inferior or weak.

1.3. Describes men as self-sufficient.

Regarding machismo, male self-sufficiency is the frequency category showing the greatest representation, with a total of 55 occurrences (see Table 1). A fundamental aspect of the macho outlook El Temach reinforces is an idea of extreme independence, creating unrealistic expectations in his audience of what a man should be, which also limits their personal growth. The video titled "10 steps to raise your social value" is the one that draws most on this representation, noting on several occasions that a man doesn't need anyone else, that he can do everything on his own, urging viewers to travel to lower their social anxiety and to have an excess of male friends. As Bonino (1998, cited in Carabí and Segarra, 2000) notes, traditional masculinity has been built on the idea of a self-sufficient, controlling, and emotionally inhibited man, which reflects stereotypes in which men are supposed to be strong, with attitudes of dominance, rejecting emotional expression.

2. Normalization of machismo

The second category refers to the normalization of machismo, in which the material analyzed showed how this concept is a recurring theme on the influencer's channel, linking it mainly to topics of heterosexual relationships combined with humorous content, such as jokes and parodies. Sharing a perspective on dating that tends to be conservative and disadvantageous for women.

2.1 Reinforces gender roles assigned by society.

Regarding the reinforcement of gender roles assigned by society, the results obtained indicate that, after describing men as self-sufficient, he tends to reinforce the gender roles assigned by society (see Table 1), influencing his audience through his comments to act more aggressively or dominantly, preventing them from expressing their feelings in a healthy way. It is stated in the video "How to rebuild your life after a relationship?" that a man shouldn't show his feelings for too long, but should instead work on looking better, arguing that you can cry, but only for an hour, because you have to work in "war mode" to look better in the future. These results align with what is proposed in point 3.3, Humorous views of romantic relationships between men and women, in Vila's (2021) research, where she mentions how the YouTubers she studied talk about relationships between men and women and, as she notes on various occasions in their videos, how a man behaves at the end of a relationship, always stereotyping men and denying their feelings.

2.2 Promotes male superiority over women

According to the videos analyzed, the one titled "10 ways to make a girl fall for you / get her hooked" shows the greatest use of this classification (see Table 1), the normalization of machismo, since it states that a man has to come across as someone hard to get, someone "expensive," and also mentions that a man has to be radical with a woman, make women recognize him as different from the rest; it adds that telling a woman no makes her "enclocharse" (get hooked), and that this sets limits with a woman. These words perpetuate male superiority over women, which makes it difficult to eradicate machismo and achieve a more equal society. Likewise, and supported by what Olivera and Rojas (2022) state, it is confirmed that the words expressed in his videos are considered a type of indirect machismo that is difficult to perceive, mostly encouraging power relations and inequality of men over women.

3. Minimization of women through words.

In the resources analyzed, a consistent pattern was identified when referring to the female gender, since women are not usually addressed with respect, but rather offensive terms are used that contribute to the normalization of machismo.

3.1 Uses denigrating terms toward the female gender.

In the video titled: "The first 10 steps of the Alpha," phrases such as "this tasty girl" ("esta morra sabrosa"), "everyone wants to connect with the tasty ones," and "only talk to the pretty one" appear, so these terms perpetuate among men a way of validating a woman based on her looks, in addition to generating discrimination and harassment. This confirms what Gil (2022) notes, highlighting the normalization of sexist and macho behaviors toward women on social media. He notes that these behaviors are not regulated by the platforms where the content is published, which leads to greater exposure and a risk for the people who consume it, since it was found that they replicate these behaviors in their daily lives.

3.2 Objectifies women as an object of pleasure for men.

Analyzing this category shows that the YouTuber perpetuates machismo when it comes to objectifying women, since his content focuses mainly on men, which leads him to dehumanize women and impacts the way sexuality is viewed, in which men's pleasure takes priority. A key finding is the constant use of humor in El Temach's videos to convey macho ideas, which aligns with what Vila (2021) says about the role of humor in the persistence of gender stereotypes; according to Vila (2021), humor is an effective tool for avoiding criticism of macho attitudes or comments, thus turning what is offensive into something "fun" and therefore acceptable.

Finally, it can be recognized that El Temach's videos reinforce hegemonic masculinity, that is, they promote the idea that the way to achieve masculinity is through dominance over women, characterized by being competitive, aggressive, and dismissive of characteristics considered feminine. A clear example is reflected in the messages aimed at his viewers about the strong man who must stand out above the rest, not only through his achievements, but through aggressive, abusive, and competitive behavior.

In videos such as "10 steps to raise your social value," the YouTuber stresses that men must be competitive and must demonstrate their power over other men, with phrases like "don't be just another one of the crowd," "be the one who leads the group," and "whoever doesn't fight, obviously, has no future," reinforcing the idea that men must work to demonstrate their superiority over women and even over other men, which supports the construction of an aggressive masculinity, which, for Vila (2021), highlights male competition as a crucial point for understanding hegemony in power relations between genders.

Conclusions

The findings of this research were obtained from the analysis of the most viral videos published by YouTuber El Temach, which show that the content generated tends to promote macho discourse and perpetuate gender inequality. This is observed both in the recurring mention of references to hegemonic masculinity and in the minimization and objectification of women, since this type of content is considered offensive and denigrating to the female gender.

These results make it possible to conclude that digital video formats uploaded to the YouTube platform contribute significantly to the normalization of machismo on this social network; although only the six most viral videos by El Temach were selected for analysis, it was possible to identify that, through humor, El Temach influences an unequal view of gender that negatively impacts the perception of relationships and the development of male and female identities through the constant use of sexist jokes and derogatory comments toward women; his content also spreads the idea that men should be dominant, competitive, and aggressive, while women are represented as an object of desire; all of this facilitates the social acceptance of macho stereotypes and attitudes, behaviors that his audience reproduces in their daily lives; additionally, it can be inferred that El Temach not only fosters a dismissive and limited perspective on the female gender, but also reinforces the idea that men are superior and competent, promoting a toxic masculinity.

Another situation that can be observed is how it becomes evident that the platform lacks the regulation needed to address this type of content, since this deficiency contributes to the normalization of machismo, allowing this content to remain visible and accessible to anyone; this highlights the need for effective, responsible regulation that takes into account the social impact this can cause, thereby creating a more respectful digital environment.

At this point it is important to mention that this research is limited to the analysis of six of El Temach's videos, and although these videos represent a significant sample, they do not cover the entirety of the content generated by the influencer across various social media platforms such as Instagram, TikTok, or Facebook. Finally, due to the time constraints of this research, it is recommended that a more in-depth analysis be carried out of the content generated by the YouTuber on other platforms, such as TikTok, Instagram, and Facebook, to identify possible similar patterns. It is also advisable to expand the analysis to a larger number of videos published on YouTube, including a study of comments from his followers, in order to determine whether there is a correlation between the content generated and the attitudes reflected in these interactions. Future research could include comparative studies among other influencers who promote similar content in order to identify any pattern YouTube might use for the dissemination of sexist and macho discourse, and thereby learn what kind of policy could be implemented to regulate this content.
