Abstract.

Jalisco is, in the national context, one of the main food producing entities. The Jalisco countryside has been industrialized and therefore increased its added value and its relevance and significance for the national, regional and local economy. However, the progress of the field has been in line with environmental deterioration. Several recent events have revealed the scope and dimension of the environmental problems: this is the case, for example, of the avocado area of the municipality of San Gabriel. This essay analyzes the public policies that the Government of Jalisco has designed, in the administration of Governor Enrique Alfaro Ramírez in the period from 2018 to 2024, to contain this environmental deterioration. For this, the Governance Plan of this government is analyzed, and methodologically, we conducted a case study: that of the State of Jalisco. The conclusions are clear: the public policies designed have not been effective in their implementation, which is why the environmental problem widens and worsens, especially in high and low-scale agricultural activities, causing a serious limit to the potential of agro-industrial development in Jalisco. Small-scale agriculture of according to the National Institute of Geography and Information Statistics -INEGI- it refers to producers with up to 0.2 hectares of irrigation and up to 5 hectares of temporary, in which family work predominates, limited access to resources and production oriented predominantly to the self-consumption; In the 2013 report presented by the Committee on World Food Security (CFS), it defines low-scale agriculture, or also known as small-scale, in relation to two opposing elements, one is the largest commercial farms with hired labor and two are landless workers (2013: 12), this agriculture constitutes the basis of food security in many countries and is an important part of the social, economic and ecological landscape of All countries, despite being ignored by policies that tend to favor large-scale farmers, remain a significant number depending on the prevailing national context, which often combines agriculture with other activities in the rural non-farm economy.

Introduction

Jalisco is, in the national context, one of the main food-producing entities. The primary sector of the economic apparatus constitutes one of the fundamental pillars of Jalisco's economic development. For this reason, business owners, workers, and the different levels of government must look after the orderly development of the agricultural, mining, fishing, and forestry sectors.

The orderly development I am referring to is described through actions related to circular-economy proposals and takes concrete shape in the care and protection of the environment, or, seen another way, it could be described as the pursuit of economic development that seeks social well-being together with environmental care — more precisely, I am referring to responsibility toward people, toward production, and toward the environment.

In this important and far-reaching task, the participation of society together with government translates, in practice, into observing constitutional precepts, and then applying the concepts of governance and public policy — and at this point, the collective action of the 2018-2024 State Governance and Development Plan: Vision 2030, of the Government of the State of Jalisco, is analyzed.

Rule of law, participation by everyone in public decision-making, and effective implementation of public actions would, in theory, translate into effective development of economic activities in general and, in particular, of those related to the primary sector of the economy.

This paper proposes that the issue of public policies related to large- and small-scale agricultural activities and environmental problems must be framed within constitutional regulatory precepts in order to achieve their sustainable development. The purpose is to provide, in an exploratory manner, some notes on public policy in matters of social programs aimed at Jalisco's agricultural sector and to offer proposed solutions to the problems presented there, under the sustainable-development approach, of course. In this regard, it is worth mentioning that public policy within public administration is defined by Vargas (2007:127) as the organic structure of the State, that is, a set of organizations — ministries, councils, prefectures, public enterprises, courts, schools, hospitals, etc. — in which resources are combined: human, financial, technological, regulatory — which are transformed into public policies, public programs, services, and products, with the aim of addressing citizens' problems, controlling their behaviors, meeting their needs or demands, and ultimately achieving an impact on their social, political, and economic objectives — improving the quality of life of their inhabitants.

Jalisco 2018-2024 state governance and development plan: vision 2030

"At the State Government we understand that society can and must participate in making the fundamental decisions for the exercise of government and the design of public policy. In this way, our project promotes a governance process. This concept should not be taken as a synonym for good government, but rather as a new arrangement of authority and power in which actors make decisions and generate binding policies, as noted by Kenneth Hanf (Hanf and Jansen, 1998).

In this essay I will show that, although public policies exist on the part of the Government of Jalisco, designed under the administration of Governor Enrique Alfaro Ramírez in the period from 2018 to 2024, an analysis of the 2018-2024 Jalisco State Governance and Development Plan: Vision 2030 — in particular pillar number 5, the Economic Development pillar, under the theme of "Rural Development," and pillar number 6, the Sustainable Territorial Development pillar, under the theme of "Environmental Protection and Management," where one of the public problems highlighted is the negative impact of primary productive activities that deteriorate ecosystems and natural resources, generating degradation, deforestation, and greenhouse gas emissions — shows that, although a healthy environment is sought through the "Program to Promote Sustainable Agricultural Production," these policies have not been effective in their implementation, which is why the environmental problem widens and worsens, especially in high- and low-scale agricultural activities, causing a serious limit on the potential for agro-industrial development in Jalisco.

Furthermore, this administration has a social program titled "Support Program for Strategic Agricultural, Fishing, and Aquaculture Projects of the State of Jalisco," whose general objective is to increase productivity among small and medium-sized production units in the agricultural, fishing, and aquaculture sector in the State of Jalisco, through the acquisition of infrastructure and equipment that ensures the development of the sector; it is aimed at producers, and the type of support is monetary and non-repayable. Likewise, the Government of the State of Jalisco offers, through various social programs with non-repayable financial support, assistance to farmers for acquiring technologies that help conserve the environment and contribute to climate action, through training services in the use and management of technologies to make farmers more productive, providing producers and rural-society actors with knowledge to access and actively participate in technology-related mechanisms, and enabling them to increase their income, among other benefits.

Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development

In the mission statement of the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development, we can see what it literally states: "We are a Ministry responsible for the State's sustainable rural development, promoting productivity in the agricultural and fishing sector, the construction and maintenance of productive rural infrastructure, the conservation of natural resources, the health and safety of production processes, training and support for producers, and the competitiveness of farm products in national and international markets that increase the profitability of productive activities"; a statement that remains only on paper and not in practice.

The institutional vision of the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development literally states: "By 2024, the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development will be a forward-looking, professional institution and a leading reference for sustainable rural development internationally, with the capacity to encourage and promote investment in rural infrastructure and the economic development of the countryside, through the conservation of natural resources for the benefit of rural producers; we are building rural capacities and the public goods needed to add value to Jalisco products for the export market"; three years into the current administration, the annual reports show no results toward what is stated here, a result of the absence of public policy, despite the existence of financial support programs. One of the public problems presented is the negative impact of primary productive activities that deteriorate ecosystems and natural resources, generating degradation, deforestation, and greenhouse gas emissions, where a healthy environment is sought through the "Program to Promote Sustainable Agricultural Production."

In this 2019 National Agricultural Survey, the use of chemical fertilizers was the technology most used by agricultural production units with commercialization, used by 67%; in open-field agriculture, native seed was used by 61.8%, the use of a hoe or coa by 60.2%, chemical herbicides by 59.0%, and chemical insecticides by 45.7%; it should be clarified that the sum of the partial figures exceeds 100% because some production units reported more than one of these practices.

Small-scale agriculture, according to the National Institute of Statistics and Geography (INEGI), refers to producers with up to 0.2 hectares of irrigated land and up to 5 hectares of rain-fed land, in which family labor predominates, along with limited access to resources and production oriented predominantly toward self-consumption. Small-scale farming, to which small-scale producers belong, is of enormous importance to the country's agricultural economy, since small production units generate 54 percent of Mexico's food production, as well as 80 percent of paid, contracted employment; small producers are also caretakers of biodiversity. The main crops grown by small-scale producers come from the milpa: corn, beans, and squash.

In the 2013 report presented by the Committee on World Food Security (CFS), low-scale agriculture — also known as small-scale agriculture — is defined in relation to two opposing elements: large-scale commercial farms with hired labor, and landless workers (2013:12). This agriculture forms the basis of food security in many countries and is an important part of the social, economic, and ecological landscape of all countries.

This report notes that small producers are the main investors in small-scale agriculture, whose production system is complex and dynamic at the same time, and that in order to formulate policies that effectively support their own investments, including investments by other agents such as the public and private sectors, it is necessary to have an understanding of some of the main characteristics of small-scale agriculture related to investment; labor is emphasized as a key element of small-scale agriculture.

Historical data indicate that small-scale agriculture, when properly backed by public policy and investment, has the capacity to effectively contribute to food security and food sovereignty, as well as substantially and significantly to economic growth, job creation, poverty reduction, the empowerment of underserved and marginalized groups, and the reduction of spatial and socioeconomic inequalities. In a favorable political and institutional environment, it can contribute to the sustainable management of biodiversity and other natural resources while also preserving cultural heritage.

It is emphasized that public policy is lacking in designing a governance framework for agriculture and rural development that supports the multifunctional roles small-scale agriculture plays in development (…); in many cases, this requires specific national-level coordination and governance mechanisms among the different ministries, public administration, and relevant stakeholders. Despite having been ignored by policies that tend to favor large-scale farmers, small-scale farmers remain significant in number depending on the prevailing national context, which often combines agriculture with other non-agricultural rural economic activities.

The Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development reported in July 2021 that Jalisco was among the five Mexican states with a five-star agricultural production rating, with 35,539,638 tons of agricultural products, noting that there are agricultural goods that, without being the highest in volume, contribute the greatest economic value to the state — the case of tequila agave — results achieved thanks to the work and important contribution of national producers who day after day uphold Mexico's name and who, with their agricultural expertise, contribute to production that meets meticulous quality standards.

Conclusions

The public policies designed by the current Government of the State of Jalisco have not been effective in their implementation, which is why the environmental problem widens and worsens, especially in high- and low-scale agricultural activities, causing a serious limit on the potential for agro-industrial development in Jalisco, and the environmental problem must be framed within constitutional regulatory precepts in order to achieve sustainable development, and, as Vargas (2007) states, public policies must be established within a framework that encompasses the common good, without distinction of ideology or partisanship, and with the firm determination that they must be made entirely free of private interests, with a spirit of service and, essentially, with transparency and accountability.

The participation of society together with government is important and far-reaching, in order to translate into practice, observing constitutional precepts, an effective governance and public policy framework.
