Abstract.

Telework as a modality of remote work and the news about it that are issued regularly, have a direct and current relationship. Telework marks the way in which the companies have to employ their collaborators to perform as virtual collaborators and/or with hybrid work. The news reports the latest developments on the subject in various parts of the world. This essay reports on telework trends as of January 2023. Determining the current trends of teleworking in the world of business is important since this mode of work has had an upturn since 2020 and to date. Knowing them allows us to determine the state of the art of the modality of work. It was carried out through the review of news programmed in the Gmail account on telework, which generated nine categories to perform the analysis and determination of trends such as the combination of telework with hybrid work, the demand for flexible work, the rethinking of work schemes, the administration of human resources with Artificial Intelligence, the use of platforms for the management of telework and hybrid work.

Keywords:

teleworking, news, hybrid work, trends

Introduction

Writing about teleworking as a work modality is relevant, because it has boomed since 2020, when isolation was mandated due to the COVID-19 pandemic. The working life of the population experienced a critical situation: continuing to operate in the traditional way exposed collaborators to infection, while stopping altogether would affect society in every sphere. Teleworking, developed with the support of Information and Communication Technologies (ICTs), was the way companies chose to preserve jobs and business operations; their collaborators adopted telework from their homes and carried out their daily work duties.

After approximately a year and a half to two years of health lockdown, the population around the world began returning to semi-in-person and in-person productive activities, taking precautions to avoid falling ill. By 2022, activities had normalized into a new normal that included semi-in-person and in-person work, without ruling out the continuation of the virtual work format. Because it was a work modality that saved companies from bankruptcy and allowed employees to keep working and earning a wage to survive, it is pertinent that telework be studied, researched, and its employability tracked in working life after the pandemic. It was an unusual situation: although telework is not a new way of working within an organization, it did receive support and demand for its use given the characteristics the pandemic required in order to keep working. This is how the question that this essay seeks to answer arose:

What trend has telework been showing in the workplace?

Telework continues in various organizations, even though the pandemic was officially declared over on May 9, 2023, by the Ministry of Health in Mexico. Saénz (2023). Since the resumption of some productive activities between 2020 and 2022, work experiences with telework arose, given the position it gained in formal workplaces at companies, which is important to analyze.

To achieve this objective, a review was carried out of the news received daily through Gmail, scheduled in an email account. An analysis was performed of 79 news items received during the first month of 2023. Over 23 days in January, a total of 79 news items were recorded. The news originated from Spain, Latin America, Ecuador, Argentina, Peru, Colombia, Bolivia, and Mexico. Why the first month of 2023? Because it was during the second half of 2022 that work activities had been undergoing regulation after the pandemic, and at the start of 2023, during the first month, the labor adjustments that had been taking place were recorded. It became widely known that telework had played a leading role during the health emergency, but now that most companies had returned to in-person work, information emerged worth considering as part of the current dynamics of telework, from the standpoint of businesses, workers, and governments.

The review and analysis led to the creation of nine categories for classifying the information and sharing the findings obtained. The categories on which the review was based were: business agreements, work modality, public administration, international outlook, current situation, and the legality of telework.

Development

Telework has shown a variety of reactions and actions on the part of those involved in its implementation. There are expected reactions documented in the news, but also other, less common ones that arise from the act of working through telework.

What is telework? It is a formal work modality that emerged in the 1970s in the United States, in the wake of an energy crisis at that time. It was suggested that work be moved to workers' homes, avoiding commutes so that they would not consume as much gasoline, which was then scarce. It was called telework, along with other terms found in the published literature: remote work, distance work, home office, teleworking, etc., whose requirement is that it be carried out from a place other than the company's premises. In recent years, given the technological pervasiveness of all human and productive activities, another requirement was added: doing so with and through Information and Communication Technologies (ICTs).

The COVID-19 pandemic rescued it from labor irrelevance, as it became the business strategy used to safeguard employees' health while continuing business operations. It was within this defined framework of telework that the analysis of seventy-nine news items was carried out.

First category: Positive impact

Aspects in favor of telework. What stands out is improved work-family balance (with clear exceptions), eating meals at home, which is less costly and home-prepared. Accessible time to rest, leisure suited to workers' needs, reduced expenses from not leaving home, and decreased stress with more effective stress management. (Santana, 2023)

Second category: environmental impact

Reducing environmental pollution by cutting down on commutes between home and the office represents a significant reduction in carbon emissions (Santana, 2023).

Suggestion 6. Tools to make telework efficient.

Telework has required an adjustment period for users, which is why technological tools are recommended to streamline it and make it efficient. Below are seven of them, explaining the benefit each provides.

1. Microsoft Teams

An ideal platform for collaborating and communicating with others instantly.

Benefits: allows real-time sharing of Word, Excel, and PowerPoint documents. Offers calls and video calls at the click of a button, scheduled from your own calendar. The app can be downloaded to your phone.

2. Mobitel virtual switchboard

This tool facilitates communication between customers and companies. Through different phone extensions, the switchboard redirects the customer to the specialists who resolve their needs. Additionally, being virtual, it handles calls 24/7.

3. Slack

As an alternative to Microsoft Teams, this platform allows for team project management, facilitating collaboration and communication among its members. Its free version offers the option to store up to 5 gigabytes of files, as well as view the 10,000 most recent messages across its various channels. The platform allows private messages to be sent to collaborators within the same organization, using gifs, stickers, files, and emoticons.

4. Google Drive

Storing large files might seem complicated, but this tool makes it easy. In its free version, Google Drive allows storage of up to 15 gigabytes; paid versions offer the option to increase available storage capacity. It facilitates the creation of Office-suite documents in real time, and allows files of any type to be uploaded. It also creates survey or exam forms.

5. WeTransfer

This tool is ideal for sending files (without necessarily uploading them to a storage folder). WeTransfer's free version allows files of up to 2 gigabytes to be sent, while its Pro version has a capacity of 200 gigabytes, and the Premium version has unlimited capacity.

6. Microsoft To-Do

This is a smart planner that allows tasks to be updated both daily and weekly. Among its benefits is the ability to share lists with friends, colleagues, and loved ones, and to keep to-do lists on your computer or phone, since it is multiplatform. Due dates can be added and tasks can be tracked.

7. Meet

Remote work will always involve constant communication and human interaction. Google Meet is a simple way to make calls (and video calls) in a group, synchronously. If you need to coordinate a team, attend a meeting with an important client, or perhaps connect with potential partners, you can do so immediately. The platform also offers the option to share your desktop, screen, or presentation. (Rodríguez, 2023)

Art Marketing, a digital marketing company, decided to keep telework as its regular work format and gave up its physical office. It is a small company made up of 4 people who shared their thoughts on telework:

Patricia Alejandro, Digital Marketing consultant

"It has been a very easy adaptation because …. we had all our processes digitized. We have collaborative work tools ……. we know we can telework from anywhere on the planet by connecting via wifi" (Santana, 2023, p.65).

Leire Navaridas, director of international accounts at Art Marketing

Our relationship with clients hasn't changed …. compared to the in-person model. Now …. when we have the inaugural meeting for a new account in Madrid… we go to the client. …… mostly they're outside of Spain, …. Telework …. allows us to eat healthily at home. (Santana, 2023, p.65)

Irene Lozano, media manager at Art Marketing.

"Saving myself …. the time and energy spent commuting during rush hour… being in a better mood, … it lets me align with my children's school schedule and be more rested and calm …, without the added stress of traffic and rushing" (Santana, 2023, p.34).

A different case was that of the Transcom employee in the city of León, Spain: a judge granted a teleworking request so the employee could care for his father, who had dementia, in 2022. The company had denied the telework request, and in court the judge upheld the employee's request based on the evidence presented (Valdez, 2023).

An employee in Catalonia lost a lawsuit after being discovered teleworking at a "chiringuito" (a small beachside establishment offering food and drinks, with an outdoor terrace or seating area for customers — the term used in Spain), where she took photos and shared them on her Facebook account, on which her boss appeared as a friend and saw the photos. The boss reported this to the company's human resources department: a pharmaceutical company that decided to sanction the employee — hired in January 2018 — for what it considered a breach of the agreed conditions of service, which fell under the Flexiday and Home Office telework modalities. These conditions had not been put in writing, which the judge noted in the final ruling, in which the employee did not prevail (Galán Feced, 2023).

A Canadian company installed spy software on an employee's computer and was thus able to justify her dismissal and even win compensation for "time theft." This already sets a legal precedent as a punishment for unproductivity. A Canadian civil court ruled in favor of a company and ordered its former employee to pay compensation for hours lost during working hours, something that could be verified with tracking software installed on her computer.

It is a controversial topic: with digital advances, corporate surveillance now goes beyond clocking in with a fingerprint — new software now allows employers to monitor what employees write and even take screenshots of their activities. Karlee Besse is the worker who is setting a precedent in defense matters for companies facing wrongful dismissal lawsuits, which in turn is sparking debate about digital privacy and workers' rights. Her case spread from Canada to the world, after a Canadian court upheld charges based on files that exceeded the allotted budget and were late, which her employer obtained through tracking software on her computer (Erosa, 2023).

In the Canary Islands, Spain, a call center employee found a way to take several hours of break time and avoid doing his job of answering customer calls. He cheated his company out of handling customers. He would call himself to avoid having to deal with customer service, appearing in the online system as busy but not working. The man had been working since 2019 and was only discovered by the company in October 2020, having apparently called himself at least 150 times. Faced with the situation, the Spanish worker claimed he had been unfairly harmed and sued the company for firing him, a legal action that was dismissed by a court in Tenerife in July 2021 (Pulzo, 2023).

Fifth category: Work modality.

The dynamics of remote work are a crucial factor in employees' decisions to stay with a company, of course referring to the most talented ones. Entrepreneur (2023) notes that the days of telework seem numbered at some companies, as it is increasingly common for corporations to ask their workers to return to in-person work. The pandemic forced lockdown and telework; now large companies are gradually returning to in-person work, while others have eliminated remote work altogether. They cite productivity, continuing to hold the belief that employees are more productive when working in person, since proximity to their colleagues fosters better communication and helps keep everyone aligned on shared objectives. Despite this, there are voices saying that 2023 will be a year of telework, and it is essential to ask what employees actually want. Harvard University economist Prithwiraj Choudhury notes in his article published in Wired that companies that refuse to adapt their work scheme to include remote work run the risk of losing their most talented employees. He stated in his piece that:

"There are two types of companies… one will embrace work from anywhere, … the second is in denial: … those companies will lose their workforce. Those … trying to turn back the clock … … will be forced to catch up" (Fernández, 2023, p.7).

Faced with the risk of an economic recession, executives surveyed by the consulting firm Ernst & Young stated that they see telework as an option for cutting costs, and so will prioritize remote work to save money. Tech leaders are doubling down on investment in telework (Sánchez, 2023). This suggests that during 2023 they will continue to support remote work for their collaborators, combined with the hybrid scheme already used in 2022 (Entrepreneur, 2023).

The human capital of teleworking organizations will travel during the summer, as reported by Gatica (2023): a Rankmi study revealed that this trend is called "workation" (work and vacation), an alternative for those who take advantage of work flexibility to travel. Companies grant this benefit with the aim of increasing performance and well-being among remote workers. Employees must inform and agree with their bosses on the dates when they will take this benefit. What is favorable is the new space that allows the collaborator to feel motivated and energized. It improves well-being against work stress at the end of the workday. Setting workation guidelines is essential, fixing working hours. The remaining time is free, with disconnection (Gatica, 2023).

Relevant information on the telework modality is what is being said and done in Mexico. Portillo (2023) mentions that it is home office and notes it has shown notable growth since then. It was once looked down on and rejected, but now the benefits it offers for workers and companies have been recognized. He describes it as a new professional trend, due to the systems emerging for quality of work and its control. One advantage that home office offers in Mexico is that it contributes to increased productivity at companies. It also reduces production costs through savings on energy and maintenance of the physical structure. The work environment improves for those who telework; the condition for these advantages is that it be organized and set out properly and well implemented. (Portillo, 2023) Another truth reported in the news is that companies do not cover the expenses generated by teleworking in employees' homes, specifically electricity and internet (Perú Retail, 2023).

Sixth category: Public administration

News from the public sector was the least found; public administration is an area of opportunity for the telework modality. An example found is from the government of the state of Nuevo León, Mexico, which in January 2023 announced it was extending the telework modality due to a resurgence of COVID-19 infections. Several agencies complied with the measure (Reporte Índigo, 2023).

Seventh category: International outlook

The Russia-NATO cold war. Attention is focused on remote workers who moved to other countries and became suspected of spying for the West. Virtual work is at the center of debate in Russia, where it is considered a threat to national security. In terms of legislation, a possible ban on remote work in certain professions is being debated (Euronews, 2023).

A country where telework has not declined is Estonia, to the point that it has created a telework network initiative designed so that workers can choose between working from home or from a space created for digital nomads. Various organizations see an opportunity to diversify work through telework, by changing settings and breaking with work routine. This may involve vacations or alternative activities that the work modality allows them to pursue as personal interests, adding value (Euronews, 2023).

Remote work in soccer is a story that stands out from Spain: head coach Alberto González of the Linares team implemented it. It works under a hybrid scheme. The team's board supports the initiative. The team is based in Jaén and coach González is in Málaga. It was a personal situation of Alberto González's that led him to make use of remote work, obviously with the support of the team's board. It presented the usual details of the modality, but it remains an innovation in soccer (González, 2023).

Workers at the newspaper El País in Spain protested the order to return to in-person work immediately. The workers expressed their absolute rejection of the measure, which took effect on January 30, 2023. They joined together and made proposals against the measure in order to negotiate it (Es noticia Veracruz, 2023).

In Latin America, the experience of other nations is needed to address telework. As in the whole world, the arrival of COVID-19 also positively impacted this region, giving telework a leading role in the workplace. Three years later, part of that workforce remained on telework, while other organizations opted to return to in-person and/or hybrid work, especially in technical areas. A Mexican human resources expert commented on the study The World of Work Today:

"They can no longer turn back on the flexible scheme, which is why it is necessary to find ways to support remote and hybrid work" (Osorio, Idarrága, 2023, p.56).

Likewise, in another study, teleworkers reported feeling relaxed and happy, with a balance between their work and personal life. This reflects the progress that the remote work model has made. They pointed to a distinction between the terms home office and telework, noting that home office is temporary while telework is indefinite, given its acceptance and positive perception in Latin America, which has put it on equal footing (Osorio, Idarrága, 2023).

In Ecuador, the number of teleworkers is declining; now past the pandemic, an assessment is being made of the use of remote work. It is noted that telework is not losing strength; what is happening is that there is still a lack of awareness of the laws and the benefits that telework can bring to the organization and to work-family balance. They moved to a hybrid schedule due to the need for in-person presence and human interaction. It has been noted that the country still does not have the digital standards required. When young people demand this work modality, that will be the moment it needs to be addressed (Zapata, 2023).

Peru presents four challenges for telework within its territory in 2023, which will test the labor sector in achieving the right conditions for teleworking.

  1. Clear legislation: home office must be regulated considering the variables involved in connecting remotely, in order to avoid excesses in working hours. The new law incorporates precisely a series of parameters regarding compensation for workers' expenses (electricity, internet, etc.), and obligations regarding health and safety (suitable spaces for home office work, maintenance of technological equipment, among others), and establishes the mandatory need for a written agreement in which the company clearly stipulates all agreed points.

  1. Technology for work monitoring: By defining the type of schedule to which employees are assigned (mixed or fully home office), it can be managed objectively in terms of clock-ins, clock-outs, permissions, absences, overtime, and lateness, since this provides benefits to both employers and workers; one of the main challenges is being able to implement innovative technological tools that allow for the correct management of employees' time.

  2. Boosting productivity: A better way of organizing tasks under this modality will yield the planned productivity.

  3. Internet connectivity and compensation: In line with legislation, it will be possible to have the technological services needed to achieve this. (Redacción Perú21, 2023)

In Peru, Law No. 31572, known as the Telework Law, went into effect on January 1, 2023, incorporating a series of regulations and items that remote work had not previously covered. The draft regulation was published by the Ministry of Labor and Employment Promotion (MTPE), and is in the process of receiving comments from both employers and workers, to later be announced (Redacción Perú21, 2023).

In Colombia, many companies are seeking to consolidate the hybrid work model to be ready for the new economy. Adopting technology and adapting work environments suited to carrying out tasks permanently, with well-being, satisfaction, and improved productivity, is necessary. Creating positive experiences within this hybrid work model is a challenge for everyone. There is a prediction that companies will provide technological support to remote workers (DPLNews, 2023). The 100% remote work model has dropped by 95% in Colombia.

However, 85.8% of employees feel happier when their workday is split between the office and home office. This matters, since among younger populations such as millennials (people born between 1981 and 1993), 64.8% would not be willing to return to a 100% in-person work model. Likewise, this trend is notable among baby boomers (people born between 1949 and 1968), none of whom are willing to return under this same scheme. Colombian companies must rethink the value proposition for their staff, since what society is asking for is precisely a balance between personal and work life. Finally, Colombians are happier splitting their workday between the office and home office, and 72% would like to work at least one day a week somewhere other than their place of residence (Vanguardia, 2023).

Eighth category: Current situation

Home office changed the world of work for many workers. Of those who experienced it, 25% do not want to give it up, and of the rest, they are split between hybrid work at 50% and in-person work at the other 25%.

Jorge Castañeda Morales, founder of Kairós Consultores, explained that 25% want to return to working in person and the other 25% definitely do not want to return to offices. He revealed that a myth has developed that employers want all their workers to return and that employees never want to go back. He stated that this is false, or at least not true, in many cases (Cárdenas, 2022).

The International Labour Organization (ILO) revealed in its surveys that 43% of workers are not satisfied with the hours they work. It also states that telework benefited productivity, work-life balance, and even employees' health. It recommends maintaining flexible working hours and generalizing telework across many sectors, because during the pandemic these measures benefited productivity, work-life balance, and even employees' health.

Telework, which had already proven its effectiveness in crises such as the 2011 Japan earthquake, "showed that it can be applied on a large scale, changing the nature of work," the ILO recalls. The contributions of new evidence showing greater flexibility for workers are positive for both businesses and employees. (Broto, 2023) Be careful! The ILO warns that longer working hours harm health. Excessive schedules increase stress, anxiety, or job dissatisfaction, with higher rates of depression and alcoholism (Broto, 2023).

Rethinking traditional work schemes to implement formulas that allow for jobs fostering greater flexibility and life balance is a positive benchmark following the COVID-19 pandemic, stated the International Labour Organization (ILO). There are severe imbalances that have been exposed and urgently need to be addressed. Mexico has one of the worst work-life balance levels in the world. The ILO states that "reducing working hours and organizing working time more flexibly can benefit economies, businesses, and workers, and lay the groundwork for a better, healthier balance between work and private life" (Morales, Fredes, 2023). Relevant data from the National Survey of Occupation and Employment (ENOE) from November of last year (2022) — the latest available data — show that, of the total workforce, 26.5% work more than 48 hours a week, that is, 15.6 million people. Meanwhile, 7.6% are underemployed, equivalent to 4.5 million people. Both measures together cover one in every three workers in the country. (Morales, Fredes, 2023) Adverse working conditions with excessive hours, or the opposite, have given rise to the labor phenomenon known as "the great resignation," which led many people to leave their jobs in search of better working conditions; work-life balance moved to the forefront of social and labor-market concerns (Morales, Fredes, 2023).

Fraud is a problem that can be exacerbated by telework. Workers are not in the office every day as they used to be, which has forced organizations to rethink many of their processes. The finance area has faced a new challenge: managing business expenses now that employees work remotely. Verifying and controlling disbursements made by an organization's employees is not a simple task. Moreover, expense fraud is even harder to identify when employees don't come into the office daily and there is no overall view of their activities. There is a solution to combat this problem: corporate cards paired with an expense management tool. Both solutions together make it possible to track, in real time and easily and effectively, the disbursements made by collaborators (El Mundo Financiero, 2023).

Telework: a challenge for marriages. The boundaries between work and family time disappear; if work is not planned, couples can face conflicts. Both husbands and wives who worked from home became more involved in household and family tasks; this also seemed to promote an increase in feelings of role conflict, psychological isolation from work, and feelings of guilt regarding their professional work (Betancourt Figueroa, 2023). Employers must set realistic expectations about how much work their remote employees can effectively handle, showing greater understanding of couples' at-home work situations.

The findings indicate that husbands with flexibility in their work schedules can provide more support to their partners in order to complete their own telework tasks (Betancourt Figueroa, 2023). Anyone who has done home office, whether partially or full-time, knows that the modality has radically changed their habits. A study of more than 100,000 professionals determined what behaviors changed and/or were acquired during remote work.
