Socio-educational intervention “I take care of myself; we take care of each other”: a strategy for psychosocial well-being at work

Intervención socioeducativa “Me cuido, Nos cuidamos”: una estrategia para el bienestar psicosocial en el trabajo

Abstract.

This article presents the results of the socio-educational intervention “I Take Care of Myself, We Take Care of Each Other”, aimed at promoting psychosocial well-being in the workplace through a preventive and promotional approach. The strategy focused on strengthening self-care, social support, and collective co-responsibility as key components to address psychosocial occupational risks. The intervention was implemented with administrative personnel in the public sector through a participatory workshop consisting of 10 in-person sessions. The activities combined experiential dynamics, collective reflection, and educational content on emotional management, empathetic communication, mental health, and organizational culture. To assess the impact, the Psychosocial Risk Factors in the Workplace Questionnaire (adapted version by Aranda-Beltrán and Pando-Moreno, 2014) was applied pre- and post-intervention, complemented by a post-intervention focus group. Quantitative findings revealed a significant reduction in psychosocial risk levels in dimensions such as job demands, workplace conditions, workload, and social interaction. Qualitative results showed increased awareness of mental health, a stronger sense of belonging, and enhanced commitment to collective well-being practices among participants. It is concluded that participatory socio-educational interventions are a viable and replicable strategy for improving the psychosocial work climate and can contribute to the integration of mental health into institutional occupational health policies.

Keywords:

Psychosocial well-being, socio-educational intervention, participatory approach

Resumen.

Este artículo presenta los resultados de la intervención socioeducativa “Me cuido, Nos cuidamos”, orientada a promover el bienestar psicosocial en el trabajo desde un enfoque preventivo-promocional. La estrategia se centró en fortalecer el autocuidado, el apoyo social y la corresponsabilidad colectiva como ejes para mitigar los riesgos psicosociales laborales. La intervención fue implementada con personal administrativo del sector público a través de un taller participativo de 10 sesiones presenciales. Las actividades combinaron dinámicas vivenciales, reflexión colectiva y contenidos formativos sobre gestión emocional, comunicación empática, salud mental y cultura organizacional. Para evaluar el impacto, se aplicó el Cuestionario de Evaluación de Factores Psicosociales en el Trabajo (versión adaptada de Aranda-Beltrán y Pando-Moreno, 2014) en modalidad pre y post, complementado con un grupo focal posterior a la intervención. Los hallazgos cuantitativos reflejan una disminución significativa en niveles de riesgo psicosocial en dimensiones como exigencias laborales, condiciones del entorno de trabajo, carga de trabajo e interacción social. Desde el análisis cualitativo, las y los participantes reportaron una mayor conciencia sobre su salud mental, fortalecimiento del sentido de pertenencia y compromiso con prácticas colectivas. Se concluye que las intervenciones socioeducativas con enfoque participativo representan una estrategia viable y replicable para la mejora del clima psicosocial laboral, y pueden contribuir a la integración de la salud mental en las políticas institucionales de salud ocupacional.

Introduction

Psychosocial well-being at work constitutes a fundamental component for the comprehensive health of individuals, as well as for the efficiency and sustainability of organizations. In the contemporary context, characterized by complex work dynamics, an increase in chronic stress, and transformations in work environments, psychosocial factors have established themselves as key determinants of workers' mental and emotional health. As international organizations such as the World Health Organization (WHO) and the International Labour Organization (ILO) point out, addressing these factors should not focus solely on risk prevention, but also on the active promotion of safe, participatory, and emotionally healthy work environments.

In Mexico, the relevance of this issue has been recognized through the Official Mexican Standard NOM-035-STPS-2018, which establishes the obligation to identify, analyze, and prevent psychosocial risk factors, as well as to promote a favorable organizational environment. This regulation presents significant challenges for the public sector, where rigid hierarchical structures, administrative overload, and a lack of recognition can negatively affect the quality of work life of administrative personnel.

Consequently, it has become essential to design and implement comprehensive strategies that, from an educational and participatory approach, strengthen individual and collective capacities for self-care, emotional management, and the development of support networks. Literature has documented the effectiveness of socio-educational interventions in organizational contexts, especially those promoting empowerment, co-responsibility, and the transformation of institutional culture (Montano et al., 2014; Leka & Jain, 2010). Likewise, Karasek's (1979) Demand-Control model provides a useful theoretical framework to understand how the combination of high job demands with low control or autonomy influences the emergence of psychosocial discomfort, justifying interventions that modify this equation from the relational and structural base of work.

This research proposes a socio-educational intervention with a preventive-promotional approach aimed at administrative personnel in the public sector. Its objective is to foster psychosocial well-being in the work environment through the strengthening of self-care, social support, and collective co-responsibility. Through the design, application, and evaluation of a participatory workshop, it seeks to generate evidence regarding the viability, relevance, and impact of such strategies to integrate mental health as a transversal pillar in organizational management.

In contemporary work environments, psychosocial risks have become one of the primary challenges for occupational health and organizational well-being. Factors such as workload overload, lack of autonomy, weak interpersonal relationships, and scarce recognition generate not only stress and emotional exhaustion, but also physical and cognitive wear that affects productivity and job satisfaction. According to the World Health Organization (WHO, 2022) and the International Labour Organization (ILO, 2024), mental health must be considered a core component of occupational safety and health policies, requiring comprehensive and preventive strategies addressing both the causes and consequences of psychosocial risks in the workplace.

In this context, the socio-educational intervention “I take care of myself, We take care of each other” was designed and implemented as an innovative initiative to promote psychosocial well-being among public sector administrative workers. The intervention adopts a preventive-promotional approach emphasizing self-care, social support, and collective co-responsibility as fundamental pillars for building healthier and more collaborative work environments. This approach relies on health education principles and participatory methodologies that recognize the importance of workers' active involvement in identifying problems, reflecting on their experiences, and co-constructing solutions aimed at improving their work environment.

The program was carried out through a participatory workshop consisting of ten sessions integrating experiential activities, collective reflection spaces, and theoretical-practical content on emotional regulation, empathetic communication, and organizational culture. Through this process, participants developed individual and group skills that enabled them to recognize psychosocial risk factors and adopt healthier coping and interaction strategies.

Furthermore, the intervention incorporated quantitative and qualitative evaluation techniques that provided a comprehensive understanding of its effects. The pre- and post-application of the Psychosocial Risk Factors in the Workplace Questionnaire (Aranda-Beltrán & Pando-Moreno, 2014), along with the qualitative analysis of a post-intervention focus group, yielded valuable evidence regarding the evolution of perceptions concerning the work environment, stress levels, and social relationships among participants.

This document presents the background, methodological framework, and main results of the “I take care of myself, We take care of each other” intervention, highlighting its contribution to strengthening the psychosocial well-being of administrative personnel. Likewise, it reflects on the implications of integrating socio-educational approaches into occupational health policies and the potential for replicating this type of model in other institutional contexts, underscoring the need for organizational strategies promoting mental health, equity, and participatory well-being in workplaces.

Theoretical Framework

Occupational health, understood as the set of activities intended to promote and protect workers' health, has evolved toward a more comprehensive approach that considers not only classic workplace hazards—such as physical, chemical, and biological factors—but also psychosocial factors affecting emotional and social well-being in work spaces (ILO, 2022). Within this framework, psychosocial risks refer to workplace conditions that affect health through chronic stress mechanisms, such as excessive workload, role ambiguity, lack of autonomy, poor recognition, and low-quality work relationships (Bakker & Demerouti, 2017).

Psychosocial Factors at Work

In Mexico, NOM-035-STPS-2018 recognizes the need to identify, analyze, and prevent psychosocial risks in the work environment, including overload, working conditions, performance compensation, interpersonal relationships, and leadership. This standard promotes institutional interventions recognizing the organizational environment as a determinant of overall worker health. According to the Demand-Control model (Karasek, 1979), when job demands increase while autonomous control over work decreases, the likelihood of job burnout, diminished commitment, and physical or mental health disorders escalates.

Socio-educational Approaches and Well-being Promotion

Promoting psychosocial well-being requires structured, context-sensitive interventions supported by participatory models. Health education has established itself as a key tool to encourage empowerment, co-responsibility, and personal and collective development for self-care (WHO, 2022). Socio-educational interventions enable the creation of environments for dialogue, reflection, and collaborative learning, which contribute to resignifying work practices and driving sustainable transformations in organizational culture (Montano et al., 2014).

From a participatory perspective, Leka and Jain (2010) argue that intervention strategies actively involving workers have greater impact and sustainability by fostering a sense of belonging and co-responsibility. This is particularly relevant in public sector work environments, where structural and hierarchical constraints complicate the implementation of preventive organizational policies.

Mental Health Intervention Models in Occupational Health

International bodies such as the World Health Organization (WHO) and the International Labour Organization (ILO) have highlighted the need to integrate mental health into occupational safety and health policies. Recent guidelines from these organizations (WHO, 2022; ILO, 2024) state that programs must adopt a comprehensive approach: structural (environmental conditions), relational (workplace interactions), and individual (personal capacities). This tripartite approach allows for effectively addressing the causes of psychosocial distress and promoting healthy work environments.

In accordance with this framework, the “I take care of myself, We take care of each other” intervention sits at the intersection of socio-educational approaches and occupational health, linking awareness-building processes, skill development, and the strengthening of the workplace social fabric as key elements to transform the psychosocial environment. The general objective was to implement and evaluate a socio-educational intervention with a preventive-promotional approach to strengthen psychosocial well-being in the work environment through capacity building in self-care, social support, and co-responsibility in workplace mental health.

Methodology

This study adopted a mixed-methods approach (quantitative-qualitative) with an interventionist and descriptive scope, aimed at evaluating the effects of a socio-educational strategy on the psychosocial well-being of public sector administrative personnel. The intervention design was based on the principles of occupational health proposed by the World Health Organization (WHO) and the International Labour Organization (ILO).

The target population comprised administrative workers from a public institution in the state of Jalisco, Mexico. A non-probability convenience sampling method was used, selecting 20 participants who expressed voluntary willingness and provided informed consent to participate in the intervention. The group exhibited heterogeneity in terms of age, job tenure, and performed functions, enriching the analysis of results. Data confidentiality and adherence to ethical participation principles were strictly ensured.

The intervention was structured into a socio-educational workshop titled “I take care of myself, We take care of each other,” consisting of 10 in-person sessions conducted weekly over a three-month period. Each session lasted approximately 90 minutes and was designed with a progressive, integrative logic, combining theoretical content with experiential dynamics, collective reflection exercises, and closing activities.

The sessions addressed the following thematic axes: Introduction to self-care and emotional health, emotional recognition and management, empathetic communication and conflict resolution, identification of workplace psychosocial factors, construction of support networks and co-responsibility, strengthening the sense of belonging, strategies for coping with work stress, healthy organizational culture, participatory evaluation of the work environment, and closing with a commitment to collective care.

The facilitators were professionals in occupational health, medicine, psychology, and social work, trained in participatory methodologies. A structured guide was used for each session, specifying concrete objectives, teaching techniques, and supporting materials.

The study worked with a group of 20 public sector administrative workers selected through convenience sampling. The intervention consisted of ten in-person socio-educational sessions designed around health education models and occupational health principles focusing on psychosocial aspects.

Two instruments were applied:

  • FPST (adapted from Aranda-Beltrán & Pando-Moreno, 2014) to evaluate pre- and post-intervention psychosocial factors. This instrument assesses aspects such as physical environment conditions, job demands, workload, task content, social interaction, work role, development opportunities, and perception of compensation.

  • A semi-structured group interview (focus group) conducted upon completion of the intervention to explore participants' subjective experiences regarding perceived changes in mental health, work climate, and collective co-responsibility. Sessions were recorded, transcribed, and analyzed with participant consent.

Quantitative analysis was performed using descriptive statistics and comparative mean analysis, utilizing frequency tables to illustrate changes in psychosocial risk levels between pre- and post-intervention. Data were processed using Excel software and reviewed alongside facilitators to ensure consistency.

Qualitative analysis was carried out through thematic coding, identifying categories from focus group narratives across three stages: pre-analysis, coding, and interpretation. This approach enabled data triangulation to provide a broader understanding of the intervention's impact.

Results

The socio-educational intervention “I take care of myself, We take care of each other” demonstrated positive effects on the perception of workplace psychosocial factors among participating administrative personnel. Developed across ten in-person sessions, the intervention integrated content focused on self-care, strengthening social support, emotional management, and building healthier organizational environments.

Quantitative Results

Through the Psychosocial Risk Factors in the Workplace Questionnaire (adapted from Aranda-Beltrán & Pando-Moreno, 2014), significant variations were identified between pre- and post-intervention measurements. Prior to the intervention, the dimensions of workplace conditions and job demands were categorized as high risk in 83.3% of cases. Subsequently, these decreased to 38.8% and 50%, respectively, indicating a substantial improvement in the perception of the physical environment and job-related demands.

In the workload dimension, a reduction in the high-risk level from 16.6% to 5.5% was observed, suggesting a positive impact on the perception of work volume and pace. Meanwhile, the work role and career development dimension decreased from 27.7% to 11.1% in high-risk evaluation, which may be linked to greater role clarity, recognition, and sense of purpose experienced during the intervention.

A complete elimination of high-risk evaluations was also reported in the dimensions of task content/characteristics and social interaction/organizational aspects. This outcome can be attributed to the reflective work conducted on organizational culture, empathy, and effective communication during sessions.

The only dimension showing no variation was performance compensation, which remained at 66.6% medium risk. This highlights a structural issue exceeding the scope of the socio-educational program, related to institutional salary policies.

Qualitative Results

The thematic analysis of the post-intervention focus group identified emerging categories that complement and enrich the quantitative data:

  • Emotional awareness. Participants reported greater recognition of their emotions, stress reactions, and the importance of healthy coping mechanisms.

  • Peer social support. Workplace bonds and the perception of mutual backing were strengthened, fostering a more cohesive climate.

  • Sense of belonging and collective agency. An appropriation of the workplace as a shared space emerged, where well-being is viewed as a collective responsibility rather than an individual burden.

  • Transformation of organizational culture. Participants highlighted an increased willingness for active listening, collaborative conflict resolution, and emotional openness within their teams.

Experiential and participatory sessions were particularly valued for their impact on self-perception and the resignification of work relationships.

Comprehensive Analysis

Triangulating quantitative and qualitative results confirms that the intervention positively impacted critical dimensions of the work environment. Improvements in perceived working conditions, stress reduction, and activation of support networks contributed to fostering a more human, reflective, and co-responsible organizational climate.

However, findings also reveal the limitations of these strategies when unbacked by structural actions. The lack of progress in the compensation dimension underscores that psychoeducational interventions must be integrated into institutional policies addressing salary fairness, recognition, and working condition enhancements.

In summary, the results validate the relevance of applying participatory models focused on mental health and emotional well-being as part of a comprehensive occupational health policy.

Discussion

The socio-educational intervention “I take care of myself, We take care of each other” demonstrated significant improvements in the perception of psychosocial factors among administrative personnel. Reductions across various risk dimensions suggest that implemented strategies successfully mitigated psychosocial risks and reinforced psycho-emotional well-being at work. Decreased risk levels in physical environment conditions, demands, workload, and work role, alongside improvements in social interaction and task content, reflect substantial transformations in daily work experience.

These findings align with recommendations from the World Health Organization (WHO) and the International Labour Organization (ILO), which emphasize integrating mental health into occupational safety and health policies. WHO (2024) calls for organizational interventions aimed at improving working conditions and environments, as well as manager and worker training on mental health to recognize and manage job stressors. Similarly, the International Labour Organization (ILO, 2024) stresses the importance of safe, healthy, and emotionally sustainable environments, underscoring the need for structural, relational, and individual approaches (WHO, 2022; ILO, 2022).

Furthermore, findings support Karasek's (1979) Demand-Control model, which posits that high job demands coupled with low autonomy or control significantly increase job stress, impacting physical and mental health. From this perspective, interventions promoting self-care, emotional management, and social support help expand perceived control margins and mitigate organizational demands' impact on well-being.

Program outcomes also mirror scientific evidence supporting positive impacts of socio-educational strategies on mitigating occupational psychosocial risks. Significant improvements were observed in key dimensions such as workplace conditions, job demands, social interaction, and task content, reflecting favorable changes in the perceived psychosocial climate.

Numerous studies document positive effects of health education interventions and psychosocial skill building in workplace settings. Montano et al. (2014), in a systematic review, concluded that organizational-level interventions—especially those strengthening social support and stress management—yield sustained improvements in worker mental health and well-being.

In public administrative contexts with rigid structures and limited scope for structural change, participatory interventions hold heightened relevance. Leka and Jain (2010) note that participatory intervention programs tend to achieve greater worker ownership, resulting in improved psychosocial indicators and longer-lasting positive effects.

Qualitative analysis reinforces this premise: participants reported increased mental health awareness, enhanced interpersonal communication, and a renewed sense of collective co-responsibility—elements reflecting a healthier, more collaborative organizational climate. These results demonstrate the value of building psychosocial skills not merely as individual self-care, but as a structural component of collective well-being.

Despite observed progress, a critical dimension that showed no improvement was perceived performance compensation. This finding aligns with literature indicating structural factors, such as pay policies, require organizational-level interventions and cannot be altered solely through individual or group empowerment strategies (Bakker & Demerouti, 2017).

In conclusion, the “I take care of myself, We take care of each other” intervention offers empirical evidence of the value of participatory socio-educational strategies in enhancing workplace psychosocial well-being. However, its reach must be complemented by deeper organizational transformations addressing structural sources of discontent, such as compensation inequity or chronic task overload.

Limitations and Recommendations

Although this research provides relevant evidence regarding the effectiveness of a socio-educational intervention to improve psychosocial well-being in administrative work environments, several limitations conditioning the scope and generalizability of results must be considered, alongside recommendations derived from these constraints.

One limitation is the small sample size (n=20) obtained via non-probability convenience sampling, which restricts finding representativeness. Future research should involve larger, more diverse samples spanning multiple hierarchical levels and functional departments to enrich comparative analysis and support result extrapolation.

Additionally, the absence of a control group prevents establishing firm causal relationships between the intervention and observed changes. Quasi-experimental designs incorporating comparison groups and multi-point longitudinal evaluations are recommended for future studies to assess effect sustainability over time.

Overall, workplace health promotion strategies should be approached from a systemic perspective, combining training, participatory, and pedagogical actions with structural decisions that ensure fair, equitable, and sustainable working conditions.

Conclusions

The socio-educational intervention “I take care of myself, We take care of each other” proved to be an effective and replicable strategy for improving psychosocial factors in administrative work settings. Through a preventive-promotional approach focused on self-care, collective co-responsibility, and mental health, significant positive changes in participants' perception of their work environment were achieved.

Quantitative findings revealed considerable reductions in risk levels across critical dimensions such as workplace conditions, job demands, workload, and work role, accompanied by improvements in social interaction and task content. Qualitative evidence reinforced these outcomes, pointing to a strengthened sense of belonging, heightened emotional health awareness, and commitment to collective well-being practices.

However, performance compensation showed no variation, confirming that structural factors require specific organizational interventions beyond the scope of socio-educational programs. This highlights the necessity of pairing such interventions with institutional policies aimed at equity, recognition, and contractual improvement.

In summary, participatory socio-educational interventions can contribute significantly to strengthening workplace psychosocial well-being when embedded within a comprehensive occupational health policy. Their implementation is particularly relevant in public sector and organizational contexts where structural constraints demand creative, sustained, and collaborative solutions.
