Tax compliance and fiscal governance in line with the UN's 2030 Agenda

El cumplimiento tributario y la gobernanza fiscal acorde a la agenda 2030 de la ONU

Abstract.

This research focuses on the review of the Constitutional duty of the Mexican citizen or “duty to be” of a tax nature as well as the duty of the Mexican State to govern or “governance” in a positive way, in relation to compliance with the 2030 Agenda. The research was carried out using the qualitative research method, exegetical method and analytical method, based on the documentary research technique. As a result of the analysis of doctrines and legal texts, it is concluded that it is essential that the public policy or proposed law consider the obligation or duty that the law is intended to cause of those governed in tax matters; as well as, the government exercises good “governance” that the population expects from its public actions, this work providing a matrix of constitutional compliance and Agenda 2030 for public policies and tax laws in Mexico.

Keywords:

Governance, Duty, fiscal, Agenda 2030.

Introduction

Today, promoting the rule of law requires promoting just societies, with effective, transparent institutions, access to public information, and the application of non-discriminatory laws and policies in favor of sustainable development, as well as the adoption of policies — especially fiscal ones —, reducing corruption, and achieving positive governance, in accordance with meeting Goals ten and sixteen of the Agenda for Sustainable Development, dated September 2015, of the United Nations Sustainable Development Summit (United Nations, n.d.). It is a plan with worldwide actions for people, actions for the planet, and aimed at prosperity, whose focal point must be to achieve sustainable social and economic progress in the world and strengthen peace (Gobierno de México, 2017).

Politicians and legislators need to make policy and law proposals based on the legal grounding of what each party is meant to do. On one hand, the governed must abide by the law, which for this research concerns tax matters; and on the other, those who govern must exercise good governance in accordance with national laws and institutions.

The problem lies in the fact that, in a society, a large number of laws are not aligned with the fundamental rights of individuals and legal entities established in the Constitución Política de los Estados Unidos Mexicanos (Political Constitution of the United Mexican States). This led the Comisión Nacional de Derechos Humanos (National Human Rights Commission, CNDH) — an autonomous public body in Mexico whose mission is to defend, promote, study, and disseminate human rights enshrined in the Constitución Política de los Estados Unidos Mexicanos, laws, and international treaties (Comisión Nacional de los Derechos Humanos [CNDH], 2023) — to record, in its December 2019 activity report, 500 regulations the institution considered unconstitutional (CNDH, 2022).

This study seeks to present both theoretical and legal arguments to determine the duty of the governed — in this specific case, the duty of the taxpayer in Mexico, or the "tax duty to be" — and, on the other hand, the "positive governance" of the State in question.

Based on the theoretical and legal review, it is proposed to follow the guidance of constitutional norms and the specific goals of the UN's 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development for taxpayers' fulfillment of tax duties and for those who govern to achieve positive governance.

Methodology

For this research, the qualitative or non-traditional method was used (Bernal, 2010), through texts for the review of theories and legal provisions on obligations and governance in general and fiscal terms, in order to describe the social phenomenon within the situation that is the object of this study. The exegetical method was used, which takes into account the text of the law and, from it, formulates reasoning based on the principles of identity, of non-contradiction, and of "there is no third option" (Vallet, 2001). The analytical method was also used to break the phenomenon down into its basic elements, under the documentary research technique.

The Mexican citizen's constitutional "duty to be"

For a society to function harmoniously, there must be rules for healthy coexistence and collective development among people; these rules are social and legal, and in both, each party within a state (government and governed) is expected to fulfill a certain duty or way of conducting themselves collectively; the positive actions expected of people in specific situations are the "duty to be," that is, the conduct a government expects from those it governs, and vice versa. If, when drafting a public policy or proposed law, due importance is not given to the "duty to be" or expected conduct of those to whom the policy or rule is addressed, the expected results will not be achieved, and the policy may even become unlawful.

It is common to hear, in political and governmental circles, expressions about how certain situations "should be," or how the actions of certain individuals or legal entities "should be," such as how taxpayers' actions "should be," and how authorities' actions "should be."

From this perspective, the "duty to be" is viewed by many schools of thought from both a moral standpoint and a normative standpoint; for this research, the theoretical approach of legal science will be adopted, since it is the one that bears on the governed party's legal sphere by force of law, and can go so far as to impose administrative sanctions (warning or fine) or legal sanctions (fine or corporal penalty) for conduct contrary to the "duty to be."

Thus, Ordoñez (2012) defines the "duty to be" as:

(…) what must be, acting within a given society, in a given time and place, always complying with the rules established for coexistence. It is the realm of the 'perfect,' to which every person must aspire despite their limitations as a human being, fully performing their duties and respecting the rights of others. (p. 3)

From the above, it is worth highlighting the conception of the "duty to be" as fulfillment of the duty or obligation expected, as the conduct or action exercised by the person (the governed) within a rule of law, organized and constituted to fulfill collective, economic, social, and legal ends. That is, perfect conduct and action, free of vices or acts subject to administrative sanction.

In a society under a rule of law, whose powers are set out in norms of a legal nature (De Pina & De Pina, 2008), this can be taken as the relationship that exists between a condition and its respective consequence, with the latter being called the "duty to be," which can be considered a logical imputation within a legal system (Kelsen, 2007). In turn, the duty to be can be considered as just law, ideal law, and as natural law (Preciado, 2011).

And at the other extreme, what is currently considered, globally, as the "duty to be" for those who govern relates to the governance of a State, since governance is synonymous with government, a framework for public management, or a set of criteria political systems must satisfy in order to achieve the objectives set (Montoya-Domínguez and Rojas-Robles, 2016) — in short, what amounts to a political "duty to be."

Beforehand, in order to meet Goals ten and sixteen of the United Nations Agenda for Sustainable Development, the politician or legislator must carry out a prior review of the proposed fiscal law to ensure it complies, hierarchically, with Mexican legal provisions, and contrast it with the goals of the 2030 Agenda that can be met in tax and justice matters. This is in order not to fall into unlawful regulations of the kind detected by the CNDH, which reported five hundred such regulations in 2019 (Comisión Nacional de Derechos Humanos, 2020).

In fiscal matters, the duty of tax compliance, or "tax duty to be," for Mexicans is set out in Article 31, section IV of the CPEUM (Constitución Política de los Estados Unidos Mexicanos), which reads: "To contribute to public spending, whether of the Federation, the States, Mexico City, and the Municipality in which they reside, in the proportional and equitable manner provided by law." That is, the duty of the Mexican taxpayer is to contribute to public spending based on their tax capacity, without excuse, and on an equal legal footing.

The aforementioned constitutional provision encompasses the following four constitutional tax principles:

  • Principle of legality: Consists in the fact that tax authorities may only do what the law empowers them to do (Carrasco, 2017).

  • Principle of proportionality: Concerns each taxpayer's contribution to defraying public expenses in relation to their economic capacity.

  • Principle of equity: Means that taxpayers subject to the same tax must be treated equally under the tax law.

  • Principle of allocation to public spending: Allocating revenue obtained through taxes to cover public expenses.

Governance

The word "Governance" comes etymologically from the Latin word "gubernare," meaning to steer a ship. In the 15th century, Charles of Orléans was the first to use the French word "gouvernance" — "gobernanza" in Spanish — which describes the art of governing (Launay, 2005). "Governance is a word that has been recurrently used since the second half of the 1990s, a relatively modern usage" (Rojas, 2010, p. 60).

Regarding the definition of "governance," there is no standardized definition of the term, since each actor involved defines it according to their own objectives and practices (Launay, 2005). Different disciplines within the social sciences give it different definitions, not all of which are associated with legal matters. In a general sense, governance is defined as: "The art or manner of governing that sets as its objective the achievement of lasting economic, social, and institutional development, promoting a healthy balance between the State, civil society, and the market economy" (Real Academia Española, 2023, para. 1), or simply the act of governing. This implies following guidelines and policies on how to govern in order to fulfill public objectives on behalf of a public entity.

The Organización para la Cooperación y el Desarrollo Económico (Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development, OECD) defines governance as the: "Exercise of political, economic, and administrative authority" (Organización para la Cooperación y el Desarrollo Económico, 2013, p. 308), and highlights the political, economic, and administrative characteristics of the exercise of authority, with authority understood as the powers or functions of individuals or bodies that take part in public power (Instituto de Investigaciones Jurídicas de la UNAM, 2016). This definition does not fully address the social or collective dimension and focuses on the exercise of authority, also known as "the power to exercise a public function" (De Pina & De Pina, 2008, p. 117).

For Fukuyama (2013), "governance" is: "the capacity of a government to make and enforce rules, and to deliver services, regardless of whether that government is democratic or not" (p. 3). His definition focuses on the public entity "government" — understood as the bodies through which the State fulfills its purposes (De Pina & De Pina, 2008) — carrying out administrative activities only, regardless of the types of State and government forms.

At the other extreme, Zurbriggen (2011) notes that governance is a process of validating public policies through the direct collaboration of ordinary citizens, and is characterized by a network of institutions and individuals that collaborate under an agreement of mutual trust to envision sustainability projects. And the primary objective of governance, according to López-Vallejo (2017), is to supply public goods and solve common problems.

The contemporary notion of governance is grounded in the close relationship between government and society, around solutions to public-space problems and decision-making and deliberative processes concerning common interests (Ordóñez-Sedeño & De Paz-González, 2017).

For his part, Rojas (2010) adds regarding governance that: "(…) it expresses the way of governing, the — non-hierarchical — form of leadership that makes it possible to achieve objectives and goals through consensus, coordination, the articulation of policies, norms, and procedures, as well as effective accountability and transparency" (p. 65). Noteworthy in this definition are accountability and transparency, relatively new concepts as far as elements of governance are concerned.

The contemporary notion of governance is built on a robust relationship between government and society, since it brings government together with society around solutions to public-space problems and, above all, around the decision-making and deliberative processes concerning common interests, which underscores the importance of governing usefully and effectively; for this reason, we recommend that, in designing public policy, it be taken into account that governance is the reorganization of society according to criteria of political, economic, and legal rationality around shared values and anthropocentric ends.

As the main intergovernmental body of the United Nations (UN) system, the Human Rights Council has defined the essential attributes of good governance, which are the following: "Transparency, Responsibility, Accountability, Participation, Responsiveness (to the needs of the population)" (Consejo de Derechos Humanos, 2024, para. 4).

For good governance of the state, it is essential that it exercise transparency in its actions as a legal entity holding public powers, so that the governed are aware of its conduct and its level of compliance with obligations and responsibilities; as well as accountability of the state regarding public resources, and awareness of the degree of social participation and the responsiveness of authorities to the problems and needs of the governed.

For governance to be considered positive, the following requirements must be met: "Avoiding tyranny, Establishing essential rights, Guaranteeing general freedom, and Fostering self-determination and contributing to human development" (Ordóñez-Sedeño and De Paz-González, 2017).

Global fiscal governance

Regarding global governance, in the world economy, in addition to the presence of companies and States, there are other global governance structures that result from the interaction and relationship between global and local governance (transnational interactive networks); there are also other specific regulatory systems that are not considered here (De Paz, 2008).

Within the community of nations, governance is good and democratic to the extent that a country's institutions, legal processes, and electoral processes are transparent, and must be free of acts of corruption, with strong accountability toward the people. This is in order to create an optimal environment for sustainable development and the rule of law. Good governance promotes: "…equity, participation, pluralism, transparency, accountability, and the rule of law, so that it is effective, efficient, and lasting" (United Nations, 2023), and identifies corruption, poverty, and violence as the greatest threats to good governance (United Nations, 2023).

Regarding Latin America, academic debate on governance has been rather scarce, and the dominant notion has been spread by cooperation donors (Zurbriggen, 2011). Nonetheless, Latin American municipal governments are the level of government that must respond to the population's substantial demands (Victory, 2019), which entails local governance, from the municipalities, to respond to community needs.

Also in recent decades, the most notable transformations Latin American countries have undergone occurred in decentralization processes to improve local public services in terms of efficiency, effectiveness, and adherence to community preferences; and the transfer of government activities to subnational levels or territorial divisions. Notable among these is the development of new ways of bringing government decisions closer to the governed, in order to encourage accountability on the part of the public entity, strengthen democratic institutions, as well as increase incentives for local economic development, and increase citizens' control over public decisions. (Zurbriggen, 2011, pp. 49-50).

Regarding tax matters, in Latin America progress has been made in accountability; tracking of funds takes place within the framework of the Alianza para el Gobierno Abierto (Open Government Partnership, AGA), which was created in 2011 and is made up of 70 countries, and sets out to: (…) increase the availability of information on government activities; support citizen participation; apply the highest standards of professional integrity; and increase access to new technologies for openness and accountability (Alianza para el Gobierno Abierto, 2011).

In recent years, governance with a fiscal focus has gained importance, both locally and globally. One example is the guidelines issued by the European Commission (EU) in 2012, which agreed on an analysis of the criteria for good global governance, with a fiscal focus, stating the following: "Member States were recommended to apply the three criteria of transparency, exchange of information, and fair tax competition when assessing third countries' tax regimes and, where necessary, to apply common 'countermeasures'" (Comisión Europea, 2016, p. 4).

In the case of Mexico, it is necessary to subject public policies and legislative initiatives, in tax matters, first to constitutional review based on Article 31, section IV of the CPEUM. Subsequently, the policies or initiatives should be contrasted against the United Nations' 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development. Among the goals of the 2030 Agenda applicable to tax matters is Goal ten, "Reduced Inequalities," which contemplates ensuring equal opportunity and reducing inequality of outcome; the adoption of policies, especially fiscal policies, to achieve greater equality; and improving the regulation and monitoring of global financial institutions and markets and strengthening the implementation of such regulations.

Also applicable is Goal sixteen, "Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions," which includes, among other things, promoting the rule of law at the national and international levels, reducing corruption and bribery; building effective, transparent, and accountable institutions; the participation of developing countries in global governance institutions; public access to information and protecting fundamental freedoms; and promoting and enforcing non-discriminatory laws and policies in favor of sustainable development (Organización de las Naciones Unidas, 2015).

The above is a proposed set of specific goals to be contrasted from the 2030 Agenda that apply specifically to tax matters in Mexico, without prejudice to legislative initiatives being contrasted against the full set of the UN's 2030 Agenda goals.

Conclusions

Being able to subject public policies or legislative initiatives on fiscal matters to prior constitutional review, and subsequently to the proposed goals of the UN's 2030 Agenda, will allow Mexico to reduce the number of regulations deemed unconstitutional and to fulfill the commitments made to the United Nations regarding the reduction of inequality and the promotion of just, peaceful, and inclusive societies, enabling citizens to fulfill their tax duty, or "tax duty to be."

And with respect to governance, it is necessary for the authority to submit to constitutional review of its public powers, to UN guidelines regarding good governance, and to compliance with the 2030 Agenda's goals on sustainable development.

Proposal

Based on the foregoing, it is proposed that, prior to the publication or application of public policy or a fiscal regulation, it be submitted to a 2030 Agenda compliance matrix, shown in Figure 1.

Figure 1.

Compliance matrix for public policy or fiscal regulation in Mexico in accordance with the 2030 Agenda

1 2 3
A Public policy or proposed fiscal regulation Fully complies with the principles of Article 31, section IV of the CPEUM Complies with at least one of Goals ten and sixteen of the 2030 Agenda
B Draft public policy or fiscal regulation proposal

Describe compliance with the following principles:

Principle of legality,

Principle of proportionality and equity

Allocation to public spending.

 List the goals the public policy or fiscal regulation proposal complies with

Note. Own elaboration based on Article 31, section IV of the CPEUM, and Goals ten and sixteen of the 2030 Agenda

The matrix cells will be completed as follows:

1A – This cell will contain the public policy or proposed fiscal regulation.

2A – This cell will be titled "Fully complies with the principles of Article 31, section IV of the CPEUM."

3A – This cell will be titled "Complies with at least one of the following 2030 Agenda goals: 10.3, 10.4, 10.5, 16.3, 16.5, 16.6, 16.8, 16.10, and 16.b."

1B – This cell will contain the fiscal public policy or proposed fiscal law to be submitted for review of constitutionality and compliance with the 2030 Agenda's goals.

2B – This cell will contain the principles in question, namely, the Principle of legality, Principle of proportionality and equity, and Allocation to public spending.

3B – This cell will contain the 2030 Agenda goals that the proposed public policy or fiscal regulation complies with.

The proposed matrix would provide certainty regarding the legality of proposed tax laws and their level of compliance with the 2030 Agenda.
