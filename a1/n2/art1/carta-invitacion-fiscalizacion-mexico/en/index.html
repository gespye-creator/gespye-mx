Abstract.

Mexico's low tax collection has led the tax authority to constantly seek tools to increase public revenue; in this context, the letter of invitation is a means that invites the taxpayer to comply with their obligations without the authority formally exercising its powers of verification. This article aims to contrast the implementation of the letter of invitation against other formal oversight methods. The research was theoretical with a qualitative approach, using the comparative and deductive methods under the documentary research technique. The results show that the letter of invitation is not limited to the physical receipt of a letter inviting the taxpayer to comply with their obligations, but also includes invitations by email, the tax mailbox, messaging, or requests. It concludes with the recommendation that the taxpayer validate the letter of invitation and comply with what is required in order to avoid the initiation of verification powers.

Keywords:

Letter of invitation, oversight, taxpayer, verification powers

Introduction

Mexico's low tax collection has led the tax authority to constantly seek tools to increase public revenue; the letter of invitation falls within this context, since, although it is not part of a formal oversight process, it does request information and clarifications regarding a taxpayer's situation which, if not provided, can lead the authority to initiate its powers of verification.

The letter of invitation as an informal method of oversight in Mexico

Today, the growing use of this instrument raises many questions among taxpayers; in this sense, the relevance of this study lies in showing the possible alternatives available to taxpayers for responding to this means of invitation from the authority.

The research objective was to contrast the implementation of the letter of invitation against other formal oversight methods; to this end, the concept of the letter of invitation is addressed, its characteristics are analyzed and contrasted with other formal oversight methods, and the paper concludes by presenting the possible advantages or consequences of not responding to the tax authority's request.

Methodology

This research is theoretical, following Villabella (2015), and was developed from the abstraction of the letter of invitation. The approach, meanwhile, was qualitative, using the comparative and deductive method (López, 2021), which focused on exploring and analyzing various aspects regarding the authority's implementation of the letter of invitation and taxpayers' receipt of it, in contrast with formal oversight methods.

The research was territorially delimited to the regulations applicable at the federal level to oversight and compliance processes for taxpayers in Mexico, and temporally, various provisions were analyzed for fiscal year 2023, using the documentary research technique, which involved reviewing laws, tax rulings, court precedent, journals, research articles, authority reports, among others.

Origins of the letter of invitation

First, it should be noted that the letter of invitation does not have an explicit, detailed definition in tax regulations; nonetheless, in practice it is understood as a personalized document issued by the tax authority through the Servicio de Administración Tributaria (Tax Administration Service) to invite the taxpayer to regularize their tax situation, to clarify inconsistencies found in their tax information, without the authority initiating oversight or collection proceedings against the taxpayer (Cofide, 2023).

The authority's invitation message can be served personally at the taxpayer's tax domicile or through various electronic means such as the tax mailbox, email, or calls (COEL Abogados & COEF Contadores, 2019), or even SMS messages or telemessages (Servicio de Administración Tributaria [SAT], 2023), which simplifies communication with taxpayers, making it less costly to send compared to directly initiating powers of verification (Dorantes and Hernández, 2023); this implies a reduction in the time the authority devotes to reviews and in the involvement of its audit team.

This invitation seeks to encourage taxpayers to voluntarily comply with their tax obligations, thereby avoiding the initiation of powers of verification (Lexstone, 2020), as well as increasing tax collection more quickly and effectively (Piña, 2022).

Although invitations to taxpayers were introduced into tax legislation in 2004 through the addition of section III of Article 33 of the Código Fiscal de la Federación (Federal Fiscal Code) ([CFF], 1981), on the grounds of improving assistance to taxpayers in complying with their obligations, it was not until 2011 that the authority began mass mailing letters of invitation to more than 600,000 individuals who had received cash bank deposits and had not taken them into account for payment of Impuesto Sobre la Renta (Income Tax, ISR), by cross-checking data based on information obtained thanks to the then-existing Impuesto Sobre Depósitos en Efectivo (Cash Deposit Tax, IDE) (Ponce and Ponce, 2012).

According to Martínez (2012), through this action the authority sought to broaden the taxpayer base as well as regularize individuals' tax situations; to this end, it issued 4 types of invitations:

a) People registered in the Registro Federal de Contribuyentes (Federal Taxpayer Registry, RFC) who had not filed their tax return despite being required to do so.

b) People who were not required to file a tax return but who had received cash deposits.

c) People whose activities were suspended and who should not have received income, since they were not carrying out any activity.

d) Individuals who had received income but were not registered in the RFC.

At this first stage, the subjects to whom the requests were addressed were entirely individuals; although, over time, legal entities were also included. In addition to the above, due to its great effectiveness and efficiency in tax collection (Universidad Nacional Autónoma de México, 2023), it was later included in section IV of Article 33 of the CFF (1981) to expand the authority's powers, justifying the sending of payment proposals or pre-filled returns, as well as communication with the taxpayer to urge them to comply with their obligations and to communicate errors or atypical situations, with the aim of encouraging taxpayer compliance and correction without these being considered formal oversight acts.

Powers of verification and the letter of invitation

Margáin (2016) explains that the powers and authority of the tax authority are aimed at verifying whether the taxpayer is fully complying with their obligations. Along the same lines, Otondo and Otondo (2020) note that oversight is grounded in the constitutional obligation to contribute to public spending and that, in keeping with this provision, the Ley Orgánica de la Administración Pública Federal (Organic Law of the Federal Public Administration) mandates the Secretaría de Hacienda y Crédito Público (Ministry of Finance and Public Credit) to carry out the collection of public revenue.

According to the Suprema Corte de Justicia de la Nación (Supreme Court of Justice of the Nation) in binding precedent Jurisprudencia 2a./J. 22/2020, the tax authority has management powers involving assistance, control, or oversight of taxpayers and their tax contributions, as well as powers of verification involving inspection, verification, assessment, or settlement of taxes, with both types of powers having different legal grounds.

Thus, when a taxpayer evades payment, or in order to prevent the taxpayer from committing tax fraud, the authority may exercise its powers of verification, which include desk audits, on-site visits, mass audits, electronic reviews, among others, in which it requires accounting records, documents, or reports either from the taxpayer or from other parties with tax liability (Otondo and Otondo, 2020).

It should be noted that, pursuant to Article 2, section XII of the Ley Federal de los Derechos del Contribuyente (Federal Law of Taxpayer Rights) (2005), if the authority initiates powers of verification, the taxpayer has the right to be informed of this fact, as well as of their rights and obligations in the exercise of these powers.

Likewise, pursuant to Article 33 of the CFF (1981), when the taxpayer receives a letter of invitation, the authority does not initiate its powers of verification; consequently, it is not necessary for the authority to inform the taxpayer of their rights, since, as the Colegio de Contadores Públicos de México (2023) maintains, the invitation request does not establish a "legal bond that obligates or conditions compliance; it is merely an exhortation"; therefore, its scope is limited to being merely an invitation, with no obligation to submit a clarification or response.

Authority oversight and means of contact

According to official figures, the recording of taxpayer oversight actions has been tracked since 2012 and is divided into two programs: the first regarding compliance oversight, which includes blocking digital seal certificates (CDS), the tax mailbox, letters, email, telemessages, SMS messages, requests, and requests from state governments; and the deep oversight program, which before 2022 was called "revenue-collection declines" ("caídas recaudatorias") and included the tax mailbox, letters, email, calls, SMS messages, and deep oversight (SAT, 2023).

Within the compliance oversight program, letters showed a steady increase from 2012 to 2014, reaching their peak in 2015 with a total of 17,599,086, then declining in subsequent years; this method of contact even stopped being recorded starting in 2020, which is related to the emergence and growth of other technological means of contact, such as the tax mailbox in 2016, as well as email, as shown in Table 1.

It can be observed that there are more means of contact in the compliance oversight program than in the deep oversight program, since state governments even take part in compliance oversight. Likewise, traditional letters began to disappear in 2019 in the deep oversight program, and disappeared in 2020 in the compliance oversight program.

Meanwhile, while SMS messages and telemessages are an important part of the compliance program, they have no relevance in the deep oversight program; the opposite is true of the tax mailbox, which began to be used in 2016 in the compliance oversight program and was implemented starting in 2019 in the deep oversight program, growing considerably and steadily from 2016 to 2022.

Receiving and responding to the letter of invitation

Upon receiving a communication from the SAT, one must identify whether it is in fact an invitation, which can be identified within the document itself by the phrase "you are invited to clarify your tax situation" (Universidad Nacional Autónoma de México, 2023); otherwise, it may simply be a notice.

Along with the above, it is advisable to validate whether the letter of invitation is authentic, which can be done directly on the SAT platform by verifying the letter of invitation's control number, the email address, and the phone number of the person who made the clarification request on behalf of the SAT; this security measure prevents taxpayers from falling victim to possible scams or extortion attempts (Colegio de Contadores Públicos de México, 2023), since it prevents them from sharing their confidential information with unauthorized third parties.

Among the most common reasons for receiving a letter of invitation are the following, which are illustrative rather than exhaustive (Alvarado, 2022; Cofide, 2023; Monroy and Mendoza, 2023; Garza and Ávila, 2024):

• Clarification of possible discrepancies between the tax returns filed by the taxpayer and the Comprobantes Fiscales Digitales por Internet (Digital Tax Receipts, CFDI)

• Compliance with outstanding tax obligations, such as filing returns or paying taxes.

• Providing additional information about transactions or tax activities carried out, or discrepancies identified in the tax information.

• Verification of the information provided in tax returns.

• Correction of errors or omissions in filed tax returns.

• Establishing dealings with Empresas que Facturan Operaciones Simuladas (Companies that Invoice Simulated Transactions, EFOS).

Additionally, it is worth mentioning that the tax authority's main tool for detecting its errors, inconsistencies, and discrepancies in income, withholdings, and taxes is the so-called CFDI (Comprobante Fiscal Digital por Internet) (Universidad Nacional Autónoma de México, 2023), "which is an electronic invoice describing a good or service acquired, the transaction date, the cost, and the taxes corresponding to payment of that transaction" (Gobierno de México, 2023), and is used by taxpayers to substantiate the income and deductions they report.

Taxpayers often do not grasp the importance of correctly issuing these electronic documents in a timely and proper manner, since these support all business activity and serve as the basis on which the tax authority reconciles the CFDI against what is reported in tax returns (Zamora, 2023), and it is from these discrepancies that possible tax omissions are detected.

When taxpayers receive a letter of invitation, they most often wonder whether or not they should respond to it; since responding is not mandatory, they could choose not to respond, as there is no effect on either their rights or their obligations (Garza and Ávila, 2024); nonetheless, among the advantages of promptly addressing a letter of invitation is that taxpayers can prevent powers of verification from being initiated against them, which entails possible fines, as well as a greater administrative burden and time invested (Garza and Ávila, 2024).

Likewise, if the authority's request is addressed, there is the advantage that, since it is considered spontaneous and voluntary compliance with tax obligations, taxpayers who choose to bring themselves up to date with omitted or erroneous tax obligations should not be subject to a fine (Argote, 2020).

On the other hand, in practice, if a letter of invitation is disregarded, the common consequences include a negative compliance opinion, cancellation of tax seals, which would prevent the company from issuing tax receipts for its operations, and the initiation of powers of verification by the authority (Argote, 2020).

Once the letter has been received and validated, it should first be read carefully, this document issued by the SAT, in order to identify the information being requested and the deadline for responding or attending the proposed appointment; it is also essential to thoroughly review the information it contains to ensure its accuracy and veracity. Once this review is complete, the taxpayer must gather the required documentation, making sure it is precise, complete, and in proper order, as well as self-correcting where applicable, so that it supports the arguments presented to the authority.

Following from the above, it is essential to follow the proper procedure for responding to the authority, based on what is stipulated in rule 2.9.13 of the Resolución Miscelánea Fiscal 2023 (2023 Miscellaneous Tax Resolution) (Secretaría de Hacienda y Crédito Público, 2022), which mentions the possibility of making clarifications to the letter or exhortation received through a specific procedural form. Thus, taxpayers may respond to requests within a period of no more than 15 days following receipt of the letter, and once they send their response to the authority, the authority has a maximum period of 6 business days to resolve or request further information regarding that matter (Colegio de Contadores Públicos de México, 2023).

If the authority requires further information, the taxpayer will have 10 business days to gather the requested information; likewise, in the case of other letters of invitation not related to tax collection matters, clarification should be made with the corresponding authority that issued the notice (Colegio de Contadores Públicos de México, 2023).

Results

A lack of detailed regulation was observed in tax legislation regarding the process for responding to the letter of invitation, from the moment the notification is received through the sending of a response to the authority. It is observed that the only established procedure for responding is contained in a procedural form sheet ("ficha de trámite"), which can generate uncertainty among taxpayers and inequalities in their treatment.

Communication of letters of invitation has evolved, from being sent physically to being carried out through digital means such as the tax mailbox, email, or a call, with email standing out as the means through which the authority has collected the most taxes from taxpayers.

On the other hand, the taxpayer does not have the right to be informed of their rights, since this is not a formal act of exercising powers of verification, but rather an exhortation (invitation) to help the taxpayer comply with their obligations.

Discussion

In the research results, it could be observed that the letter of invitation is implemented to comply with what is established under Article 31, section IV of the Constitution regarding the contribution to public spending, and is issued in accordance with management powers, urging the taxpayer to comply.

Likewise, we also share Castro's (2013) view that the letter of invitation lacks proper grounding, in that it does not detail any clear procedure for responding to it, and it can additionally be noted that every act of the authority must be duly grounded and justified in accordance with constitutional principles.

It is evident that there is a need for further scientific research on this topic. The current literature on this subject is limited, and therefore additional studies are needed that delve deeper into aspects such as its impact on taxpayers, its effectiveness as an oversight tool, and its conformity with the principles of equity and proportionality.

This future research could shed light on important issues related to taxpayers' rights and transparency in oversight processes, which, in turn, could provide recommendations and improvements in the regulation and practice of the letter of invitation.

Conclusions

The letter of invitation as an informal method of oversight in Mexico

Although the letter of invitation has its origins in the 2004 legislative amendment, it was not until 2011 that the tax authority began using this means on a mass scale, and not until 2012 that the authority began recording the actions through which it invites taxpayers to comply with tax obligations.

It has been shown that, although this act is not considered a formal oversight act, its use has become widespread in practice and has proven effective in increasing tax collection. Nonetheless, gaps have been identified in its regulation and procedures, which can lead to uncertainty and inequalities in the treatment of taxpayers.

In this sense, the authority's invitation to the taxpayer has taken various forms since it began with letters delivered by mail, evolving from physical to electronic means to increase its reach and lower its cost, especially since fiscal year 2016.

The letter of invitation is an informal oversight method that the authority will continue to freely exercise, which is why taxpayers must be prepared and reconcile their tax information so that, when a notice of this kind arrives, the discrepancies are minimal and identifiable.

Ideally, there would be a right to a defense before the courts, where evidence and arguments could be presented in this verification process and where an external reviewing authority could impartially assess this evidence; likewise, the authority should specifically state, in its notices, how it arrived at the discrepancies stated in its requests.
