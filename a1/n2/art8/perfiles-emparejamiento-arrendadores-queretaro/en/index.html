Identifying profiles for matching landlords and tenants of properties in Querétaro

Abstract. The difficulty of matching landlords with tenants in a leasing process is due to the influence of demographic, social, economic and technological factors on the behavior of both participants and the promotional tools they use to advertise and search for a home in rent such as traditional media, real estate agents, real estate portals, social networks and other digital marketing tools. The present study seeks to understand the relevance of digital promotion media and the factors that influence the leasing process and affect the behavior of the owner and tenant, through an exploratory cross-sectional study with a quantitative approach, applied in Queretaro, Mexico. The results constitute six different profiles, three for landlords and three for tenants to know demographic characteristics, socioeconomic preferences and the environment related to housing, as well as the main technologies in promotional media. A digital marketing strategy is proposed to promote a more effective matching process.

Keywords:

house leasing; external factors; matching; digital marketing promotional strategy.

Introduction

In recent years, the Zona Metropolitana de Querétaro (Querétaro Metropolitan Area, ZMQ) has grown economically, generating a wave of immigration; from 2005 through 2020, 195,760 arrivals in the state were recorded, compared with 60,760 departures from the area (INEGI, 2020), which has generated a boom in the real estate sector in the city, with greater internal mobility between 2020 and 2021 following the COVID-19 pandemic, when 75.8% of online searches were for rental housing compared to 24.2% for housing for sale (Lamudi, 2022).

Taking into account the importance of housing leases, which refers to the signing of a legal contract in which the landlord temporarily hands over their home while the tenant makes use of it in exchange for a monthly rent payment (González, 1993), it is important to consider that the leasing process takes place through four steps: promotion, contact, formalization, and drafting of the lease agreement (Esparza, 2012). In this sense, the first of these (promotion) presents a common problem, which has been termed "matching."

Matching involves finding a suitable home for the tenant, while for the owner it involves renting their property to the right tenant, involving three factors that initially affect it: the first factor made up of characteristics that identify owners and tenants in relation to the type of housing; a second factor, related to the promotional media used by owner and tenant to advertise and search for housing; and the third factor linked to the existence of major forces affecting the dynamics of leasing (Ruheen, et al. 2019).

Thus, the purposes of this article are established as: identifying the importance of these major forces that hinder matching; locating the behavioral profiles of those involved; and proposing a digital marketing methodology to generate an efficient connection between the parties involved in the lease.

Frame of reference

For the purposes of this paper, leasing is made up of the landlord —owner—, the tenant —renter—, and the property. The latter is classified into six types: economic, popular, traditional, medium, residential, and residential plus (INEGI, 2020; Comisión Nacional de Vivienda, 2010). Properties are also characterized by having a location, whether in a condominium or a gated community (Amaya, 2007; Rodríguez, 2007); a price, an amount resulting from price comparison (Méndez, 1987; Herrera, 2022); an appraisal and the value of the property (Jaramillo and Agudelo, 2018; Herrera, 2009). The type of housing is also taken into account (CONAVI, 2010; Martínez and Ospina, 2017; Méndez, 1987; Zambrano, 2016); the amenities it offers (Amaya, 2007); and the place and surroundings where it is located (Jaramillo and Agudelo, 2018; Zambrano, 2016; Miranda, 2021; Cubillo, 2017; Di Virgilio, 2009; Suárez and Delgado, 2010; Coulomb, 1991).

As mentioned earlier, the leasing process begins with the promotion of a property (Esparza, 2012), where a match is sought between the landlord, who offers the property, and the tenant, who demands the property (Torres, 2019; Isart, 2018; Stetelman, 2012). This promotion is carried out through the use of different media, such as real estate portals, social networks, traditional media, and real estate agents (Stetelman, 2012). In addition, there are demographic, social, economic, and technological factors that influence consumer behavior (Stanton et al., 2007; Kotler and Armstrong, 2008; Fisher and Espejo, 2011), which externally affect the leasing of a property.

Important demographic factors with an influence on leasing have been identified, such as the formation of peripheries, migration, and urban mobility, whose effects can be seen in the leasing processes present in different cities around the world (Miranda, 2021; Cubillo, 2017; Di Virgilio, 2009; Suárez et al., 2010; Coulomb, 1991; Luginaah et al., 2010; Ji and Yang, 2008; Quiroz, 2013; Pujadas, 2009; Ramírez, 2018).

Regarding the formation of peripheries, this makes it possible to identify the location of social strata, types of housing, and areas with the most leasing activity. In city centers are located political and administrative activities; in the first and second peripheries, greater leasing activity due to greater urban infrastructure, public services, and condominium amenities aimed at middle and upper social strata; and finally, the third and fourth peripheries, whose main characteristics are that they have little infrastructure, insufficient services, and self-built housing, aimed at lower social strata (Miranda, 2021; Cubillo, 2017; Di Virgilio, 2009; Suárez et al., 2010; and Coulomb, 1991). In addition, the search for better opportunities and quality of life gives relevance to migration as a factor in affordable leasing, mainly within the first and second peripheries, in safe, well-connected neighborhoods (Luginaah et al., 2010; Ji and Yang, 2008; and Quiroz, 2013).

Thus, urban mobility involves residential movement within the same city and can occur either downward, from the center toward the first and second peripheries, or upward, from the outer peripheries toward the center, due to the proximity of jobs and urban infrastructure, means of transportation, and roadways that facilitate everyday mobility between gated middle- and upper-class residential areas, in relation to age, marital status, family life cycle, tenure, education, and socioeconomic level (Pujadas, 2009; Ramírez, 2018; Suárez et al., 2010). Thus, for example, the greatest mobility is found in a profile between 25 and 35 years old, single or separated, without children, with a high educational and socioeconomic level, and without homeownership.

In addition to demographic factors in leasing, there are also influential social factors, such as the relationship between urban infrastructure and the time it takes to lease a property, understanding infrastructure as a property's proximity to parks, stadiums, sports facilities, and roadways; and considering public services as the existence of schools, universities, transportation, shopping centers, etc. — elements that show a constant pattern, in which the closer properties are to these infrastructure and service areas, the greater their demand and the shorter the time needed to lease a property (Agudelo et al., 2017; Agudelo et al., 2015).

On the other hand, for properties to be rented quickly, in less than two months, characteristics such as the following are essential: square meters of construction, parking spaces, number of floors or levels, and rental prices. The larger the property's area, with more than one parking space, more than one level, and a reasonable price, the shorter the time needed to rent it (Agudelo et al., 2017).

Regarding economic factors, the authors Zambrano (2016), Jaramillo and Agudelo (2018), and Méndez (1987) found that the formation and increase of rental prices is closely tied to a property's profitability. The elements considered for setting a property's rental price and its increase are related to the type of housing, geographic location, urban infrastructure, public services, and the amenities the property has (Zambrano, 2016; Méndez, 1987).

If a property has enough physical space and is located in the first peripheries where infrastructure and services are more abundant, rental prices are higher and its supply and demand increase. However, this reduces its profitability, since the return on investment is recovered more slowly compared to properties that are smaller in size and located in the outer peripheries. This makes the rental price more affordable and, therefore, profitability higher (Jaramillo and Agudelo, 2018).

Finally, Stetelman (2012) and Isart (2018) identified the use of different promotional media and user behavior on digital real estate platforms as the technological factors affecting matching. On one hand, the promotional media most used by both owners and tenants to advertise and search for rental housing are: the digital real estate platform, a tool used by people who already have prior knowledge of leasing, owing to its practicality, detailed information, and photos of the property; real estate agents, hired by landlords or tenants renting a property for the first time, thanks to their experience in the real estate market; and traditional media — banners or signs — used by people familiar with these media (Stetelman, 2012; Larceneux et al. 2015).

Focusing on digital platforms, these are used mostly by owners between 45 and 55 years old, familiar with technological devices and the strategies for listing their property for lease, since they have enough time to do it themselves; they save on the commission paid to real estate agents, and view the activities related to posting listings and searching for tenants as easy tasks (Isart, 2018).

Research methodology

The main problem lies in the fact that there is no proper matching between landlord and tenant within the leasing process in the ZMQ, which can be explained along two lines. On one hand, it concerns the influence of demographic, social, economic, and technological factors that affect both the leasing process and the behavior of landlord and tenant, and on the other, it concerns the promotional media both parties use to advertise and search for a rental property.

Having identified the problem, it was determined that a digital marketing model should be generated to lay the groundwork for effective matching. To this end, a cross-sectional, exploratory study with a quantitative approach was carried out to learn about the demographic, social, economic, and technological variables, as well as the behavior of the owner and tenant, through an instrument with the characteristics of a questionnaire (Hernández et al. 2014).

The questionnaire was developed in four phases. In the first, a table was prepared for each influencing factor: demographic, social, economic, and technological, along with its variables as analyzed by the reference works. In the second, indicators within each factor were identified, which were taken into account as the items for the questionnaires. In the third, the variables that fit the owner's questionnaire, the tenant's questionnaire, or both were classified. And, lastly, the two questionnaires were created, one for landlords and another for tenants.

Considering that the main objective is to create a digital promotion strategy for matching, it was of the utmost importance to delimit the sample, which was obtained through non-probabilistic sampling, in order to thoroughly examine the demographic, social, economic, and technological factors that affect both leasing and the behavior of landlord and tenant (Hernández et al., 2014).

A total of 66 people in the ZMQ were surveyed, of whom 33 were owners and 33 were tenants. This sample size was achieved through two sampling strategies: convenience sampling and snowball sampling. For the convenience sample, 18 landlords and 33 tenants who were accessible were contacted; the rest of the owners were obtained through snowball sampling, by asking the landlords themselves whether they knew other people who could take part in the research (Hernández et al., 2014).

Results and analysis

The results obtained made it possible to: 1) determine which promotional medium has the greatest influence on leasing, along with its relevance, 2) identify the external factors that most influence leasing and the behavior of owner and tenant, 3) form six profiles, three for landlords and three for tenants, and 4) propose the basis for creating a digital promotion strategy.

On one hand, among owners, the most widely used digital platform was Facebook Marketplace, owing to its practicality, since posting is simple, and the reach its algorithms provide makes it possible to find potential tenants. Isart (2018) found that one of the reasons owners post on a digital platform is the belief that this tool is more efficient for prospecting tenants.

On the other hand, among tenants it was Vivanuncios, a platform that, according to Forbes (2014), is the most visited real estate site among Mexicans and is associated with the property's visibility, without the need to arrange a prior appointment to see it in person, and with the wide range of housing options thanks to specialized filters, since these make it possible to narrow them down according to each tenant's needs.

Nonetheless, it was observed that hiring real estate agents and using traditional media — "for rent" banners — are other promotional strategies used by tenants and landlords. Those who hire the services of a real estate agent seek to benefit from their experience in the real estate market, which ensures them a time savings they lack due to work obligations. Finally, those who use a traditional medium do so out of familiarity and habit with these tools.

Thus, the proposal for an effective matching strategy should consider bringing together "Digital Tenants," who use digital platforms and demand medium-type housing, with "Advised Owners," who offer traditional-type housing and hired the services of a real estate agent to promote their property for lease.

Following on, "Advised Tenants," who hire real estate agents to help them search for medium-type housing, can be connected with "Traditional Owners," who relied on a promotional banner, given that they do in fact provide medium-type housing for lease.

Finally, "Traditional Tenants," who rent a residential property through a promotional banner, would pair well with "Digital Owners," since the latter offer housing of the same type for lease, although they use digital platforms.

Conclusion

According to the analysis of the results, this research concludes that the external factors that most influence leasing and the behavior of landlords and tenants are demographic, social, economic, and technological factors, which made it possible to obtain six profiles identified by their different variables, in order to learn who they are, what preferences they have in relation to properties, and what promotional strategies they use. This paper found that demographic, social, and economic factors influence one another like a chain reaction, where demographic factors affect social factors and social factors affect economic factors. That is, demographic factors indicate preferences and inclinations in relation to social variables and all the desirable characteristics in each group with respect to a property, and these, in turn, affect the economic factor, the rental price, and the reasons for leasing.

In addition, it is concluded that real estate portals are the main media influencing the process of leasing a property in the ZMQ. In other words, it is the promotional medium most used by landlords to post and promote their property in order to find a tenant, and by tenants to find a property. It is expected that the profiles created and the groundwork of the proposed digital promotion strategy will help future research apply this knowledge to develop a more effective matching model between landlords and tenants, one that can change the dynamics of traditional leasing, and above all, provide a solution to this problem, which is so common in housing leasing not only in the ZMQ, but throughout Mexico and other parts of the world.
