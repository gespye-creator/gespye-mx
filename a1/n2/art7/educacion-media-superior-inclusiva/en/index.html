Abstract.

Adolescence is a crucial stage in the formation of young people, where physical, psychological and social changes converge that can lead to problems if not properly channeled. In particular, the incidence of risky behaviours such as addictions, violence or delinquency has increased in recent decades among young people from disadvantaged backgrounds. Faced with this situation, a promising line of intervention is the use of physical-sports activity programs for education in values and the promotion of healthy lifestyles. Some studies point to positive results of initiatives in this regard, such as improvements in self-esteem, reduction of antisocial behaviour or adoption of habits favourable to health. However, rigorous planning is required, considering the physical and psychological barriers of these young people, as well as a comprehensive perspective involving various social agents. The purpose of this study is to evaluate the effectiveness of a sports-educational program implemented with adolescents at risk from a disadvantaged context. Specifically, pre-post-intervention changes in variables such as self-concept, prosocial behavior, substance use, and school engagement will be analyzed. The expected results are an empirical contribution to the potential of physical-sports activity in the positive training of vulnerable young people.

Keywords:

Inclusive Higher Secondary Education, Organizational Management, Educational Innovation, Diversity

Introduction

Preparatoria 22 de Tlaquepaque is among the most recent metropolitan additions to the Sistema de Educación Media Superior (Upper Secondary Education System) of the Universidad de Guadalajara network (Red UdeG), opening its doors in the 2019 A academic term. The school is committed to meeting the upper-secondary educational demand of the Tlaquepaque geographic area, serving young students from the surrounding neighborhoods, as well as students who, for various reasons, were not admitted to other schools within the Red UdeG.

Preparatoria 22 de Tlaquepaque offers only the general high-school curriculum organized by competencies, with a strongly humanistic, student-centered approach and a constructivist orientation, whose purpose is to provide students with a general education that allows them to function in different areas of academic, cultural, and working life. The school's particular mission is the well-rounded formation of citizens who are aware of their current environment and oriented toward the common good. This school's vision is for Preparatoria 22 de Tlaquepaque to be a statewide benchmark for educational quality that transforms its community with social responsibility (SEMS, 2021).

The Preparatoria 22 campus has 7 buildings with classrooms and computer and experimental-science labs, in addition to a library and an entrepreneurship center; each classroom is equipped with 45 seats, a whiteboard, a screen, a desk and chair for the teacher, air conditioning, windows providing natural light, and lamps for artificial lighting. Its student population is made up of students from the municipalities of Tlajomulco, El Salto, Guadalajara, and the very municipality of Tlaquepaque where the school is located.

Challenges

Preparatoria 22 de Tlaquepaque serves a heterogeneous student population, coming from areas with a high risk of drug use. Specifically, it is located in the Lomas de Tlaquepaque neighborhood, classified as a priority area for addiction prevention. There are also significant differences with other schools in the metropolitan area in terms of academic performance, since the school admits applicants with the lowest middle-school averages; in general, it serves a population with few study habits and a low perceived sense of academic achievement.

Among the risk factors this population faces are: easy access to drugs around the school, negative peer influence, some students' dysfunctional family context, as well as vulnerabilities inherent to adolescence such as low self-esteem, perceived achievement, and adjustment problems; these factors have been identified by Preparatoria 22's academic and tutoring areas. It has been found that, currently, the most common problems for Preparatoria 22 de Tlaquepaque's student body could be narrowed down to the areas of health, poor nutrition, anxiety and/or depression, and addictions. For this paper, we will focus on the latter, addictions.

According to data from the Estudio Básico de la Comunidad Objetivo (Basic Study of the Target Community), carried out by the Centro de Integración Juvenil (Youth Integration Center) in 2018, in which, based on certain risk factors or conditions, Tlaquepaque's neighborhoods are sorted according to level of need for attention, Lomas de Tlaquepaque (the neighborhood where the school is located) is classified as a high-risk area for drug use requiring priority attention — a problem that is evident among some of our students and that was further aggravated by the pandemic, consistent with national figures.

The problem of illicit substance use is multifactorial; various studies highlight three factors: macrosocial factors, which relate to social structures and dynamics (socioeconomic status, legality, access to drugs); microsocial factors, which refer to direct personal interaction with family and school groups and individual attributes, such as psychological state, personal coping and adaptation tools, low perceived achievement, and negative self-esteem; other studies add lack of attachment to the school community and the existence of school-related problems as risk factors (Bruno et al., 1992). Thus, given the risk factors the student population faces, developing effective strategies for the prevention of and response to drug use is imperative; on one hand, information was available on referrals for addiction in cases where drug use had already become a matter out of control, and on the other, there were those experimenting with drug use for the first time due to easy access around the school, the influence of friends, and the vulnerability characteristics inherent to our students' stage of development. This situation shows the need to implement effective preventive strategies adapted to the characteristics and risk factors of this student population, in order to reduce the impact of addictions and promote healthy lifestyles among adolescents.

Theoretical and reference framework

There is currently a substantial increase in the number of young people who have social problems related to addictions, violence, and delinquency; upon identifying this problem, various spaces dedicated to intervening with people affected by any of these situations have been created, generating programs that make it possible to deal with and put an end to these unfavorable activities through physical activity and sport, focusing on promoting values that prevent addictive behaviors among young students and, in turn, improving their quality of life, opening up space for positive alternatives within their communities and providing them with benefits that are not only physical but also psychological, social, vocational, recreational, and personal (Jiménez and Durán, 2015).

It is in this way that it is found that one of the places where young people can develop and distance themselves from the various vices and addictions that an uncontrolled environment can offer is within school facilities, since this is where they spend a large amount of their time. That is, schools are institutions that, in some way, generate a favorable environment for explaining and fostering specific cognitive, behavioral, and healthy developmental behaviors, serving as a means of support for achieving improvements in living conditions through the creation of various physical activity and sports-practice programs (Llanos-Muñoz et al., 2023) that keep students away from social problems and drug dependence, achieving improvements in their lifestyles.

Now, Heider raises the fact that we attribute results only to personal or environmental causes, rather than to a combination of both factors. From this, we can take that when young people are exposed to both positive outcomes and the attribution of achievements to their individual traits, it is easier to determine that they will have a positive outlook, since they will operate in relation to a sense of pride and satisfaction (Lalljee, 1981). These improvements in quality of life cannot be achieved overnight, but rather can come about by encouraging young people to devote significant effort to sports activities and practices; this can be achieved through various situational factors that generate an improvement in young students' motivations, one of the main motivations structured within the sports and physical-activity environment being the pursuit of success, thereby triggering motivated behavior and serving as an incentive for avoiding failure (Monroy and Sáez, 2012). With this, we can observe that Atkinson's (1964) achievement motivation theory comes into play, which indicates that motivation to achieve a goal arises from an internal conflict between the desire to succeed and the desire to avoid failure.

Consequently, another theory that supports young people's intentions to engage in certain behaviors or activities is the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB), which indicates that such intentions depend on their personal attitudes, the social norms they perceive, and their sense of control or self-efficacy. This theoretical model has previously been recommended for creating effective public-health communication interventions. Therefore, it could be expected that a well-designed sports program, one that reinforces young people's negative attitudes toward drugs, norms rejecting use, and their self-efficacy, would reduce their intentions and behaviors related to substance use. That is, expectations regarding the preventive capacity of school sports initiatives of this kind are positive (Peña-y-Lillo, 2019). It can thus be seen that the TPB highlights the advantages of encouraging physical-sports activity through the reinforcement of motivational antecedents such as attitude, subjective norms, and perceived control during adolescence. This developmental stage is crucial for the formation of healthy habits among young people. Therefore, supporting the aforementioned motivational components can effectively promote the practice of exercise in this population, with the resulting well-being benefits that this entails. In conclusion, strengthening positive attitude, social norms, and perceived behavioral control is a promising way to establish regular physical activity among adolescents (Huéscar et al., 2014).

Method

Preparatoria 22 has 3 instruments for assessing students' situations. Within Preparatoria 22's Academic Records office, there is a form (Ficha P22) that allows an interview to be conducted each semester with newly enrolled students, containing direct questions about certain situations, such as: How many times a day do you eat? Do you have any disability? Are you or your partner currently pregnant? among others that give us an opening for detection through their answers. The referral form is a questionnaire that allows teachers and school staff to detect a situation through assignments or exercises, comments, or actions; with this, as its name indicates, they can refer the student. And the third is for those who go directly to the educational services area, whether themselves or their parents, informing us of some situation at home.

Results and discussion

As a result of the primary instrument, important material was generated for academic decisions at Preparatoria 22; for the purposes of this paper, we refer specifically to the results for sports activities, in which it was found that 62% prefer to engage in sports and recreational activities, and 4 out of every 10 high-school students practice some physical activity inside or outside of school.

The "Tu prepa en movimiento" ("Your High School on the Move") program emerged, implemented based on the hypothesis that promoting sports and other recreational activities reduces problems such as anxiety and depression, as well as addictions. Tu Prepa en Movimiento offered samples of different sports organized by the Physical Education and Sports Academy, and various sports teams were formed, which helped reduce the incidence of referrals in the Educational Guidance area and resulted in fewer students showing symptoms of depression and/or anxiety.

It is important to mention that there have been obstacles to participation in activities, such as:

Drug distribution in the area around the school.

Family situations that foster drug use

Having only one court within the campus

Schedule conflicts between students' and coaches' availability

Opposition from parents to their children's participation in the program

Despite this, there have also been obstacles, which have been navigated as much as possible but which are sometimes limiting, such as having only one court within the campus, or finding schedules that align with students' and coaches' availability, and even, though it may seem hard to believe, parents who oppose their children practicing a sport because it means they must stay at school longer. Consistent with prior evidence, the participation of 108 students in various sports disciplines (softball, baseball, basketball, soccer, volleyball, and chess) was accompanied by a reduction of between 50% and 85% in the number of referrals for addiction compared to previous periods. Additionally, the extra recreational activities (rallies, athletic competitions, choreography, etc.) fostered motivation, a sense of achievement, and school engagement among participants.

It is worth mentioning other strategies considered important for well-rounded development and for fostering early involvement in research, sustainable development, and social commitment, which have been promoted. The first is the consolidation of the Tlaqueracing team, which takes part in the F1 in Schools technological challenge, in which students work under the same principles as a real Formula 1 team; that is, they design, model, test, manufacture, and assemble miniature Formula 1 vehicles, while developing soft skills (collaborative work, oral and written communication, negotiation, among others) and STEM skills (science, technology, engineering, and mathematics) — a team that has already qualified to attend the World Finals in Singapore, to be held in September 2023.

The second is three fledgling entrepreneurship projects, which foster sustainable development, led by high-school teachers, and which around 50 students have already joined.

Conclusions

The management carried out by the school's different members — academic, administrative, and operational staff, coaches, as well as authorities together with members of the educational community such as parents, and institutions such as the Municipal Government, COMUDE, the Tlaquepaque Police, CUCEA and CUCEI, and the Physical Culture Coordination Office — made clear that, without proper interdependent collaboration and organizational management, implementing the program would not have been possible. The "Tu prepa en movimiento" program showed positive initial results in promoting healthy lifestyles and preventing risky behaviors among students. Specifically, through the promotion of sports and recreational activities, it achieved a considerable decrease in the number of referrals for problematic substance use compared to previous years.

In conclusion, Preparatoria 22's experience provides encouraging preliminary results regarding the potential of sports-educational programs for behavioral prevention in vulnerable environments. Nonetheless, greater infrastructure resources, schedule coordination, and multi-level work with families and communities are needed to substantially expand its impact. Building on its current foundation, the program can continue to be strengthened as an effective tool for promoting mental health and psycho-socio-educational prevention among this student population. School officials are committed to continuing to work along these lines. And although upper secondary education today continues to present major challenges, efforts focused on addressing students' needs must be accompanied by the clear understanding that we are forming people who need to be seen as unique individuals, and that before becoming great professionals, they must be people of integrity and citizens committed to their environment.
