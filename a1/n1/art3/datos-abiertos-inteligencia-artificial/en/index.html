Resumen.

El presente estudio realiza un revisión de la producción científica a través del análisis bibliométrico de datos sobre el estado del conocimiento que reporta Scopus en su repositorio en la conjunción de trabajos en inteligencia artificial (IA) y datos abiertos (DA), cuyos hallazgos permiten disipar las dudas sobre las implicaciones que las variables que producen estos campos de conocimiento generan y además, vislumbrar el enorme potencial de intervención de todas las disciplinas para la solución de problemáticas sociales, medioambientales, políticas, entre muchas otras. También es posible encontrar en este documento, información sobre el estado del arte de las aplicaciones de las disciplinas ante diversas hipótesis y otros indicadores de impacto de los tópicos de estudio.

Palabras clave:

Datos Abiertos, Inteligencia Artificial, Innovación, Gestión del Conocimiento, Bibliometría.

Abstract.

This study reviews the scientific production through bibliometric analysis of data about the state of knowledge reported by Scopus in its repository in the conjunction of research in artificial intelligence (AI) and open data (OD), whose findings allow us to dispel doubts about the implications generated by the variables produced in these fields of knowledge and also to glimpse the enormous potential of intervention of all disciplines for the solution of social, environmental and political problems, among many others. It is also possible to find in this document information about the state of the art of the applications applied by the disciplines to different hypotheses and other indicators of the impact of the topics of study.

Keywords:

Open Data, Artificial Intelligence, Innovation, Knowledge Management, Bibliometrics.

Introduction

Artificial Intelligence (AI) has appeared in our lives as a significant tool for value generation in our society, thanks to its multiple applications that make tasks more efficient in various (and practically all) fields, ranging from basic education to the aerospace industry (as indicated by Nalcı et al. (2023) in the work they presented on artificial neural networks and materials for said sector). Therefore, talking about AI is not only trendy but elemental to understand the innovation processes that humanity will experience in the years to come.

Open data: a vehicle for artificial intelligence innovation

Another innovation that goes hand in hand with this research work and that, as of the date of this writing, remains essential to analyze, understand, and, above all, apply, is Open Data (OD). Another tool that, through the technological deployment it implies and the legal and political framework its existence generates, creates innovation through the availability of data for all people and purposes. Therefore, it is socially powerful and inclusive, among other things, because it opens the doors to immense forms of knowledge.

This work carries out an evaluation of the most significant research on the threshold of scientific production, as an approach to the knowledge of the interrelationship presented by open data up to the year 2023 with artificial intelligence, to present as a result a perspective of the first ten years (or a little more) of the emergence of these variables in the priorities of people who do science. It also seeks to contribute with a look at the trends as a prospective for what will come in the next five or ten years, according to the considered works.

Why would this work be necessary? Taking the economic and administrative sciences and their impact on social sectors as a platform, this research is necessary due to the rapid growth of AI applications in both public and private organizations. Therefore, talking (writing) about this relatively new phenomenon in the neighborhood simply contributes to expanding knowledge on the subject and extending the possibilities to new forms of use for innovation and improvement in processes, communication, research, among others, and that all this leads to improving the conditions under which the organizations of the future will operate, more focused on the well-being of people, which incidentally implies a better environment.

In the same sense, OD will be, as long as access to information does not take root as a primary (common) practice in the daily work of organizations, a reason for examination in our current society, which, thanks to new information and communication technologies, requires accessible and manageable data to operate. Thus, both variables (although not the only ones handled in this document) - AI and OD - represent two topics that are inevitably committed to being studied.

News and published opinion in mass media and internet social networks, which have adapted from traditional mass broadcasting channels, daily document fears (human-computer) towards AI, echoing bad practices in its use, ranging from plagiarism in schoolwork (McClure, 2018), the substitution of human jobs by algorithms (Li & Huang, 2020), to autonomous driving (Cugurullo & Acheampong, 2023). Therefore, towards the transition of this technology, it is necessary to strengthen points of view and isolate knowledge to mitigate the impact of those concerns.

The same goes for OD, perfectly documented by the research work of Beno et al. (2017) regarding the barriers faced by users (problems accessing, understanding, and reusing these data), OD providers (fear of revealing their operational information, data security and privacy, and legal implications), and, of course, the political and legal (governmental) environment that faces enormous challenges in providing a clear and practical operating scheme.

Context of Artificial Intelligence (AI)

For this section of the research, the basic concepts in the context of AI and the fundamental definitions available in the literature are used alongside aspects of its understanding's composition for the economic and administrative sciences. Likewise, a brief history through key moments is included.

AI, despite being a concept that enjoys significant popularity today, has its origins in the 1950s, particularly since 1956 as an academic discipline, when Marvin Minsky and John McCarthy used the term to organize and conduct a summer workshop at Dartmouth College in New Hampshire (United States) funded by the Rockefeller Foundation. This setting attracted the participation of transcendental figures in the field, such as Nathaniel Rochester (who years later designed the IBM 701, the first commercial computer) and mathematician Claude Shannon (promoter of information theory), thereby establishing the foundations of a new knowledge generation ecosystem aimed at innovation in devices that emulate human intelligence (Haenlein & Kaplan, 2019).

Over time, computing capacity and the availability of information in the information technology ecosystem fostered the emergence of expert algorithms (capable of solving complex mathematical operations), artificial neural networks (Hebbian Learning) - later identified as deep learning - which emerge with the objective of imitating human brain learning (Haenlein & Kaplan, 2019).

On the other hand, Buchanan (2005) explains that the historical conceptualization of AI extends further back than academic approaches, as philosophers and inventors in different eras of humanity have conceptualized and realized ideas of intelligent artifacts whose main purpose is to improve people's lives.

For practical purposes in this study, AI is defined as an algorithmic set capable of interpreting data from the environment, understanding it, and using it to perform certain tasks (Haenlein & Kaplan, 2019), and in multiple fields (Khan & Chandna, 2023). The wealth of possibilities of AI instills in the topic a hopeful opportunity to improve processes in various disciplines that are explored later in their interaction with OD to create innovation in organizations (Morabito et al., 2024).

Context of Open Data (OD)

Now it's the turn of open data (OD, for its acronym in Spanish) - as they are also theoretically known - which, in this section, just like the previous one on AI, is discussed from its historical and contextual foundations to the extraction of academic definitions, aiming to establish the bases for its understanding in this writing.

Open data: a vehicle for artificial intelligence innovation

OD arises from the conception of freedom of information, enshrined in Article 19 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, which defines freedom of expression as a basic right of individuals. Based on this, various global and local initiatives shaped a scheme aimed at openness and access to public (and gradually, also private) information. This was consolidated with President Barack Obama's 2009 Memorandum on Transparency and Open Government, which opened the door to implementing open government data portals (later by more countries around the world) available to be used by all people (Charalabidis et al., 2018).

With this, open data was consolidated as a technical, technological, and organizational phenomenon that implies the ability to generate access with total freedom to obtain and reuse data, in a scheme involving the government, business sector, and citizenship (Orłowski, 2021).

Among the benefits of open data are: greater transparency, accountability, trust in government, greater citizen involvement and empowerment, improvement and innovation of government services, greater development of social innovations, greater business competitiveness, economic growth, data reuse, generation of new forms of data, and equal access to information (Charalabidis et al., 2018).

According to the International Open Data Charter (2023), open data is based on the following principles: openness, timeliness, accessibility and usability, comparability and interoperability, improving governance and social empowerment, inclusive development and innovation. Under this scheme, the fundamental mechanisms are established that allow observing the relevance that this public policy (and possible lifestyle) has for the development of humanity today, and more than fourteen years after its consolidation.

Once the conceptual framework and theories promoting the understanding of these two variables (AI and OD) have been established, a review based on the scientific elements of the recognized scientific repository Scopus where they interact will be developed below.

Interrelation of AI and OD for Innovation

In this section, the universe of consolidated scientific production extracted from the Scopus repository and dating from the period between 2010 and 2023 is explored. Production is mostly concentrated in the field of computer science (45.2%), mathematics (15.3%), engineering (11.1%), social sciences (5.8%), decision sciences (5.4%), medicine (2.2%), business, management and accounting (1.8%), physics and astronomy (1.8%), planetary sciences (1.4%), environmental sciences (1.4%), and others (8.6%) (Elsevier B.V., 2023).

In the same way, there is a rich production of conference papers (72.3%), articles (20.8%), among other documents, with affiliations mainly in the United States, Germany, Italy, France, United Kingdom, China, among others. The oldest document dates from 2010 and focuses on interconnected open data that can be found in the cloud and that, thanks to the semantic web, can be reused by multiple systems such as AI (Jain et al., 2010).

In the first years taken into account by this review of scientific production between OD and AI, documents can be found referring to different algorithms focused on locating OD for multiple applications (Brickley, 2010; Missier et al., 2010), AI applications based on information from sectors (such as the document presented by Mohaghegh (2011) on modeling oil reservoirs) and the generation of systems that learn from open data repositories (Bull et al., 2013). In this first period, more isolated works for both variables begin to be visualized, with slight interrelations.

From 2014 until 2018, a consolidation in scientific production with AI and OD themes is observed, with works such as those by Cordeiro et al. (2014) focusing on information management about humanitarian logistics and machine learning, or that presented by Ding et al. (2015) referring to intelligent models for decision-making in accessible tourism for people with disabilities, or the work that Babli et al. (2016) also developed regarding tourism focused on climate data.

The richness of approaches makes the peak stage of scientific production a paradise of topics where AI meets data on: security and crime (Rocca et al., 2016), semantics and sentiment through social media posts about brands (Saif et al., 2016), information on diseases and projections on medicines (Celebi et al., 2017), recognition systems and real-time Big Data (Rafferty et al., 2018), traffic accident prediction models (Cuenca et al., 2018), among countless diverse topics.

Finally, from 2019 to date (2023), research stand out due to their citation level on: improving the quality of OD for intelligent systems (Ferretti et al., 2019), public value generation (Criado & Gil-García, 2019), open science (Burgelman et al., 2019), and open-source intelligence (Pastor-Galindo et al., 2020), predictive neural networks for multiple phenomena and disciplines (Barrera et al., 2020; Ma, 2022; Norori et al., 2021; Rodgers et al., 2023), noticing a consolidation of topics towards concerns about the operability of AI through OD.

The interrelation of these technologies (OD and AI) with innovation in multiple fields is indisputable; furthermore, the connection of changes with technological evolution that does not discriminate disciplines or origins of problems to be solved is appreciated. Methodology of the Research Work

Below, the procedure under which the data obtained from the Scopus repository (Elsevier B.V., n.d.) will be analyzed is defined, in order to generate an explanation that reinforces its understanding and relevance. This database is recognized internationally thanks to the quality and rigor of the works indexed there (Powell & Peterson, 2017). Therefore, this study follows a qualitative methodology that allows exploring the field preliminarily and generating a necessary preamble for knowledge (Hernández Sampieri & Mendoza Torres, 2023).

Particularly, this work uses the bibliometric analysis of a database - described below - from which patterns in scientific production can be identified based on coincidences in the literature in terms of keywords, authors, countries of origin, among others, and which is possible thanks to the graphical outputs made possible by different platforms specialized in this type of data processing (Qu et al., 2023).

To generate the body of data to analyze, a search was carried out in the Scopus scientific database under the following parameters: Documents whose title was "Open Data" and also matched "Artificial Intelligence"; this process yielded a total of 423 works dating from 2010 to date (2023). From this, a database was generated and downloaded containing basic information to generate citations for the documents (author or authors' names, document title, year of publication, among others), bibliographical data (affiliation, ISSN, editors, etc.), abstracts, funding, among other data.

The database obtained in .RIS format, which is a plain text file that can contain bibliographical data (Biblioteca de la Universidad de Sevilla, 2023), gathers, among other things, 3,639 keywords, as well as data from 1,425 authors. In this way, there is a basic framework for analyzing the information through a bibliometrics system that allows finding similarities in the information.

The program used to perform this analysis was VOSviewer (Centre for Science and Technology Studies, n.d.) in version 1.6.20 for macOS, which allows mapping data based on the identification of co-occurrences between them; these data can refer to the authors of scientific documents or their keywords (van Eck & Waltman, 2014).

To generate the cluster visualizations, the analysis in VOSviewer was carried out using the database described above through a keyword co-occurrence analysis, generating the graphical representations of Figures 1, 2, and 3 showing data for all topics with five or more occurrences (142 in total), whose findings are described below.

Discussion Based on Findings

Innovation, despite not being a declared topic for this study, is the inherent link in this comparison between open data and artificial intelligence, as the very nature of these variables leads to manifestations of their existence in the form of new proposals in the use of information technologies to solve problems in various social spheres and the economy.

Although the interrelations of keywords appear in distinct colored clusters, they are not mutually exclusive; most interrelate with other sets and show that heterogeneous labels can exist that conceptually interrelate with related fields. However, this is something that analysis software like VOSviewer (or we, the researchers ourselves) should improve for future analysis.

Thanks to the findings, it can be understood that the conjunction between AI and OD remains a field for further knowledge extraction, especially for exploring new technologies in how storage and information extraction processes take place in the human mind (neural networks), with the aim of strengthening algorithms that emulate human reasoning.

It is foreseeable that data science and intelligent algorithms (deep learning) will not only be the task of computer sciences, but will require multidisciplinary intervention, especially from social sciences, since the design and study of public organization frameworks for data opening is a fundamental task.

Conclusions

The information presented in this study from the examined data makes it possible to look at the exceptional opportunity for dissertation on the paradigms represented by data science and deep learning through neural network schemes—a juncture that, for social sciences such as economic and administrative sciences, extends to the management of resources that are paramount for the fulfillment of these purposes.

Open data: a vehicle for artificial intelligence innovation

Another important aspect that draws attention in this work is the format in which research on these fields was presented, making it debatable that works presented in forums (conference papers) abound alongside a lack of deeper or more decisive dissertations on the phenomena.

This study is also an opportunity to dispel fears surrounding artificial intelligence and the replacement of people in various professional spheres, which for some represents this technological advancement. Through understanding the implications of the presented variables, these knowledge efforts can be viewed as a window to evaluate implications and pursue flaws to minimize risks.

Recommendations

A call is made to disciplines (especially economic and administrative sciences) that do not participate—or do not do so significantly—to intervene, present their discussions, and propose solutions in the field of artificial intelligence. And in the future, to not stop measuring and documenting the impact of these new technologies on society, the achievement of basic rights, and environmental sustainability.
