Abstract.

The text presents the experience of several entities of the University of Guadalajara in the creation of process management and certification systems. The organization's hierarchy has allowed each entity to create and adapt its own systems to its particularities and context. The objective that these entities follow is to achieve continuous improvement of processes in areas such as finances, school control, academic services, information security and governance. Management systems have evolved at different rates within each department, creating a valuable knowledge bank. The university's current strategic framework is addressed and emphasizes participatory planning. Recognizes the university's commitment to making its processes more efficient. The beginning of the design of management systems at the beginning of the 21st century and the certification of processes in different entities is described. Although efforts have been made to establish an institutional system, it has not evolved due to the particularities of each entity. In summary, the text presents the development of Quality Management Systems at the University of Guadalajara and emphasizes the importance of sharing experiences and improving processes in a diverse organization.

Keywords:

Continuous Improvement, Management Systems, Processes, Quality, Leadership.

Introduction

This paper seeks to share the experience that some entities of the Universidad de Guadalajara have had in the creation of management systems and process certification. It is important to start by clarifying that a general system in this regard has not been implemented in the institution, a situation that is considered to derive from the commitment to a decentralization that has allowed each university entity, such as the university centers or the education systems, to generate and adapt their own quality systems due to their particularities and context. In this sense, this reflection exercise has allowed internal dialogue about the experiences, obstacles, coincidences, and possibilities that lie ahead by sharing this interest in the continuous improvement of processes in different areas of management processes: Finance, School Control, Information Security in Technologies, and Administration.

Being such a diverse institution due to its dimensions, Management Systems have evolved at different paces and times in each dependency, generating, with these experiences, a highly valuable knowledge bank for the institution and for other similar entities that may eventually decide to seek certification. In the following pages, the aim is to develop how the design of quality management systems in the Red Universitaria has progressed, as well as the challenges and good practices that had to be implemented when developing the Management Systems.

Background: the current strategic framework of the Universidad de Guadalajara

The Universidad de Guadalajara is organized into a Red Universitaria resulting from the decentralization that took place in 1994 with the objective of "decentralizing the services and functions of the institution towards the different university entities and forming a more agile, dynamic institution open to contextual opportunities" (Universidad de Guadalajara, 2023, para. 3). Today, according to the statistics presented by the Rector General, Dr. Ricardo Villanueva Lomelí, in his Activity Report corresponding to the year 2021, the Universidad de Guadalajara is present in 110 of the 125 municipalities of the State of Jalisco, in which 16 university centers and 175 campuses of the Sistema de Educación Media Superior – known as SEMS – are located, and the Sistema de Universidad Virtual – named SUV -, through which 324,340 students were served in 2021 alone.

On the 25th anniversary of our Alma Mater, celebrated in 2019, the same Rector General acknowledged in an interview that "we have transitioned from centralized planning, conceived and directed by experts and executive personnel, to participatory planning" (Villanueva, 2019). It is precisely this participatory planning that has allowed us, as an institution, to include other planning methodologies, new management models, and observe with greater emphasis the control of our processes and the evaluation of results. The decentralization that characterizes us today has also allowed the generation of various efforts, the proposition of different projects, and, of course, the inter-departmental dialogue that allows us to share experiences and improve processes and models.

The scope of this diversity and dialogue is currently observed in the "Plan de Desarrollo Institucional 2019 – 2025, Visión 2030. Tradición y Cambio" of the UdeG, in whose integration different agents of the university community participate: students, researchers, directors, administrative workers, professors, etc. Under this PDI – as this Plan is commonly named -, the university entities are governed by a mission, a vision for 2030, 13 values manifested in the Código de Ética, observing the 17 Sustainable Development Goals, attending to three Principles of Institutional Management, namely: Governance, Austerity, and Transparency and Accountability; three Policies: Transversal, Inclusion, Innovation Management, and Uncertainty Management, three trajectories: Upper Secondary Level, Undergraduate, and Postgraduate, organized under four substantive purposes: Teaching and Academic Innovation, Research and technological and knowledge transfer, Extension and Social Responsibility, and Culture Dissemination.

Without analyzing the particularities of each of these elements of our PDI; they are simply enumerated because, within their framework, the commitment has been made to carry out, recover, or, where appropriate, renew the quality management systems that have been generated in some of the entities of the Red Universitaria in the last two decades.

Quality Culture at the Universidad de Guadalajara: the beginning.

The design of management systems in the institution dates back to the beginning of the 21st century, when in 2001, following the Programa Nacional de Educación 2001-2006, the Subsecretaría de Educación Superior e Investigación Científica implemented programs for the development of education that considered as one of their objectives the quality assurance of Higher Education Institutions through the accreditations of specialized organizations. In alignment with this, the then Rector General of the Universidad de Guadalajara, José Trinidad Padilla López, stated the following in his general work program:

In consistency with the new times, we will promote the change of organizational culture in the direction of efficiency and effectiveness, always seeking the optimal use of institutional resources. Therefore, we will set as a goal obtaining the ISO 9000 quality standard certification for our administrative processes. (Padilla, 2001, p.7)

Under this context, the process begins with the accreditation of a work team from the formerly named Coordinación General Administrativa as auditors in ISO 9000 with the objective of supporting the implementation processes of Quality Systems in administrative processes.

In 2002, a strategy to raise awareness of the Quality Culture was implemented, and work began to document and implement the certification of the main management processes, that is, those carried out by the Coordinación de Control Escolar and the Dirección de Finanzas. In the same framework, and as part of the work program regarding quality, in July 2003, certification was obtained for the processes related to the "Consulting service in the design and/or updating of management systems" of the Unidad de Sistemas y Procedimientos attached to the Coordinación General Administrativa.

Thus, in the period from 2004 to 2005, various entities of the Red Universitaria obtained the ISO 9001:2000 certification, which is observed as an efficient implementation of university management systems. Some of the entities that achieved the certification of their processes are the following:

  • Centro Universitario de Ciencias Exactas e Ingenierías (CUCEI), "Information selection and admission process".
  • Centro Universitario del Norte (CUNorte), "Diagnosis of educational needs, institutional design, and administration of learning units offered in said center".
  • Coordinación de Control Escolar, "Process of issuance and registration of titles, diplomas, and degrees. Student admission process".
  • Dirección de Finanzas, "Comprehensive administration of financial resources of the Universidad de Guadalajara".
  • Centro Universitario de la Ciénega (CUCiénega), "Teaching-learning process -from promotion, dissemination, training, and degree completion- of the bachelor's degrees in: Administration, Human Resources, Education, Marketing, International Business, Law, Accounting, Computing, Chemical Engineering, Pharmacobiological Chemist, Industrial Engineering, Computer Engineering, and Journalism".
  • Vicerrectoría Ejecutiva, "Institutional policies and strategic planning design of the Red Universitaria".
  • Centro Universitario de Arte, Arquitectura y Diseño (CUAAD), "School Control Processes".
  • Centro Universitario de la Costa Sur (CUCSur), "School Control and CRAI-Library Processes".

In 2005, the identification of transversal processes among Network Entities also began to consolidate Quality Management systems that involve more than one dependency. However, the attempt to generate a single institutional system by macroprocess at the Network level did not prosper, considering the particularity of each of the entities that comprise it, as well as the diversity of contexts in which each one is located. That is why the decision to establish a Quality Management System depends on each of these entities.

On the other hand, from 2005 to date, the Universidad de Guadalajara has undergone structural transformations of varying scope, both due to internal reorganization of areas and inter-departmental movements, and more recently a Reengineering that took place in 2020 concluding in 2022. In this transit of more than 15 years, some of the certified Red entities have been modified, and several did not migrate to subsequent versions of the ISO 9001:2000 standard; the main reason for this, it is considered, has stemmed from the lack of both financial and human resources or, alternatively, the incorporation of new laws and provisions that change the operation of the processes; a clear example of the latter is the entry into force of the Ley General de Contabilidad Gubernamental in 2012, which came to reform all the financial and accounting processes of the Red Universitaria.

The implementation of management systems: the experience of the Red Universitaria

The implementation of Quality Management Systems at the Universidad de Guadalajara has been carried out in a very diversified way, some with the accompaniment of an external consultant and others requesting the collaboration of talents from the institution who have the experience; the advantages and disadvantages of both scenarios are diverse, however, the objective has ultimately been achieved.

The key factor has been the use of a systemic approach to review the available information and to ensure that the information is used for decision-making in an effective manner. In this sense, the reviews have focused on the following three points:

  • Identify opportunities for improvement, learning, and innovation.
  • Evaluate the improvement actions carried out including aspects of adaptability, flexibility, and receptiveness in relation to the organization's vision and objectives.
  • Identify whether the resources provided have been adequate and whether they have been used effectively in achieving the organization's objectives.

For effective and efficient improvement, innovation, and learning, it has been fundamental that the people in the certified entities have the aptitude and are enabled to make judgments based on data analysis and the incorporation of lessons learned.

Likewise, we consider that, in the field of design and implementation of university management systems, the common denominator is the commitment of management, which is paramount to the success or failure of these processes. The challenge is not to achieve certification, it is to maintain and strengthen it over time.

Under this approach, it will be permitted to share in the following pages the experience of the different systems developed and still in force, regarding five specific topics: importance of Top Management in implementation; Integration of normative and regulatory frameworks; system management and implementation of good practices.

Importance of Top Management in the implementation of management systems

Current competitiveness and growing changes have caused organizations to seek some mechanism that guarantees sustainable success in meeting their objectives.

A proven mechanism that guarantees the fulfillment of its objectives is the implementation of a "Quality Management System," which allows decision-making and establishing objectives in the institution to achieve the improvement of its substantive processes. For public universities, such as the Universidad de Guadalajara, the challenge is no different.

Within the framework of the implementation of "Management Systems" and "Good Practice Schemes", it is essential to establish the extent to which "Top Management" must commit, since it becomes a necessity for the organization to function competitively. Here, it should be specified that, in the cases of university management systems, Top Management has been the rector of the university center, the general coordinator, the high school principal, etc.; that is, one must not forget that the systems have been implemented at the dependency level, not transversally in the Red Universitaria. Thus, Leadership and Commitment are a requirement aimed at ensuring that "Top Management" is exercising effective responsibility in the face of the need for improvement in the organization. Furthermore, that strategic vision becomes a fundamental piece for success in the development and implementation of Management Systems in Organizations.

Recurrently, executives continue to consider "Management Systems" as extra work to routine activities, which leads in many cases to a decrease in interest to the point of dropping this initiative or abandoning implementation. Why does this happen? Does top management assume the commitment and leadership to influence the change in mentality that is necessary to comply with the requirements of a Quality Management System? Or conversely, it is interesting to ask the question, are quality professionals ready to involve top management in these processes?

At the Universidad de Guadalajara, it can be observed that the constant of success in the different implementation projects for Quality Management Systems has been the vision, direction, and commitment of Top Management. Going hand in hand with Top Management has been an effective strategy that has allowed the identification and integration of internal and external considerations and requirements, the context of the organization, and its regulations, necessary to successfully carry out the implementation of a Management System.

It is important to mention that each of the Management Systems implemented within the university community of the UdeG are aligned with a strategic framework, which establishes direction and leadership, identifies and provides the necessary resources, maintains a documentary base, trains and communicates to its personnel, evaluates and improves. The result of this work model is reflected in the fulfillment and improvement of the substantive and adjective processes of this alma mater.

In summary, the leadership, presence, and participation of Top Management allow them to imprint their vision and identify obstacles or risks in advance, making the appropriate adjustments to achieve the objectives, with a team that is involved, strengthened, and focused on the same goal.

The UdeG is convinced that the sum of efforts regarding the implementation of management systems allows identifying the path to follow, that is, where it starts and where it is going, how to know that it has been achieved, and how to maintain and improve. A committed leader, in addition to being the strategic piece, will obtain valuable information required for decision-making in the exercise of their function.

It is worth closing this section by contextualizing the experiences and reflections faced over time regarding the implementation of Management Systems in the University. Undoubtedly, looking back a few years allows us to identify that in the management systems of the university network the team starts with great enthusiasm; however, the implementation actions begin to generate chaos in daily activities, since it breaks the schemes in which work was being done, causing a slowdown, due to the situations they encounter and resistance to change as the main factor.

Top Management is no stranger to these scenarios where the initial plan is optimistic, but during implementation, adjustments need to be made, which can cause conflicts among participants and/or interested parties, in some cases leading to abandoning implementation projects. However, the tenacity of Top Management together with key pieces of the work team have made it possible to cross that moment of chaos, achieve the success of the implementation, and begin to obtain and recognize the benefits. Once the system achieves its objective, it is recognized that the effort was worth it and the best thing is knowing that there is a convinced, visionary strategist capable of taking the organization to the next levels of maturity.

Integration of normative and regulatory frameworks

Making the strategic decision to implement a Quality Management System involves a series of internal and external analyses in organizations. The ISO standard itself refers to the obligation to analyze the context in which it develops; for this, it is extremely important to make an evaluation of those factors that represent a strength or an opportunity for improvement. Once how they are found is determined, the applicable regulations, both internal and external, must be identified. A fundamental part of this process is the review of normative frameworks in conjunction with the regulatory frameworks that govern the procedures under which activities are developed.

The normative frameworks, like the ISO standards, establish those "MUSTS" that are considered an obligation to monitor and comply with, since they represent a very important basis for defining the documentation of all those processes, subprocesses, and procedures that make up the Quality Management Systems (De la Torre & Bejarano, 2013). ISO standards determine the mandatory compliance in their requirements "ability to consistently provide products and services that meet customer and applicable statutory and regulatory requirements" (ISO 9000, 2016); these are also part of the reference framework.

Meeting customer requirements and operating under continuous improvement models are the main objectives of a Quality Management System; however, the normative obligations that regulate must be considered, for example, to pass subjects, students (clients) would request to do so only by attendance, however, the internal rules of the UdeG require meeting a measurement parameter (average).

In this sense, when establishing the bases of an effective Management System, documenting the processes requires considering all those standards, laws, policies, and internal and external regulations that are of mandatory applicability (ISO 9001, 2016). However, this represents a huge challenge, since regulations and requirements sometimes do not work together, given that government agency requirements complicate and hinder the development of processes.

The normative and regulatory framework of a Quality Management System marks a reference of what is required to be fulfilled, which is mainly composed of:

  • ISO Standard to be implemented
  • Internal regulations
  • External regulations

At the Universidad de Guadalajara, internal regulations are publicly available on websites, which speeds up their consultation and communication. These regulations are composed of the following documents:

  • Ley Orgánica de la Universidad de Guadalajara
  • Estatuto General de la UdeG
  • Estatuto del Centro Universitario
  • Reglamento de ingreso de aspirantes
  • Reglamento de egreso
  • Procedimientos del Presupuesto de Ingresos y Egresos
  • Reglamento de Adquisiciones, Arrendamientos y Contratación de Servicios de la Universidad De Guadalajara

Regarding external regulations, there are various documents and standards to which processes and procedures must adhere. On the one hand, being a public institution, it has obligations regarding transparency and accountability; therefore they adhere to federal standards regarding finance and public administration; governed by state public resource exercise laws and guidelines, among others. The variability is wide considering the diversity of fields in university work.

Having clarity about the normative and regulatory frameworks that apply to the processes that make up the Management Systems at the Universidad de Guadalajara has been of great importance for the operation to be carried out adequately and has also allowed the improvement of processes, not only for those certified Network entities but for the whole University in general, as there are exchange forums in which experiences and best practices have been shared that sometimes have strengthened the updating of rules and policies.

System management

The reflection on management systems and how they are controlled must be carried out considering two realities: the exemplary cause and the efficient cause. By exemplary cause we understand the standards, principles, ideas, guidelines, among others that dictate "how it should be", that is, they pose the utopian reality of the design, development, implementation, and control of a Quality Management System. And by efficient cause, we observe what happens in an organization when it implements a management system.

Now, the "should be" of a QMS starts, as mentioned, from the standards, principles, and guidelines in relation to the context and objectives of the organization, which implies development in a conscious commitment to the compliance-application of the Quality Management Principles; congruently, the control of the QMS implies in turn, an attitude of permanent openness to change, which should derive from an overall vision, with an analytical-deductive mentality of the reality from which each institution interacts.

Under this perspective, it must be emphasized that the seven quality management principles should not be lost sight of and must be observed by all members of the organization from its different levels, starting with management (Gutiérrez, 2005). These principles are: customer (or user) focus, leadership, engagement of people, process approach, continuous improvement, evidence-based decision making, and relationship management.

With this perspective and with the aim of conducting and operating an organization successfully looking towards quality improvement, the best use of resources and above all the satisfaction of its users, the Universidad de Guadalajara has sought and applied different strategies to achieve this and other objectives. One of the tools is management systems which have had different contexts, stages, and times, causing changes, sometimes superficial and other times essential. Below, the points that each entity considers most outstanding from its experience in the management of its respective systems will be shared.

Coordinación General de Extensión y Difusión Cultural

For this coordination, the core part of its QMS control is the commitment of top management, which together with the process leaders have established the guidelines, activities, and directives for the review and evaluation of the system, which, periodically, seeks to verify and evaluate the convenience, adequacy, efficacy, efficiency, and effectiveness of the system, essential activities in the search for continuous improvement. For their part, each leader and process manager, in managing their task, has determined their objectives and indicators, as well as the periodicity for their measurement, continuous evaluation, and decision-making regarding the risk management process.

Sistema de Educación Media Superior Escuela Preparatoria No. 5

In this dependency, the allocation of budget for the development of its activities is understood as a fundamental part of a QMS, such as: the programming and payment of internal and external audits as appropriate; constant training and the corresponding inputs for a proper development of the activities that the QMS implies.

CUCiénega

It is considered that the functions of the QMS manager determine the effectiveness of the actions regarding the results, that is, their performance, which potentiates any opportunity for improvement and contributes to raising awareness of the requirements and needs of the user at all levels of the organization and evidence-based decision making. These functions are framed in the planning, organization, direction, and control of verification activities of the fulfillment of corrective actions, and review of the results of the evaluations, internal and external audits, compliance with legal requirements and other requirements that the organization has, the measurement of user satisfaction, and the proposal of actions for continuous improvement. For its part, the leadership exercised by the Rectorate, which is primarily responsible for the System and continuous improvement, constitutes the cornerstone of the participation of all members of the organization.

CUAltos

In this University Center, the control of its QMS is exercised from top management, showing the importance of leadership, exposing the benefits of having a QMS, analyzing the advantages of having one and its impact before the accrediting bodies of Educational Programs (PE), which shows the commitment with which it is carried out, resulting in the optimization of efforts.

CUCSur

In this dependency it is considered that to have an adequate control of the QMS it is essential to form a Quality Certification Subcommittee, which from its experience has been integrated by: Top Management (Rector, Academic Secretary, Administrative Secretary), the Management Representative, the QMS Manager, and process leaders, thus patenting commitment and leadership at all levels of the organization, who, based on the internal and external context, as well as the efficient administration of resources: human, material, financial, technological, and infrastructure, ensure the maintenance of the QMS, supported by the procedures of bimonthly visits, internal and external audits, processes that have been described and documented to establish action lines, constancy, and traceability.

Implementation of good practices

Finally, regarding the implementation of good practices, it can be commented that, with the intention of efficiently and effectively complying with product and service requirements, to ensure customer satisfaction, Management Models have been implemented in the different Management Systems of the Universidad de Guadalajara that are prepared to face the risks and seize the opportunities of the institution.

Among the actions that have favorably impacted the quality management systems themselves and added value to the processes are those listed below.

  1. Commitment of the personnel involved in the certified processes to learn and apply all the "shalls" and "shoulds" of the standard.
  2. Awareness of Management and personnel regarding the benefits and importance of implementing a Management System.
  3. Opportunity to exercise leadership, persuasion and negotiation capacity, as well as improvements in the working relationship and empathy between the different dependencies.
  4. Frequent meetings of the System leaders with Top Management, weekly or biweekly, as required, based on their communication management procedure.
  5. Permanent analysis and evaluation of processes, identifying opportunities for improvement, the possibility of expanding the System, incorporating new processes, and eliminating others.
  6. Identification of organizational training gaps to thus establish action plans that adjust to reality. Continuous training for personnel and the audit team, through effective, coordinated, reliable, continuous, and sustainable annual training programs and plans, allowing correcting deficiencies in personnel competencies and skills to thus better fulfill job profiles.
  7. Use of technologies that make the System available to all personnel through internal information management platforms, as is the case of "Intranet" of the Coordinación General de Extensión y Difusión Cultural.
  8. Control of documented information in Master Lists, coding and classification of formats and documents that allow safeguarding all internal and external documented information.
  9. Simplification of formats and the elaboration of manuals and instructions.
  10. Periodic reviews by the System manager to the certified processes and those to be certified which includes all internal and external documented information, documentary control, and the updating of information on the Intranet, in the case of Extensión y Difusión Cultural.
  11. Execution of internal and external audits to determine the degree of compliance with the requirements present in all ISO Regulations, as well as adherence to university regulations and in this way identify non-conformities and opportunities for improvement in order to establish action plans to resolve them and take advantage of them as appropriate.
  12. Constant improvement of the QMS so that processes become more efficient and have a better performance, thus achieving customer satisfaction.
  13. The majority of the personnel within the scope of the certification know and use the platform where the interaction of processes and the control master list are described; likewise, the high degree of commitment to attend to users in the best way supporting them in whatever they need, without a "bad face".
  14. The implementation of the program of bimonthly, quarterly visits or according to the process, for the follow-up of improvement projects, the analysis of their risk management matrices, the indicators, and essentially the execution of their processes.

The management systems of the Universidad de Guadalajara are committed to the search, application, and evaluation of actions that provide performance improvements, strengthening those actions that require it and eliminating the non-significant ones, with the purpose of reducing risks and making their processes more efficient; replicating the positive experiences that can be used in other dependencies of the same University.

Conclusions

The characteristics of a QMS of the Universidad de Guadalajara cannot be concretely defined, since one of the challenges it presents is the transversal management in its processes, in just one of them; however, what can be identified are the actions and strategies that have supported achieving the consolidation of the QMS that have been implemented in the Red Universitaria; all the systems of the Network raise some guidelines they have taken and that are their way of landing and putting into practice the guidelines and regulations proposed to make a reality.

Among the major difficulties identified, they can be concentrated into three. On the one hand, resistance to change among workers, collaborators, and process participants. The implementation of specific methodologies to comply with the corresponding ISO standards has faced this problem in its early stages. The second, related to the previous one, has to do with the perception of the participants of the certified processes who usually observe the implementation of new practices as an increase in their activities, which leads to pessimism and apathy regarding compliance with the implementation strategies.

Lastly, the issue of financing. The lack of budget allocated to management systems has also been observed as a limitation for their promotion or implementation. Although the Universidad de Guadalajara has Quality as a management principle, the budget it has must be prioritized annually in each of the entities of the Red Universitaria, so achieving an allocation for the design, implementation, and management of a system becomes a challenge and at the same time a satisfaction.

However, despite the identified problems, the benefits of implementing management systems within our institution have allowed observing the effectiveness and efficiency of processes and resources more routinely; even allowing observing future possibilities for expansion and implementation at a higher level. They have made possible a better performance and control of processes, giving voice to our users and adding value to services.

The conclusion in this sense is that the management of continuous improvement depends on and develops from six points:

  • Management leadership.
  • The establishment of a Quality and continuous improvement committee.
  • Training and motivation of QMS members.
  • That a management system documenting processes is developed.
  • Maintain active communication about all changes, since these affect all members of the institution.
  • Identify new projects that arise from problems.

Finally, to close this dissertation, another aspect that seems fundamental for the future of the institution's management systems is taken up again.

Following the invitation of the Asociación Nacional de Universidades e Instituciones de Educación Superior (ANUIES) for the Universidad de Guadalajara to integrate into the recently formed Regional Network: Quality Management and Continuous Improvement, the work teams that have and have had management systems have met to share experiences and good practices regarding QMS.

In this way, a multidisciplinary team was formed by personnel from nine university dependencies, among which seven have an active certificate and two more were certified and are considering the possibility of renewing their certification. Pioneers in the implementation of management systems in the UdeG and other institutions collaborate in this group, and it even has the participation of an expert in the field, who has served as an external advisor and participated in the certification processes of some dependencies. Also part of the team is the Coordinación de Mejora Regulatoria e Innovación para la Gestión of our University, whose participation allows having the vision of the applicable standard, which becomes an additional advantage of this multidisciplinary team.

Upon integrating this team, the following are defined as areas of work:

  1. Savings in both human and economic resources of the Systems.
  2. Joint audit program.
  3. Form a network of Internal Auditors.

In this sense, it is considered that this working group is a great opportunity for the growth and strengthening of management systems, not only because of the experience some of its members have, but also because of the dialogue that can be generated between the diverse disciplinary profiles and contexts in which the participants' experiences are located. In this way, there is a commitment to growing this internal network and collaborating both with other entities of the institution and with other Universities and public entities in the region; it is believed that strengths, when shared, can contribute to Continuous Improvement in other systems, and that is why the initiative of integrating the Regional Quality Management Network is embraced.
