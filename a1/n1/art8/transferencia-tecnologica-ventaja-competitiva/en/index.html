Technology transfer as a competitive advantage in artisanal businesses

La transferencia tecnológica como ventaja competitiva en las empresas artesanales

GESPYE GESTIÓN PÚBLICA Y EMPRESARIAL (e-ISSN: 3061-8630)

Year 1, No. 1 (Jan-Jun 2024) • pp. 83-92 • DOI https://doi.org/10.32870/gespye.vi1.8

Received: 15/12/2023 • Approved: 22/02/2024 • Published: 30/04/2024

Abstract.

The purpose of this paper is to show technology transfer as a catalyst in the modern world, and its relationship with the growth of artisanal enterprises that, although they use traditional means, are allowed to compete effectively and efficiently in any market, even as competitors of those that carry out industrialized production. Artisanal companies can take advantage of their characteristics to gain a competitive advantage. A descriptive, documentary and explanatory theoretical methodology was used. It is important that the handicraft process considers everything that occurs in its environment or context, respecting the characteristics of this type of production, which is considered unique, thus preserving its authenticity and cultural value. It is concluded that handicraft companies have important characteristics and can take advantage of them to obtain significant competitive advantages.

Keywords:

Technology transfer; artisanal companies; competitive advantage.

Introduction

Artisanal companies are those that dedicate the production of their goods using traditional methods and manual techniques. Handicrafts are recognized not only as souvenirs, but they also form part of an intangible heritage that is gaining increasing recognition worldwide. The companies dedicated to their production are aware that they have the potential to contribute to social, economic, and cultural development.

This allows them to compete more effectively and efficiently, as well as to participate in any market, even alongside those carrying out industrialized production. It is important for the artisanal process to consider everything occurring in its environment or context, respecting the characteristics of this type of production which is considered unique, thereby preserving its authenticity and cultural value. Artisanal companies possess key characteristics that can be leveraged to gain significant competitive advantages.

Technology transfer is essential for economic and social progress by creating opportunities and spreading innovation, as well as generating new knowledge that can drive the development of new products, improve industrial process efficiency, or generate new jobs. This will help bridge technological gaps between countries or regions, promoting a more equitable and sustainable development.

Artisanal Companies

The origin of handicrafts in Mexico is a cultural heritage dating back to pre-Hispanic times, which has been modified over the years with new techniques, designs, and materials. Therefore, it is important to preserve and protect it as a guide for future generations through knowledge management as well as technology transfer (Sales, 2013).

The very need to enter a globalized world has required the formalization of new ways of working, processes, knowledge, and consequently other forms of organization, as activities carried out in workgroups can have significant reach regarding innovation, technology transfer, and the economy itself. If these workgroups could collaborate, changes in vision would be proposed; by formalizing their businesses, they can integrate research and development as new resources and increase their capabilities to achieve greater competitiveness.

Artisans constitute groupings that require forms of organization that enable them to be more competitive. Therefore, it is important to mention that in their effort to strengthen work systems for adaptation to the environment, companies seek relationships with others that allow them to increase market share, contributing not only to their own growth but also to that of their state. By creating artisanal businesses, the possibility arises to create new ways of working to improve the performance of micro and small enterprises and generate competitive advantages (Ramírez, 2011).

Over time, manufacturing in artisanal enterprises has given rise to the creation of new crafts, as they have had to make changes in their production process through innovation and technology implementation, generating a transition from a rural economy to an industrialized economy. In the mid-19th century, in Latin America, there was a defeatist perception regarding the dichotomy between the modern and the archaic, transforming progress into positivist thought (Dos Santos, 2006).

Artisanal enterprises have very specific characteristics such as:

  1. Manual production: processes in these companies largely depend on manual labor, which tends to be slow and detailed.
  2. Focus on quality: attention to detail in product manufacturing is very high, as they are committed to excellence in their work.
  3. Creativity and design: plays a major role as their products are usually unique and inspired by cultural traditions, history, and creative innovation.
  4. Customized production: artisanal businesses offer custom-made products to meet the specific needs and preferences of their clients.
  5. Valuation of craftsmanship: art is valued and craft-making is a skill passed down from generation to generation; these businesses are rooted in local communities that help preserve cultural traditions.
  6. Sustainability: the use of natural materials and environmentally friendly manufacturing techniques emphasize the sustainability of artisanal enterprises.

Despite the prevalence of industrialized production, artisanal enterprises produce a wide variety of unique products ranging from ceramics, jewelry, clothing or textiles, and woodworking to food products. They continue to be valued for their attention to detail and their ability to offer excellent, high-quality products, which will maintain their distinctive connection to history and culture.

Family businesses have a strong connection with their communities; they frequently participate in community events and collaborate with other local businesses, fostering a stronger connection with consumers and building a loyal customer base. Consumers increasingly value sustainability, as they consider artisanal manufacturing techniques to be more environmentally friendly and perceive these businesses as practicing higher ethical standards than large corporations. Although production may be traditional, these businesses adapt quickly to modern marketing trends through websites, social media, and online sales platforms.

Technology Transfer

Technology transfer is a process in which knowledge, skills, processes, and technological products are shared to form the system known as a company, but it can also form other types of organizations such as government. The technology transfer process can occur in various ways, including collaboration between companies known as collaborative networks, business networks, clusters, and strategic alliances, among others. Within these, there are institutions that acquire technology, hire technical experts, and obtain patents.

Challenges may arise, such as intellectual property, guarantees in collaboration agreements, and the adaptation of applied technology to specific contextual needs. Therefore, creating public policies and regulatory frameworks that foster fair technology transfer and bring benefits to all parties involved is crucial.

Hernández (2007) points out that characteristics in product development within artisanal enterprises should be identified, where radical innovations are scarce since they depend on the creativity and ingenuity of artisans. This will allow the identification of elements susceptible to modification in production processes, which require formal and informal knowledge, routines, as well as technological and organizational skills.

Universities are a key factor for scientific and technological development as they combine unique attributes and a high concentration of individuals who generate new knowledge and skills to undertake technological developments. This enables the creation, dissemination, and socialization of knowledge, creating synergy with artisanal businesses. Both universities and businesses need to respond to new challenges and research and development problems to conceive new applications for developed technology.

The technology transfer process must occur through collaboration among enterprises, higher education institutions, research centers, and government, enabling patent generation or technical expert hiring. It is essential for economic and social progress, contributing to closing technological gaps between countries and regions while promoting more equitable and sustainable development. As challenges arise—such as securing collaboration agreements and adapting technologies to meet specific local needs—it is necessary to create public policies and regulatory frameworks that encourage fair and beneficial technology transfer for all parties involved.

Although at first glance it might seem that artisanal businesses do not need advanced technology due to their traditional methods, the selective integration of technology can provide competitive advantages, impacting the competitiveness of such enterprises. Technology transfer must be carried out carefully and respectfully regarding the nature of these businesses; while technology does not replace manual labor, creativity can be deployed as a tool to enhance these companies' competitiveness in today's market.

Competitive Advantage

Competitive advantage is an attribute or resource possessed by companies that allows them to stand out in their industry and outperform competitors. These advantages can arise from different areas and vary depending on the type of company and its context. According to Porter (2007), companies achieve competitive advantages when they execute strategic actions to be more efficient than competitors, such as cost leadership or offering differentiated products or services.

Competitiveness cannot be conceived without Porter's five forces, which can be summarized in the following points:

• Direct competition forces increased productivity and innovation generation.

• Consumers with new demands drive competitiveness by requiring quality products and services.

• Information exchange with companies in the same sector can promote a continuous flow of new ideas.

• Investment in research and development generates hard-to-imitate products and services, creating a competitive advantage.

Porter also mentions that a network of support levels must exist to assist related companies and foster their growth. However, it is necessary to highlight the contributions of Etzkowitz and Leydesdorff (2000) with the Triple Helix model, which identifies the essential components for economic functioning and growth: industry, government, and academia.

The government plays an important role by striving to incentivize companies to improve performance; however, higher education institutions must also intervene, as they are responsible for providing the labor market with professionals possessing required skills and knowledge—more creative, inventive, motivated, proficient in other languages, and skilled in Information and Communication Technologies (ICTs) to contribute to organizational success.

The World Economic Forum (2016) defines competitiveness as "the set of institutions, policies, and factors that determine the level of productivity of an economy, which in turn sets the level of prosperity that can be achieved." In evaluating 137 countries, Mexico stagnated at 51st place in this area, retaining the same position as the previous year.

Competitiveness in artisanal businesses may differ from that of large corporations, but it remains essential for their market success, provided they identify and apply factors that contribute to quality, customization, differentiation, community connection, and sustainability.

Methodology

Sampieri (2018) notes that all research work must be grounded in two approaches: qualitative and quantitative, and from their combination, a third approach emerges: mixed methods. Having described how the initial stages of the research will be conducted, this study aims to employ a mixed-methods approach, relying not only on written sources but also on fieldwork, both striving to explain the causes and effects of data regarding artisans in Tonalá, Jalisco.

This paper utilizes a descriptive, documentary, and explanatory methodology to develop a reflective proposal based on a literature, conceptual, theoretical, and empirical review.

Discussion

Technological achievements arising in the 20th century surpassed those of previous eras. In large corporations, machines carry out most industrial work, helping produce unique and affordable goods, meet market demand, and generate new knowledge. During the 1970s, significant changes occurred across various areas of the labor market, leading to greater diversity in the workforce.

Artisanal enterprises must leverage their capabilities, as identifying their creations as unique requires giving weight to intangible value—hence the importance of trademark registration. Through experience, the artisanal sector knows how to value its creations; repeating techniques leads to perfection, but this must also be replicated in organizational systems. Protecting resources and capabilities will provide this sector with sustainable advantages.

Artisans are considered experts in their techniques, but that does not prevent them from strengthening their capabilities and using knowledge management to their advantage. They must defend their traditions while transmitting knowledge and generating innovative ways to remain active in new markets. Miller (1993) points out that organizations fail because they concentrate their successful activities on the past without exploring new variants; therefore, a review of administrative practices is proposed.

Artisanal enterprises are regularly founded or led by master artisans expert in their trade, owners of their businesses, creating and selling products independently. Other enterprises are started by creative entrepreneurs with a broader vision, developing and marketing products by leveraging their creativity and entrepreneurial skills. Larger or more established artisanal businesses exist, directed by management teams overseeing all facets of the operation. Artisanal collectives collaborate to produce and sell products, sometimes operating as non-profit organizations or cooperatives making collaborative decisions. Family businesses also exist where family members serve as leaders making strategic decisions, and some artisanal companies are founded by artists or designers who use their creativity and talent to offer products.

Opportunities to be seized lie within the crises encountered in difficult economic environments, using strategies that better connect organizations with society to solve poverty, health, environmental, economic, and social issues, as organizations directly influence a country's economic development. Given global shifts, Porter's proposals should be re-evaluated, as they were originally framed from a supply perspective rather than a demand perspective.

In July 2016, the Ministry of Economy (SE), the National Institute of the Entrepreneur (INADEM), the National Bank of Foreign Trade (BANCOMEXT), and the National Institute of Statistics and Geography (INEGI) presented the National Survey on Productivity and Competitiveness of Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (ENAPROCE). The survey showed that micro-enterprises account for 75.4% of total employed personnel, followed by small enterprises at 13.5% and medium enterprises at just over 11%. Therefore, if micro and small enterprises contribute the highest percentage to GDP, it would be advisable to evaluate the business structures supporting these enterprises.

New theories exist to help managers discover fresh administrative management strategies, considering a more holistic, challenging, and innovative perspective. According to Dussel (2001), competitiveness is the dynamic integration process of countries and products into international markets, depending on both supply and demand conditions.

As noted by A. Ali (2007), the crumbling of trade barriers and the growing appetite of consumers in the developed world for goods produced in distant locations generate both fear and optimism. This indicates that consumers continuously develop new needs and require new satisfiers; if companies fail to respond with equal speed, they may disappear or be absorbed by those willing to invest in research and development and move away from protectionism.

The local economy indicates that economic opportunities offer better prospects in origin countries, likely because local organizations have finally learned that talent should remain at home and that new leaders with fresh ideas should be better utilized. Quality goods do not necessarily have to be expensive, and if locally made goods are high quality, less will be spent on logistics, reaching consumers faster.

These are characteristics that the artisanal sector can leverage in its favor by highlighting qualities of creativity and innovation. If artisans wish to remain in their communities while gaining recognition elsewhere, combining resources from government, universities, and business will ensure productivity and achieve desired competitiveness.

In 1955, the National Bank of Foreign Trade conducted studies on handicraft production to lay the foundations for economic assistance to plastic arts and artistic crafts, encouraging export; in Mexico, handicraft commercialization has largely occurred through retailers and in tourist areas (Cordero, 2007).

Knowledge is a key factor strengthening companies in developing new products, placing heavy emphasis on creativity and enabling better business management. Establishing formal administration leads to higher product innovation and increased effectiveness in artisans' living standards; process knowledge is considered a major source of development (Harris et al., 2000).

Some of the ways technology transfer can positively impact artisanal enterprises include:

  1. Process improvements: implementing state-of-the-art technology such as specialized machinery or advanced software tools optimizes production processes, making enterprises more efficient and reducing time and costs.
  2. Quality enhancement: required standards can be guaranteed by incorporating inspection, monitoring, and tracking technologies into production.
  3. Market expansion: adopting new technology allows artisanal enterprises to reach new markets via e-commerce platforms and social media, boosting sales and product visibility.
  4. Innovation and differentiation: technology transfer stimulates innovation in design and production, enabling the creation of unique, differentiated products.
  5. Training and skills development: implementing new technology requires training and development for employees, ensuring tools are fully leveraged while improving employability.

A competitive advantage is essential for long-term business success in an increasingly competitive and dynamic market, where these advantages must be continuously identified and cultivated to maintain industry relevance and profitability—thus linking artisanal enterprises with technology transfer and competitiveness.

University research contributes to industrial innovation and serves as an important driver for industrial property registration and technological development (Mansfield, 1995). Academic knowledge is transferred through mechanisms like patent generation, know-how, and trade secrets. University-industry collaboration fosters research and consulting contracts (González, 2013).

Conclusions

Artisanal enterprises can be led by individuals with diverse capabilities and skills, from artisans working individually or in teams to manage their organization, to creative entrepreneurs or artisan collectives. Regardless of leadership, they share a deep commitment to quality, authenticity, and creativity.

Competitiveness differs across enterprises regardless of size, but it is fundamental for artisanal businesses to build competitive advantages. Consumers value attention to detail and unique craft characteristics; offering distinct products helps attract customers seeking alternatives to large brands. Personalized attention enhances customer satisfaction and builds loyalty.

The importance of competitiveness lies in company resources and capabilities. With globalization, contributing factors become more fluid; while price and quality remain essential, workers' wages, knowledge, skills, and abilities are vital to optimizing administrative management and driving productivity, provided job security is assured.

The challenge lies in how quickly organizations adapt and respond to change—the ability to generate profitability tied to survival capability. Artisans are no exception; globalization has reached them, demanding innovation without sacrificing tradition. Competitiveness in artisanal businesses seeks to preserve authentic community connections while applying strategies to compete successfully by attracting consumers who value product authenticity and quality.

Artisanal enterprises must take initiative while accepting government support for financing, university support for education and training, and collaborative team support for knowledge sharing. They must apply management processes required for knowledge management to reinvent themselves, taking advantage of technology transfer and digital tools to access new markets while registering tangible and intangible assets through intellectual property.

It must be ensured that technology transfer is context-sensitive, respecting the unique characteristics of artisanal production so that authenticity and cultural value are preserved.

Linking artisanal enterprises with technology transfer generates significant benefits, helping improve process efficiency, quality, and competitiveness, while broadening market reach, innovation, and differentiation.
