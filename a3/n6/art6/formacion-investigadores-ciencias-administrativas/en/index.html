Abstract.

A longitudinal study examines the evolution of the faculty at the Department of Administration of the University Center for Economic and Administrative Sciences (CUCEA), where the number of research professors has decreased due to the personal life circumstances of the professors themselves. Therefore, the training of researchers should be a priority for knowledge generation, as it would allow the University of Guadalajara to improve its teaching, academic management, mentoring, and research activities—tasks that are sometimes hindered by factors such as budget constraints. This prevents the incorporation of more researchers in the face of increasing retirements and deaths of professors, as well as their participation in programs like PRODEP and SNI. To maintain a competitive educational level, the consolidation of new research professors must be promoted. The data was obtained in two periods, 2012 and 2024, through surveys of the Department's research professors. The approach is qualitative, descriptive, and non-experimental, and it demonstrates that the research staff needs to be strengthened.

Keywords:

Sciences, administrative, research, administration, professors.

Introduction

Research has been, since its inception, the pillar upon which the great transformations of human knowledge are built: without research there is no progress, and without progress there is no social, technological, or economic evolution. In an increasingly interconnected world, university centers not only train professionals but also have the responsibility to promote the generation of new knowledge through the training of researchers, which requires a structured effort from universities and governments to guarantee their permanence, development, and consolidation. Globally, universities with solid research models have become innovation leaders that link their researchers with industry, government, and international organizations; however, a lack of institutional support, insufficient funding, and precarious working conditions limit the advancement of knowledge in many regions. Therefore, understanding the situation of researcher training is a strategic necessity, as a university that does not invest in research is reduced to transmitting information without contributing to global knowledge.

In Mexico, university research faces structural challenges that have hindered its growth and consolidation as a strategic axis of development. Although the country has highly prestigious institutions, the decrease in public funding, administrative obstacles, and the lack of full-time positions have limited the training of new research staff. While the Sistema Nacional de Investigadores (SNI) and the Programa para el Desarrollo Profesional Docente (PRODEP) have attempted to strengthen scientific production, access to these incentives remains limited, especially in administrative and economic areas.

At the state level, Jalisco has a great responsibility in the consolidation of researchers, as it is one of the states with the greatest economic and technological dynamism and home to the Universidad de Guadalajara. Even so, it faces a lack of resources and scarce support for academic bodies: the reduction of the university budget and the high teaching load have limited the generation of knowledge applicable to the state's development. Therefore, it is a priority to strengthen the training of researchers with adequate funding, job stability, and links with the productive sectors. Because research is a substantive activity of universities, it represents the opportunity to generate new knowledge through the work of its research professors; however, it has been declining due to the reduction of federal and state resources and the precarious allocation of funds for computer equipment, specialized software, academic stays, and travel expenses.

Human resources linked to research are permanently decreasing, whether due to retirement or, recently, to the increase in deaths resulting from the COVID-19 pandemic, despite the hiring of adjunct professors who, in most cases, are only dedicated to teaching. To understand this situation, two surveys were applied, in 2012 and 2024, from a longitudinal perspective, in the Department of Administration of CUCEA, addressing: educational degree; type of appointment; gender; job seniority; lines, projects, and objects of study; research products; academic bodies; main difficulties; funding, and research networks. The analysis shows the need to incorporate research professors to strengthen teaching, academic management, tutoring, and research, functions that federal organizations evaluate through key indicators to allocate budget.

The contribution of this study lies in placing the phenomenon in a historical moment of transformation of the teaching profile and answering: how does the situation of the teaching staff influence the generation of knowledge in the face of the generational relay of researchers?, and what is the role of research professors in the permanence and consolidation of the staff given the generational change? Given retirements and deaths, and in a global context where leading universities have consolidated innovation ecosystems, Mexico requires policies that guarantee academic and scientific competitiveness. Therefore, it is proposed to create internal mechanisms and guidelines, as well as support programs from the University Center and the Universidad de Guadalajara, to incorporate new researchers and support existing ones.

Legal Aspects

Knowledge of legal aspects is essential when investigating the internal development of academic personnel at the Universidad de Guadalajara, and in particular at CUCEA, as these normative frameworks guarantee transparency, methodological rigor, and ethics, protect teachers' rights, and ensure that the findings are valid and applicable to institutional improvement. CUCEA's internal regulations and university provisions establish criteria for hiring, promotion, evaluation, funding, and participation in academic bodies, which are crucial for understanding the growth of teaching staff.

The normative foundation legitimizes the research before academic and governmental bodies and facilitates its implementation in educational policies. The articles to which this study adhered are indicated below:

Article 3 of the Constitution establishes the compulsory nature of education up to the higher level, and in section X, points out that it corresponds to the State, and in particular to federal and local authorities, to implement policies to promote inclusion, permanence, and continuity (D.O.F./CPEUM, D.O.F.2019).

The Federal Education Law (D.O.F./Ley General de Educación, 2019), establishes in its Article 53, the development of research, science, humanities, technology, and innovation, and in section II the support of the capacity and strengthening of scientific research groups.

The Education Law of the State of Jalisco (P.O./ Ley de Educación del Estado de Jalisco, 1997), mentions in its Article 68 that universities and higher education institutions must perform the following functions: teaching, research, dissemination of culture and environmental protection, and extension and linkage.

The General Statute of the Universidad de Guadalajara, in its Article 126, establishes in the structure of the Academic Secretariat the existence of the Research Coordination, which will be "responsible for the planning, operation, evaluation, and dissemination of scientific research and technological development projects under the responsibility of the University Centers. Thus guaranteeing compliance with the legal aspects demanded by this research.

Substantive Aspects

In Mexico, the task of growth and development of research has fallen mainly on Higher Education Institutions (IES) with the help of both public and private sectors. However, international organizations such as the World Bank or UNESCO report that to date our country spends the equivalent of 0.5% of its GDP on research and development, so this figure is below the global percentage which is 2.3%, since countries like Israel, Korea, or Finland allocate up to 3% of GDP, a figure that seems very distant in Mexico. Adding to this that about 70% of this expenditure comes from public funds (government budget), it is possible to observe that as a country there are still many areas of opportunity. (Villanueva and Amorós, 2019).

Through the analysis of different indicators of production, visibility, impact, collaboration, leadership, and excellence in the period 2008-2018, it was highlighted that Mexico has been the second in Latin America in producing scientific knowledge after Brazil for over 20 years. The Mexican Republic ranks 28th by the volume of scientific production, with more than 25,000 scientific articles annually presenting new results of national and international impact. (Toche, 2019).

Speaking of the evolution of scientific production, Mexico's contribution to global knowledge increased from 0.7 to 1% in five years. This is said to be an encouraging figure; but comparing Mexican growth with that of Brazil, it falls behind, as the South American country produces more than three times as many articles each year, nearly 80,000. Mariusz Janczur, researcher at the Faculty of Sciences of the Universidad Autónoma del Estado de México points out; that in Mexico there is too much centralism in research, referring to the organism that manages research, Consejo Nacional de Ciencia y Tecnología (CONACYT). Conversely, the countries with the best science in the world are those that have a decentralized system like the United States or Great Britain. (Toche, 2019).

Despite its importance, the budget allocated to research and, above all, to academic personnel has decreased in recent years: the freezing of full-time positions and the financial insufficiency to hire temporary staff seriously affect this activity and put the continuity of academic bodies at risk.

On the other hand, the current situation of public universities and in particular the Universidad de Guadalajara (UDG), presents an aging academic staff and professors dedicated to teaching, and consequently a decline in research staff. In addition, there is currently no program for the incorporation of young researchers who have graduated from the doctoral programs of the institution or other national and foreign universities, despite the incorporation of young professors to the ranks of the professorship who are on average between 30 and 35 years old. The challenge posed at UDG is how to replace retired or deceased professors with young researchers who are hired with permanent positions and thus encourage continuity in research projects derived from the lines of research defined by departments and centers or institutes.

For this reason, the Department of Administration of the Universidad de Guadalajara has analyzed, in the period 2012 and 2024, the evolution of research activity based on the survey applied to adjunct and career professors, which details the characteristics of researchers regarding the type of appointment, seniority, lines of research, type and quantity of publications, number of members with PRODEP profile, members of the SNI, membership in academic bodies or research groups, allocated budget, and main problems in carrying out this task.

Method

This study employs a qualitative, descriptive, and non-experimental longitudinal design, aimed at analyzing the characteristics of academic personnel conducting research in the Department of Administration of CUCEA and demonstrating the need to incorporate new research teaching staff given the mobility and attrition of faculty, in order to maintain research lines, continuity of publications, and incorporation into programs like PRODEP and the Sistema Nacional de Investigadores (SNI).

Data collection was carried out in two stages; the first, conducted in 2012, consisted of compiling institutional statistical records of the Department of Administration and applying a structured questionnaire combining open and multiple-choice questions directed at faculty conducting research. This instrument collected information on academic trajectory, research activities, productivity, and institutional participation.

The second stage, carried out in 2024, replicated the comparative design through the analysis of updated reports on departmental activity, public institutional statistics, and the application of a second multiple-choice questionnaire directed at full-time and adjunct research faculty. The longitudinal comparison of both data sets allowed for identifying trends, continuities, and structural changes in the department's research capacity. The study is relevant for educational leadership because it analyzes how the training and consolidation of researchers strengthen the strategic capacity of higher education institutions, understanding research as a key organizational process for academic decision-making (Romero, 2019).

Discussion and Conclusions

The discussion is based on a qualitative, interpretive, and comparative approach, which contrasts the empirical data from the questionnaires applied to full-time and adjunct professors with the documentary analysis of institutional reports and historical statistics of the Department of Administration. This triangulation, of inductive logic, allowed the identification of patterns of evolution, permanence, and transformation in academic management and the research development of the faculty, and reflection on their structural, social, and academic implications.

In 1994 the University Reform began, creating the Network of Thematic Centers in the metropolitan area and Regional Centers in the State of Jalisco, which shaped the departmental structure that today establishes the basic functions of teaching, research, academic management, and tutoring at the Universidad de Guadalajara. Establishing the gender of the academic staff at the Department of Administration at CUCEA is essential to make visible the balanced participation of women and men in teaching and research, which strengthens equity and intellectual diversity in scientific production and constitutes an indicator of quality and institutional responsibility.

PRODEP and SNI Members. Lines of Research. Identifying the research lines of academic personnel is essential to understanding the orientation and coherence of scientific work, as they reflect the institution's priority areas and the capacity of teacher-researchers to generate relevant knowledge with social impact. Furthermore, they allow for the integration of academic bodies and the direction of resources toward highly relevant topics (De la Torre & Pérez, 2018).

According to Garza and Ledezma (2015), an institution that properly manages its research lines achieves greater academic visibility, increases its productivity, and improves its national and international competitiveness.

Main difficulties of academic bodies. Both in 2012 and 2024, the main difficulties pointed out by professors are repetitive and hinder the proper functioning of academic bodies: the lack of communication among their members and the late information about events from the CUCEA research coordination—which usually arrives a few days before and prevents processing resources on time—and the lack of financial support from the Department, due to insufficient resources allocated by the University Center, to participate in national and international congresses and, especially, to conduct fieldwork.

There are weaknesses in achieving effective teamwork, a lack of time to carry out research work, since a good number of members of academic bodies are saturated with teaching classes, tutoring and academic management tasks, or as part of the departmental college, even with special projects of the administration department or other areas of the university center, which prevents them from dedicating a certain number of hours per week to research work.

There is no interdisciplinary work that allows the exchange of experiences of different academic bodies, at least within the department, to strengthen techniques or the use of research tools. The few supports that can be obtained are through the management of some professors who, due to their appointment or recognition, find the procedures easier.

Research products are the result of individual effort and not of the joint work of the academic body; some professors lack the commitment to contribute and advance the work team.

In some cases, professors are unfamiliar with research methodology, so the work is carried out in an improvised manner and detached from the basic guidelines of the research process, which are crucial elements for generating knowledge adhering to scientific rigor and international research standards that allow the center's researchers to expand their projects outside the center itself.

Academic body funding. Research networks are fundamental collaborative structures that articulate researchers, academic bodies, and institutions at national and international levels, strengthening the circulation of knowledge and facilitating access to external funding (De la Cruz and Méndez, 2019).

Funding is a fundamental element for research, as it allows for executing projects, forming networks, sustaining academic mobility, and acquiring supplies and technology. According to Álvarez and Carrillo (2020), access to institutional, state, or federal funds directly affects the productivity and the degree of consolidation of academic bodies.

The origin of operating resources for academic bodies has not changed in the last ten years; in most responses given by researchers, 44% have conducted research with their own resources, 25% with support from the institution CUCEA/UDG, 19% do not require funding to perform it, and 12% through support from national and international organizations, see graph 7.

Research Networks. Research networks are essential for enhancing scientific collaboration, broadening the impact of academic projects, and strengthening researcher training. By integrating diverse academic communities, they promote interdisciplinarity, transversality, and transdisciplinarity, fostering the exchange of knowledge and access to shared resources. Their importance lies in the capacity to generate collective knowledge with social relevance, respond to global challenges, and elevate the quality of scientific production. It is shown how they favor academic mobility, research internationalization, and participation in high-level calls. Networks are strategic pillars for the sustained advancement of university research.

The inclusion of academic bodies in research networks was not considered in the 2012 questionnaire but was incorporated in 2024 due to its value for the evaluation of PROESDE-UDG, PRODEP-SEP, and for admission or renewal in the SNI (CONACYT).

In this regard, professors who responded to the 2024 survey indicated: 56% belonged to a national or international network, and 44% stated they did not belong to any. The ones mentioned were:

  • Red Alianza Pacífico de Investigación

  • Red Iberoamericanas de Estudios del Desarrollo (RIED)

  • Red Internacional de Investigadores en Competitividad (RIICO)

  • Red Regional de Centros Universitarios (UDG)

  • Red Internacional de Investigación en Articulación Productiva en el sector primario (RIIAP)

As can be seen, most researchers participate in international networks, which provides the opportunity to improve the research conditions of the Institution (UDG/CUCEA).

Based on the above, some recommendations are presented to improve the research function in the Department of Administration. To respect the opinions of survey participants, all proposals were considered and grouped into three categories: communication and information, resources, and research.

a) Communication and information.

  • Foster teamwork and achieve collective integration.

  • Share academic body projects to enrich them with information from researchers.

  • Greater interest and formality in research work.

  • Disseminate research seminars among students.

  • Share research experiences and give freedom of participation according to professors' interests.

  • Form work networks among members of the division, the University Center, the University Network, other national and international universities, and public and private organizations.

  • Intra-institutional and inter-institutional communication regarding research.

  • Generation of databases where research information can be shared.

  • Dissemination of scientific products (publications, journals, presentations, among others).

  • Seek to socialize what researchers do and form support groups for this work.

  • Dissemination of options for graduate studies in master's and doctoral programs, both national and foreign.

  • Develop a directory of research and outreach journals and make them known so professors can publish.

b) Resources

  • Distribution of a greater amount of resources for professors, funding for participation in congresses, publications, cubicles, voice recorders, digital cameras.

  • Involve teaching professors who want to dedicate themselves to research as collaborators in academic bodies and research groups.

  • Strengthen the commitment of professors to participate in research projects.

  • The creation of a tutor figure for young researchers who can serve as an orientation and guide.

  • Publication of academic products in collaboration with publishers.

  • Seek external resources to conduct research.

  • Support for social service practitioners for research work.

c) Research

  • Research should be directed toward areas that are relevant and important with a sense of social utility by sector.

  • Monitoring and Evaluation of professors through personal monthly or bimonthly reports on research results.

  • Follow-up on research projects.

  • Approaching local, national, and international researchers to exchange experiences and research results.

  • Awareness program to promote research as a complement to teaching.

  • Implementation of integration workshops where ongoing work and research lines are presented.

  • Support in forming research groups and book editing.

  • Analyze how professors can dedicate more time to research.

  • Strengthen academic bodies and integrate more professors into them.

  • Link research with university outreach, extension, and dissemination.

  • Discharge class hours for research work.

  • Incorporation of SNI member professors with an adjunct professor category to full-time professors.

  • Some additional suggestions for conducting seminars were: schedule them with a certain periodicity and in both shifts; in addition to being linked to academic bodies, convene professors in general; let them serve to guide professors towards research and become a way to train and introduce them to research; based on the results of the seminars, schedule conferences where the progress of research work is made known.

Analysis of Results

Qualitative analysis allows for understanding the evolution of the faculty, from their incorporation to their consolidation as researchers, identifying patterns, trajectories, and obstacles, as well as the motivations and contexts that influence their permanence and their incorporation into programs such as PRODEP and SNI. This consolidation not only reflects individual productivity but the collective impact on academic bodies, consolidated lines, and collaboration networks. The findings of the 2012 and 2024 stages are presented based on the instruments applied and institutional documents.

When conducting the comparative analysis of the surveys applied to the research professors of the Department of Administration of CUCEA, in 2012 and 2024, interesting results were obtained, among them mentioning that the faculty staff from 2012 to 2024 has increased by 68.5%, despite the deaths and retirements of full-time professors, finding that around 75% of the professors are adjunct. Regarding the educational degree, there has been significant progress; professors who had a master's degree for 2012 were only 53, for 2024 there are already 160, having an increase of 302%. Only 25% of professors have a doctoral degree, so considering these figures, it is important that all those teachers with a doctorate have full-time status, and begin to incorporate professors with a master's degree with greater seniority and activity in research support. However, this is not the case; therefore, the vast majority, according to the research surveys applied to professors, are not interested in participating or carrying out any research project up to this point.

It is shown how the number of SNI members has increased considerably, going from 5 to 24 members; however, 50% of the members have an appointment as an adjunct professor, which can complicate their consolidation in the research work of the Department of Administration and its impact on the University Network. On the other hand, regarding professors with a PRODEP profile, they have decreased from 27 to 24 in recent years due to the death of some of them and the retirement of others.

On the other hand, it is possible to appreciate when analyzing the lines of research and those who conduct it, that there is an increase in production and despite the loss of full-time professors, the work carried out falls on the active faculty. However, for the number of professors who have joined, there is no direct relationship between research production and the participation of new professors. Adding that there is a variety of procedures for the entry of new researchers into academic bodies, as well as a lack of financial support, due to budget adjustments in the Department of Administration and the University Center, so only a few professors belonging to academic bodies have the opportunity to participate in national and international congresses since most are paid out of pocket.

An obstacle that prevents carrying out research projects is the lack of time, due to the saturation of teachers in teaching classes, tutoring, and academic management tasks, or even with special projects of the department itself or other areas of the university center, which prevents them from dedicating hours a week to this task.

Most of the researchers in the department of administration conduct their research work with department colleagues, which facilitates communication and continuous progress of the work. However, a third do it individually and only a few exchange with national and international researchers, so this part would have to be reinforced by establishing institutional links with universities in the country and abroad.

Part of the daily work of academic bodies implies the involvement of professors with a PRODEP-SEP profile, and SNI-CONACYT, and doctoral studies and researchers as part of an institutional policy. Therefore, it would be convenient to analyze their operation in detail and make proposals that allow a harmonious development of the professors and therefore of the area to which they belong. In this regard, it is worth mentioning that of the ten academic bodies, the level at which they are found is: seven are in formation, 1 in consolidation, and 2 consolidated. As mentioned previously, it is difficult to change from one status to another due to the scarce production that exists in them.

While it is true that funding is an important aspect for carrying out research work, especially in field projects, in some cases it is necessary to travel nationally and internationally, and it is difficult for researchers to divert personal income for this purpose. Unfortunately, the current situation experienced by the university is a reflection of the permanent decrease in federal and state budgets allocated to this activity, and by not having resources to carry out this work, research projects are adjusted to the economic possibilities of researchers who decide to invest in this area.

An outstanding aspect is the increased participation of researchers and academic bodies in local, national, and international academic networks. This allows the permanent exchange of processes, ideas, and contacts for carrying out specific projects based on the lines of research.

Conclusions

Academic research in the Department of Administration at CUCEA is not a mere complement in the training of teachers and students, but an essential element for the evolution of knowledge, innovation, and the social and economic progress of the state and the country. The comparative analysis of the 2012 and 2024 surveys identified crucial transformations in the development of the faculty, their level of training, and their participation in knowledge generation, revealing opportunities and challenges.

One of the most significant results is the 68.5% increase in staff during the last twelve years; however, this growth is not in direct relation to that of research projects, and about 75% of teachers are adjuncts, so the majority do not have optimal conditions to research continuously. This limits the consolidation of strong groups and reduces scientific production, which falls on the 12% of full-time professors, even though research is an enhancer of proposals and business models for the development of the state and the country.

The training level has improved: teachers with a master's degree have quadrupled, going from 40 in 2012 to 160 in 2024, and 25% have a doctorate. Even so, most academics with graduate degrees do not have a full-time position, which restricts their participation in research and reveals a gap in resource allocation. A notable achievement is the increase of researchers in the SNI, from 5 in 2012 to 24 in 2024, a reflection of the individual effort of teachers; however, 50% of them have an adjunct appointment, which hinders the stability and continuity of research.

Regarding academic bodies, of the 10 existing ones, 7 are in formation, 1 in consolidation, and only 2 are consolidated, which evidences structural difficulties associated with the decrease of full-time professors and the lack of institutional support. The greatest challenge is insufficient funding for academic and field projects: given the progressive reduction of the federal and state budgets, most teachers finance many of their activities with personal resources, which limits production and discourages participation.

The strengthening of research must be an institutional priority backed by policies that guarantee adequate funding. The decrease in teachers with a PRODEP profile, from 27 in 2012 to 24 in 2024, reflects the difficulty in keeping active researchers. The University must promote funding programs, incentives for scientific production, and inter-institutional collaboration schemes.

A positive aspect is the increased participation in national and international academic networks, which allow establishing inter-institutional links, sharing knowledge, and consolidating high-impact research. Research can no longer be seen as a complementary activity: in a globalized and competitive world, knowledge generation is fundamental for economic and social development and for Mexico's scientific positioning.

In this transition moment, CUCEA has the opportunity to become a benchmark in applied research, improving educational quality and training professionals with a critical and proactive vision. Betting on research is not a luxury, but an urgent necessity to boost the university's impact.

Investing in research is betting on a more solid future, where knowledge and innovation are pillars of a more competitive Mexico. The question is not whether research is important, but how and when decisions will be made to strengthen it structurally and sustainably.

In general, the research work of the Department of Administration has maintained continuity for ten years regarding who performs it, despite having fewer and fewer full-time professors and more adjuncts. The panorama is conservative due to difficulties in different areas, so it will be necessary to increase income and resource management at the University Center and Department levels, and review institutional guidelines to privilege this activity. Let us bet on science, promote research, and build a world where knowledge is the most powerful tool to transform realities.
