Abstract.

This study analyzes the relationship between admission indicators and academic performance among undergraduate students enrolled in a Human Resources program at a public Mexican university. Using a quantitative, correlational, and cross-sectional design, the study examines the associations between high school grade point average, scores obtained on the Academic Aptitude Test (PAA), and academic outcomes during the undergraduate program, including indicators of academic trajectory and performance on the General Examination for the Completion of Undergraduate Studies (EGEL). The analysis is based on institutional academic records and standardized examination results from two student cohorts. Pearson correlation analyses and independent-samples t tests were conducted to explore relationships between variables and differences across groups defined by admission calendar and employment status. The results reveal statistically significant associations between admission indicators and academic outcomes at graduation, particularly for verbal components of the PAA, which show consistent relationships with performance across EGEL disciplinary areas. In contrast, the high school grade point average is more strongly associated with indicators of academic regularity throughout the program. Overall, the findings provide empirical evidence relevant for strengthening admission processes, academic diagnosis, and student support strategies in higher education, highlighting the value of using multiple indicators to better understand academic trajectories and outcomes.

Keywords:

Academic performance; admission indicators; academic trajectory; Academic Aptitude Test (PAA); General Examination for Graduation from the Bachelor's Degree (EGEL)

Resumen.

Este estudio analiza la relación entre los indicadores de ingreso y el rendimiento académico en estudiantes de licenciatura inscritos en un programa de Recursos Humanos de una universidad pública mexicana. Mediante un diseño cuantitativo, correlacional y de corte transversal, se examinan las asociaciones entre el promedio de bachillerato, los puntajes obtenidos en la Prueba de Aptitud Académica (PAA) y los resultados académicos durante la formación universitaria, incluyendo indicadores de trayectoria académica y el desempeño en el Examen General para el Egreso de la Licenciatura (EGEL). El análisis se basa en registros académicos institucionales y en resultados de evaluaciones estandarizadas correspondientes a dos cohortes de estudiantes. Se aplicaron análisis de correlación de Pearson y pruebas t para muestras independientes con el fin de explorar las relaciones entre las variables y las diferencias entre grupos definidos por el calendario de ingreso y la condición laboral. Los resultados evidencian asociaciones estadísticamente significativas entre los indicadores de ingreso y el desempeño académico al egreso, particularmente en los componentes verbales de la PAA, los cuales muestran relaciones consistentes con el rendimiento en las distintas áreas disciplinares del EGEL. En contraste, el promedio de bachillerato se asocia de manera más fuerte con los indicadores de regularidad académica a lo largo del programa. En conjunto, los hallazgos aportan evidencia empírica relevante para el fortalecimiento de los procesos de admisión, diagnóstico académico y estrategias de apoyo al estudiantado en la educación superior, destacando el valor de utilizar múltiples indicadores para comprender de manera más integral las trayectorias y los resultados académicos.

Palabras clave:

Introduction

The academic performance of university students constitutes a central concern for higher education institutions, as it is linked to retention, graduation efficiency, and the quality of educational processes (ANUIES, 2019; INEE, 2019). Within this framework, identifying factors associated with academic performance throughout the university trajectory and at graduation is key to strengthening admission, diagnosis, and student guidance mechanisms (ANUIES, 2019).

Several studies have noted that academic indicators prior to entry, such as upper secondary grade point average and scores on standardized aptitude tests, are significantly associated with academic performance during the first university semesters, although the magnitude of these relationships tends to be moderate (González & Velázquez, 2015; Rosales Gracia et al., 2021). Nevertheless, empirical evidence also suggests that the predictive capacity of these indicators diminishes when analyzing long-term outcomes or exit evaluations, raising the need for more integrative analytical approaches (Martínez González et al., 2016; Delgado & Rocha, 2019).

In the Mexican context, the Academic Aptitude Test (PAA) is utilized as one of the main selection and diagnostic instruments across various public and private universities. This test assesses general cognitive skills, particularly in verbal and mathematical domains, which are considered relevant for academic performance in higher education (College Board, 2021). However, various studies have shown that the predictive utility of the PAA varies depending on the type of skill assessed and the disciplinary area, questioning its homogeneous interpretation as a predictor of university academic performance (Bárquez Antillán & Mascareño, 2009; Rosales Gracia et al., 2021; Kuncel & Hezlett, 2007).

On the other hand, the General Examination for Graduation from the Bachelor's Degree (EGEL) constitutes a standardized evaluation designed to measure the attainment of disciplinary competencies at the end of undergraduate studies (CENEVAL, 2023). Analyzing the relationship between admission indicators and performance on the EGEL allows for a broader understanding of academic performance from a longitudinal perspective, connecting initial student conditions, their academic trajectory, and graduation outcomes (Martínez González et al., 2016; Delgado & Rocha, 2019).

Based on this approach, the present study aims to analyze the relationship between admission indicators—specifically PAA scores and high school grade point average—academic trajectory, and performance on the EGEL among university students in the economic-administrative area. The analysis seeks to provide empirical evidence contributing to the debate on the validity and scope of admission instruments, as well as to institutional decision-making aimed at strengthening academic accompaniment processes and improving educational outcomes in higher education (ANUIES, 2019; INEE, 2019).

Problem Statement

In recent decades, higher education institutions have intensified the use of entry academic indicators—such as upper secondary grade point average and standardized aptitude tests—to strengthen selection, diagnostic, and student support processes (ANUIES, 2019; INEE, 2019). However, an open debate persists regarding the actual capacity of these indicators to predict university academic performance, particularly over the long term and at graduation (Delgado & Rocha, 2019; Martínez González et al., 2016).

In Mexico, the Academic Aptitude Test (PAA) has consolidated as one of the most widely used instruments to evaluate general cognitive skills deemed relevant for entry into higher education (College Board, 2021). Although academic aptitude tests have shown significant associations with initial academic performance, available evidence is not conclusive regarding their capacity to explain academic performance throughout the entire university trajectory or their relationship with outcomes obtained in standardized exit examinations, such as the General Examination for Graduation from the Bachelor's Degree (EGEL) (Martínez González et al., 2016; Delgado & Rocha, 2019).

Furthermore, university academic performance cannot be understood solely from entry results. The academic trajectory, understood as the set of patterns regarding curricular progress, academic regularity, and course credit methods throughout the undergraduate program, constitutes a central component in explaining the final outcomes of the educational process (Rosales Gracia et al., 2021; INEE, 2019). In this sense, indicators such as credits earned in regular and extraordinary evaluations, as well as the final graduation GPA, provide relevant information on student continuity and academic achievement (González & Velázquez, 2015; Rosales Gracia et al., 2021).

Despite this, few studies comprehensively analyze the relationship between admission indicators, academic trajectory, and performance at graduation as measured by the General Examination for Graduation from the Bachelor's Degree (EGEL) (Martínez González et al., 2016; Delgado & Rocha, 2019). This lack of longitudinal analyses limits the understanding of mechanisms through which students' initial conditions articulate with educational processes and final learning outcomes (INEE, 2019).

Likewise, contextual factors such as the admission calendar and student employment status have been identified as variables that may affect academic performance by introducing differences in admission profiles, study dedication opportunities, and educational experiences throughout the program (Castaño Vélez et al., 2009; INEE, 2019). However, empirical evidence regarding the impact of these variables on performance at graduation remains heterogeneous and, in many cases, insufficiently systematized (Delgado & Rocha, 2019).

In this scenario, there is a clear need for empirical studies that integrate multiple indicators and allow for a longitudinal analysis of the relationship between entry conditions, academic trajectory, and performance at graduation (Martínez González et al., 2016; Delgado & Rocha, 2019). Addressing this problem is essential not only for assessing the validity and scope of admission instruments but also for generating inputs to guide the design of institutional strategies for academic accompaniment and student performance enhancement in higher education (ANUIES, 2019; INEE, 2019).

State of Knowledge

Research on academic performance in higher education has repeatedly highlighted the relevance of entry academic indicators as initial predictors of university performance. In particular, upper secondary grade point average and standardized aptitude test scores have been widely used to anticipate performance during early training cycles, although the explanatory strength of these variables usually falls within moderate ranges (González & Velázquez, 2015; Rosales Gracia et al., 2021).

Various studies conducted in Mexican universities have documented statistically significant associations between admission GPA and university academic performance, especially in terms of regularity and academic achievement during the first semesters (González & Velázquez, 2015; Rosales Gracia et al., 2021). These findings suggest that prior performance functions as an indicator of academic habits and general competencies that foster more stable and continuous school trajectories (Rosales Gracia et al., 2021). However, literature also cautions that its predictive capacity decreases when analyzing long-term outcomes or more complex indicators of academic performance (Delgado & Rocha, 2019; Martínez González et al., 2016).

In the case of standardized aptitude tests, empirical evidence shows differentiated results depending on the type of skill evaluated. Subtests associated with verbal skills—such as reading comprehension and writing—tend to present more consistent associations with university academic performance compared to mathematical skills, whose relationship with performance tends to be more variable and dependent on the disciplinary field (Bárquez Antillán & Mascareño, n.d.; Rosales Gracia et al., 2021; Kuncel & Hezlett, 2007). These results have led scholars to question the homogeneous interpretation of overall admission test scores and to propose more disaggregated analyses by skill type (Kuncel & Hezlett, 2007).

Regarding performance upon graduation, measured through the General Examination for Graduation from the Bachelor's Degree (EGEL), available studies indicate that results in this evaluation are moderately associated with admission indicators, albeit with significant variations across knowledge areas and student profiles (Martínez González et al., 2016; Delgado & Rocha, 2019). Some works have noted that cumulative performance throughout the degree program, reflected in the graduation GPA and academic trajectory regularity, may play a mediating role between students' initial conditions and outcomes achieved on the EGEL (Martínez González et al., 2016).

Academic trajectory has been conceptualized as a dynamic process that integrates not only academic performance but also institutional and contextual factors that influence curricular progress (INEE, 2019). In this sense, literature has documented that trajectories characterized by a higher proportion of credits approved through regular evaluation and lower reliance on extraordinary evaluations are typically associated with better academic outcomes and a higher probability of completing studies in a timely manner (González & Velázquez, 2015; Rosales Gracia et al., 2021).

Additionally, it has been noted that contextual variables such as student employment status and entry cohort timing can introduce relevant differences in academic experience. Students who combine study and work often face greater time constraints and academic workload, which can affect performance (Castaño Vélez et al., 2009; INEE, 2019); however, some studies suggest that these conditions may also promote organizational skills and autonomy depending on the institutional context, explaining the heterogeneity of reported results in the literature (Delgado & Rocha, 2019).

Taken together, the current state of knowledge highlights the need to advance toward integrative approaches that articulate admission indicators, academic trajectory, and performance at graduation, overcoming fragmented analyses centered on a single point in the educational process (Martínez González et al., 2016; Delgado & Rocha, 2019). From this perspective, it is pertinent to develop studies providing contextualized empirical evidence and contributing to a more comprehensive understanding of academic performance in higher education (ANUIES, 2019; INEE, 2019).

Conceptual Framework

The analysis of academic performance in higher education has been addressed from multiple theoretical perspectives, which agree that university performance is a complex and multidimensional phenomenon. Far from being explained by a single factor, academic performance results from the interaction between student entry conditions, educational processes developed throughout the academic trajectory, and various contextual factors influencing the educational experience.

From this perspective, entry academic indicators—such as upper secondary grade point average and standardized aptitude test scores—have been conceptualized as proxy measures of general cognitive competencies, study habits, and prior academic capital. Admission GPA, in particular, has been interpreted as an indicator of sustained performance in previous school settings, associated with academic discipline, self-regulated learning, and familiarity with evaluative demands. For their part, standardized aptitude tests, such as the Academic Aptitude Test (PAA), aim to measure general cognitive skills under controlled conditions, mainly in verbal and mathematical fields.

Specialized literature has noted that these indicators capture distinct dimensions of prior academic performance, and therefore their explanatory capacity tends to be complementary rather than substitutive. While admission GPA relates to performance regularity over time, aptitude tests help identify specific skills that may affect university learning, particularly during early training cycles.

University academic performance can be analyzed both as a cumulative outcome and as a dynamic process. In this sense, academic trajectory is conceived as the set of patterns regarding curricular advancement, course accreditation regularity, and evaluation types throughout the undergraduate program. Indicators such as credits earned in regular and extraordinary evaluations, as well as final graduation GPA, offer relevant information on student continuity and academic achievement. Trajectories characterized by a higher proportion of regular accreditations are generally associated with more favorable academic outcomes and a higher likelihood of timely graduation.

On the other hand, performance at graduation, measured through standardized evaluations such as the General Examination for Graduation from the Bachelor's Degree (EGEL), constitutes an indicator of disciplinary competency achievement at the end of undergraduate education. From a longitudinal approach, performance on the EGEL can be interpreted as the outcome of the interaction between admission conditions, academic trajectory during the degree, and educational opportunities offered by the institution. In this light, the EGEL reflects not only acquired knowledge but also students' ability to integrate, apply, and transfer learning within standardized evaluation contexts.

Additionally, literature recognizes the influence of contextual factors, such as admission calendar and employment status, on university academic performance. These variables can impact the educational experience by introducing differences in admission profiles, available study time, and how students handle academic demands. Although their impact is neither uniform nor strictly deterministic, including them in the analysis allows for a more comprehensive understanding of academic performance and avoids reductionist interpretations based solely on cognitive indicators.

Overall, this conceptual framework posits that university academic performance must be analyzed from an integrative and longitudinal perspective that connects admission indicators, school trajectory, and performance at graduation. Under this approach, the present study aims to empirically examine these relationships, offering contextualized evidence on factors influencing academic performance in higher education.

General Objective

To analyze the relationship between admission indicators, academic trajectory, and performance upon graduation, as measured by the General Examination for Graduation from the Bachelor's Degree (EGEL), among university students in the economic-administrative field.

Specific Objectives

  • To examine the relationship between high school admission GPA and scores obtained on the Academic Aptitude Test (PAA).

  • To analyze the association between PAA subtest scores and results obtained across EGEL disciplinary areas.

  • To evaluate the relationship between academic trajectory, measured through credits earned in regular and extraordinary evaluations, and performance on the EGEL.

  • To compare academic outcomes at graduation based on the admission calendar and student employment status.

Research Question

What relationship exists between admission indicators, academic trajectory, and performance upon graduation, as measured by the General Examination for Graduation from the Bachelor's Degree (EGEL), among university students in the economic-administrative field?

Study Design

The study was developed under a quantitative approach, using a non-experimental, correlational, comparative, and cross-sectional design. This design is appropriate for analyzing the relationship between admission indicators, academic trajectory, and performance upon graduation using observational data from institutional records and standardized evaluations without variable manipulation.

The analytical purpose of the study was to examine associations between academic variables and contrast differences across groups defined by contextual student characteristics, particularly admission calendar and employment status, in order to identify relevant patterns of university academic performance.

Participants and Context

The study population consisted of 259 students from an undergraduate program in the economic-administrative field belonging to two consecutive entry cohorts. However, the sample size varied depending on the specific analysis conducted, based on the availability and completeness of information for each variable considered.

To ensure methodological consistency, explicit inclusion and exclusion criteria were applied, retaining only cases with complete information for the variables analyzed in each statistical procedure. As a result, general descriptive analyses considered the total available sample (N = 259), while analyses linked to performance on the General Examination for Graduation from the Bachelor's Degree (EGEL) were conducted with the subsample of graduated students who took the exam (N = 119).

This variation in sample sizes reflects the longitudinal nature of the analyzed variables and does not compromise the validity of the results, as each analysis was performed with the methodologically justified number of pertinent cases.

Variables and Instruments

The analyzed variables were grouped into three blocks:

  • Admission indicators, including upper secondary grade point average and scores obtained on the Academic Aptitude Test (PAA), both in overall score and in Reading, Writing, and Mathematics subtests.

  • Academic trajectory indicators, operationalized through credits earned in regular and extraordinary evaluations, as well as final graduation GPA.

  • Performance indicators at graduation, measured through scores obtained in the disciplinary areas of the General Examination for Graduation from the Bachelor's Degree (EGEL).

Additionally, contextual variables such as admission calendar and student employment status were incorporated to explore differences in academic performance associated with these characteristics.

Academic information was obtained from official institutional records, while PAA and EGEL scores originated from standardized databases generated by the organizations responsible for these evaluations. The operational definition of each variable, its measurement scale, and classification criteria are presented in Table 1 and Table 2; the sample size estimation procedure is described below.

To verify the statistical sufficiency of the available population, the minimum sample size for each cohort was estimated using the finite population formula, n = (N · Z² · p · q) / [(N − 1) · e² + Z² · p · q], assuming a 95% confidence level (Z = 1.96), a 5% margin of error (e = 0.05), and an expected proportion p = q = 0.5, which maximizes variance and yields a conservative estimate. For the 2018-A cohort, with an active population of N = 88 students, the minimum required sample size was 66 students; since 79 were included, the theoretical threshold was exceeded, ensuring cohort representativeness. For the 2018-B cohort, with N = 81 active students, the estimated minimum sample size was 69 students, and the analysis was conducted on the entire population (n = 81), operating as a census scheme.

Procedure and Data Processing

Prior to statistical analysis, a data screening process was carried out, which included verifying internal consistency, eliminating duplicate records, and identifying outliers. Missing data were handled through valid case analysis for each statistical procedure. In analyses where the proportion of missing values was minimal and corresponded strictly to continuous variables, simple mean imputation was exceptionally used to preserve estimate stability. In cases with higher missingness, corresponding records were excluded from specific analyses.

Likewise, assumptions of normality and homogeneity of variance were verified to determine the appropriateness of parametric tests. In analyses where violations of these assumptions were identified, corresponding statistical corrections were applied.

Statistical Analysis

Data analysis was conducted using descriptive and inferential statistics. Pearson correlation coefficients were used to examine relationships between continuous variables, while group comparisons were performed using independent-samples t tests.

To enhance result interpretation, statistical significance values were reported alongside effect sizes, including Cohen's d, Hedges' g, and Glass's delta, accompanied by their respective 95% confidence intervals. This approach allows for evaluating the practical magnitude of observed differences and associations beyond statistical significance.

All analyses were conducted using the SPSS statistical software package (version 20), adopting a significance level of p < .05 for result interpretation.

Ethical Considerations

The study complied with ethical principles for research involving academic data. Information was treated confidentially and anonymously, ensuring that results do not allow individual identification of participants. Data were utilized exclusively for research and academic analysis purposes, in accordance with applicable institutional guidelines.

Results

Results are presented according to the study objectives, organized into three sections: (a) admission indicators and performance at graduation, (b) admission indicators and academic trajectory, and (c) contextual factors associated with academic performance. This structure provides a progressive narrative that connects initial student conditions, their university trajectory, and outcomes at graduation.

Admission Indicators and Performance at Graduation (EGEL)

The analysis of the relationship between Academic Aptitude Test (PAA) scores and performance in the disciplinary areas of the General Examination for Graduation from the Bachelor's Degree (EGEL-CENEVAL) revealed differentiated associations depending on the type of evaluated skill.

Verbal subtests of the PAA showed positive and statistically significant associations across all EGEL areas. In particular, the Reading subtest showed moderate correlations with Organizational Management and Quality Management, Marketing Management, and Human Resources Management, whereas its relationship with Financial Management was weaker, though still statistically significant. Similarly, the Writing subtest demonstrated low-to-moderate positive associations across all four evaluated EGEL areas (see Tables 3 and 4).

Discussion

The findings of this study provide empirical evidence regarding the complex relationship between admission indicators, academic trajectory, and performance at graduation in higher education. Consistent with prior literature, the results confirm that entry indicators are significantly associated with university academic performance, although the nature and magnitude of these associations vary depending on the skill type and outcome measure analyzed (González & Velázquez, 2015; Rosales Gracia et al., 2021; Kuncel & Hezlett, 2007).

Particularly noteworthy is the differentiated predictive capacity of PAA subtests. While verbal skills (Reading and Writing) showed consistent, statistically significant positive correlations across all disciplinary areas evaluated by EGEL, mathematical skills displayed limited relationships, being associated only with Human Resources Management. This pattern aligns with studies questioning the uniform interpretation of global admission test scores and highlights the relevance of analyzing verbal comprehension and written communication skills as foundational competencies for learning in social sciences and business disciplines (Bárquez Antillán & Mascareño, 2009; Kuncel & Hezlett, 2007).

Regarding high school grade point average, the results reinforce the view that prior sustained performance serves as a reliable indicator of academic regularity and study habits throughout university education (Rosales Gracia et al., 2021). High school GPA demonstrated stronger ties to trajectory indicators—such as ordinary credits approved—than to standardized exit exam scores. This suggests that while high school GPA reflects student discipline and persistence over time, standardized exit evaluations like EGEL capture specific disciplinary mastery, which is also influenced by cognitive aptitude and academic experiences during undergraduate study (Martínez González et al., 2016; Delgado & Rocha, 2019).

Regarding contextual variables, differences observed between admission calendars (A and B) in student age profiles highlight the importance of considering entry dynamics when designing academic support strategies. Older students entering in calendar A may present distinct profile characteristics, work commitments, or educational gaps that warrant tailored institutional interventions (Castaño Vélez et al., 2009; INEE, 2019).

Conclusions

This study demonstrates that admission indicators, academic trajectory, and graduation performance are closely articulated, supporting the need for an integrative, longitudinal approach to student performance evaluation in higher education. The main conclusions derived from the study are as follows:

  • Verbal skills measured at entry (Reading and Writing on the PAA) serve as consistent predictors of performance across disciplinary areas in business administration exit examinations (EGEL), underscoring the vital role of reading literacy and communication skills in higher education success.

  • High school grade point average is a strong indicator of academic trajectory stability and course completion regularity throughout the program, reflecting long-term academic habits.

  • A single entry score or global index is insufficient to capture the multidimensional nature of student academic potential; institutions should utilize disaggregated subtest scores and trajectory metrics for diagnostic and guidance purposes.

  • Contextual factors, such as entry calendar age variations and student work status, generate distinct student profiles that higher education institutions must address through flexible tutoring, academic monitoring, and remedial support programs.

In summary, strengthening admission processes and academic support mechanisms requires moving beyond simple selection criteria toward comprehensive diagnostic frameworks that utilize entry and trajectory data to promote student retention, academic success, and high-quality graduation outcomes.
