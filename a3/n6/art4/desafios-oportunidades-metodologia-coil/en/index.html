Challenges and opportunities of the COIL methodology in Higher Education: Technology and Pedagogy

Desafíos y oportunidades de la metodología COIL en la Educación Superior: Tecnología y Pedagogía

Abstract.

The present research work addresses the inherent duality in the implementation of the methodology known as Collaborative Online International Learning (COIL) in Higher Education, as well as identifying the challenges and opportunities that arise for its proper functioning within its ecosystem through educational innovation. This analysis focuses specifically on the critical interaction between technology and pedagogy throughout the entire development of the COIL cycle, from planning to closing. The main opportunity lies in the capacity to achieve an effective and scalable internationalization at home through the use and integration of technology, allowing students from different cultures to collaborate on real projects, thus developing essential global and intercultural competencies that are accessible to all without the high costs demanded by physical mobility. Furthermore, from a pedagogical perspective, COIL allows faculty to design active learning experiences that foster critical thinking and collaborative problem-solving at a distance.

Likewise, some challenges arise in the COIL ecosystem, with technological issues being the most prevalent, exposing problems such as disparity in platform access, the digital divide between partner institutions, the management of disparate time zones for synchronous interactions, and the need to ensure cybersecurity and data privacy. At the pedagogical level, the challenges are even more complex: including the need to harmonize curricula and teaching styles between different academic cultures, mitigate conflicts arising from communication differences, and establish fair assessment mechanisms that measure both individual and group learning in transnational collaborative environments. This research explores successful technological-pedagogical integration strategies, such as the adoption of online Universal Design for Learning (UDL) models and the use of asynchronous tools to promote flexibility. It concludes with a series of practical recommendations aimed at administrators and faculty. The present article is supported by a qualitative, documentary approach, which was carried out through the review of specialized literature and national and international academic sources. This analysis allows for a deep reflection on faculty training in COIL design, and offers a vision for the standardization of minimum technological protocols and the creation of institutional support frameworks that transform initial challenges into catalytic elements for a sustainable and global educational innovation in the 21st century.

Resumen.

El presente trabajo de investigación aborda la dualidad inherente a la implementación de la metodología denominada Collaborative Online International Learning (COIL) en la Educación Superior, así como identificar los desafíos y oportunidades que se presentan para el buen funcionamiento en su ecosistema mediante la innovación educativa, este análisis se centra específicamente en la interacción crítica entre la tecnología y la pedagogía a lo largo de todo el desarrollo del ciclo COIL, desde la planeación hasta el cierre. La oportunidad principal radica en la capacidad para lograr una internacionalización en casa efectiva y escalable mediante el uso y la integración de la tecnología, permitiendo a estudiantes de distintas culturas colaborar en proyectos reales, desarrollando competencias globales e interculturales esenciales y que sean accesibles para todos sin los altos costos que la movilidad física exige, aunado a esto y desde una perspectiva pedagógica, COIL permite al profesorado diseñar experiencias de aprendizaje activo que fomentan el pensamiento crítico así como la resolución colaborativa de problemas a distancia, así mismo, en el ecosistema COIL se presentan algunos desafíos, siendo el tecnológico el que más se presenta, exponiendo problemas como la disparidad de acceso a plataformas, la brecha digital entre instituciones asociadas, la gestión de zonas horarias dispares para las interacciones sincrónicas y la necesidad de asegurar la ciberseguridad y la privacidad de los datos. A nivel pedagógico, los retos son aún más complejos: incluyendo la necesidad de armonizar planes de estudio y estilos de enseñanza entre diferentes culturas académicas, mitigar los conflictos derivados de las diferencias comunicacionales y establecer mecanismos de evaluación justos que midan el aprendizaje individual y grupal en entornos colaborativos transnacionales. Esta investigación explora estrategias exitosas de integración tecnológica-pedagógica, tales como la adopción de modelos de Diseño Universal para el Aprendizaje (DUA) en línea y el uso de herramientas asíncronas para promover la flexibilidad. concluyendo con una serie de recomendaciones prácticas dirigidas a directivos y docentes. El presente artículo es fundamentado por medio de un enfoque cualitativo de tipo documental, mismo que fue realizado mediante la revisión de literatura especializada y fuentes académicas nacionales e internacionales. Este análisis permite realizar una reflexión profunda en la formación docente en el diseño COIL, y ofrece una visión para la estandarización de protocolos tecnológicos mínimos y la creación de marcos de apoyo institucionales que transformen los desafíos iniciales en elementos catalizadores para una innovación educativa sostenible y global en el siglo XXI.

Introduction

The Internationalization of Higher Education is an imperative of the 21st century, which in turn has generated the need to train citizens who are increasingly better prepared regarding their global competencies. In the review of specialized literature concerning globalization and business competitiveness, which obliges the student body to be increasingly qualified, it allows us to conclude that the substantive challenges and transformations occurring at different levels of the social system, including the educational realm, present a diversity of factors as key drivers (León and Madera, 2016). The importance of internationalization lies in its potential to foster in students the development of soft skills and intercultural competencies, such as better understanding the world and its interdependencies (Ramírez and Bustos-Aguirre, 2022).

Historically, this goal has been mainly associated with student and faculty mobility—an option that, while valuable, turns out to be costly and exclusionary for a vast majority of the university population due to the high costs involved in changing residence, whether nationally or internationally.

Following the health emergency caused by the SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19) virus in 2020, more effective strategies based on deeper academic collaboration have been implemented, such as the COIL methodology, which is among the most widely used in Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) in Mexico. The health emergency modified how technology was used in the classroom, transforming it into a fundamental component of education (Arriaga and Lara, 2023).

International student mobility experienced a strong negative impact due to the aforementioned pandemic, considering that the percentage of students participating in mobility programs does not exceed 1% of total enrollment in Mexico (Bustos-Aguirre et al., 2022). This percentage was directly affected as the sudden confinement caused by the pandemic abruptly forced both faculty and students to carry out daily activities from home (Galbán et al., 2022).

Theoretical Framework

COIL is defined as a pedagogical approach that connects students and professors from different countries to work together on projects that integrate collaboration, technology use, and intercultural reflection within their regular courses (Ramírez and Bustos-Aguirre, 2022). This not only helps strengthen soft and disciplinary skills but also supports a better understanding of digital literacy and the development of enhanced intercultural competencies among students. Furthermore, the COIL project utilizes competency-based learning (Argudin, 2006). This methodology, applied for the benefit of students, has highlighted many positive aspects, since through this tool, students can experience an internationalized educational experience complementary to the in-person nature of their curriculum (Quintana-Ordorika et al., 2022).

Given this conceptualization, it can be confirmed that the COIL methodology is an innovative teaching methodology that enables participating students to improve their professional perspective, allowing them to focus on a currently internationalized and global labor market (Crawford et al., 2020; Jung et al., 2022), which in turn has generated greater participation in internationalization from home.

The COIL methodology has been supported by the following learning models for its proper operation and application, according to Robles et al. (2023):

Table 1.

Learning models and support in the COIL methodology

Learning Model Main Focus and Learning Mechanism
Behaviorist Model Focuses on conditioning and direct response; learning is achieved through constant repetition and the application of stimuli or reinforcements.
Cognitive Model Explores the structure of thought; focuses on how students process, organize, and retain information, prioritizing deep understanding and the development of mental skills.
Constructivist Model Promotes active and self-directed learning. Students are protagonists who actively construct their own knowledge through guided exploration and real-world problem-solving.
Sociocultural Model Highlights the influence of social and cultural context. Knowledge is acquired and developed primarily through social interaction and collaboration within a shared environment.
COIL Methodology Based on online international collaboration between students and faculty from different cultures. Its goal is to boost interaction, foster intercultural understanding, and optimize virtual teamwork and communication skills.

Source: Based on Robles et al., 2023

Table 1 outlines the learning models that have supported the COIL methodology. This has served as a bridge for innovation in the transmission of knowledge between faculty and students in internationalization through this educational model for implementation across various universities worldwide.

The rise in the application of the COIL methodology has transformed the way the educational process is improved throughout its ecosystem, allowing faculty to develop and design new ways to transmit active learning that stimulate and foster critical thinking and the use of tools that facilitate remote problem-solving. The success of this methodology, however, lies in a delicate and critical balance: the interaction between technology and pedagogy among students and faculty throughout its complete cycle, from the planning and collaboration phase to its final evaluation.

Challenges Facing the COIL Ecosystem

Despite its promises of innovation and equity, the COIL ecosystem is not exempt from complexities, generating a duality that requires deeper analysis. The challenges facing the use of this methodology are notable and include the persistent digital divide and technological inequality observed in regions such as Latin America and South America. Additionally, managing time zone diversity, data security, and disparity in institutional platforms impose significant barriers to effective synchronous collaboration.

Table 2 presents the main obstacles that must be addressed to ensure the successful and equitable implementation of the COIL methodology in Higher Education.

Table 2

Main Category Specific Challenge Detail and Critical Impact
I. TECHNOLOGICAL (Infrastructure and Access)

1. Digital Divide and Disparity in Access

2. Platform and Tool Issues

3. Synchronicity Management

4. Data Security and Privacy

1. Unequal access to high-speed internet, reliable devices, and differences in digital skills management among participants.

2. Incompatibility or lack of standardization among institutional Learning Management Systems (LMS), requiring licenses or software that are not universally available.

3. Time zone differences that complicate the coordination of mandatory synchronous sessions and limit live interaction.

4. Need to ensure IT security and compliance with data protection laws, such as GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation), when sharing confidential information between different countries.
II. PEDAGOGICAL (Design and Assessment)

1. Curricular Harmonization

2. Management of Cultural Diversity

3. Design of Inclusive Experiences

4. Challenges in Assessment

1. Difficulty in aligning learning objectives, content, academic workload, and course development timelines between academic programs in different countries.

2. Mitigation of conflicts and misunderstandings arising from differences in communication styles, social norms, and the need to actively promote intercultural competence.

3. Ensuring activities are accessible and equitable, requiring the application of Universal Design for Learning (UDL) principles in virtual environments.

4. Establishing fair and transparent assessment mechanisms that effectively measure both individual (disciplinary) learning and collaborative contributions within the transnational group.
III. INSTITUTIONAL (Support and Sustainability)

1. Lack of Institutional Support and Recognition

2. Specialized Teacher Training

3. Sustainability and Scalability

1. Lack of institutional support frameworks (financial or legal) and absence of incentives or teaching load recognition for faculty facilitating COIL.

2. Critical need for specific training in COIL pedagogy (intercultural design, remote facilitation) and in the advanced use of technological tools.

3. Difficulty in maintaining long-term partnerships and scaling the model beyond pilot projects, demanding the standardization of minimum quality protocols.

As shown in Table 2, the main obstacles at the technological level outline situations that became prominent after the 2020 pandemic. Technology adoption generated resistance to change, turning uncertainty into one of the main rejection factors for faculty, particularly affecting individuals who struggle to cope with uncertain situations (Caruth and Caruth, 2013).

In this sense, at the pedagogical level, challenges focus on the complexity of harmonizing curricula, teaching styles, and, crucially, establishing assessment mechanisms that are fair and accurately measure individual learning in transnational contexts (Ramírez and Bustos-Aguirre, 2022), with technological inclusion for students being an essential part of the teaching-learning process.

One of the challenges facing HEIs is undoubtedly support for technology use, as well as the integration of specialized faculty training for the proper functioning of the COIL methodology.

Main Opportunities and Benefits of Using the COIL Methodology

The application of the COIL model in higher education represents a catalyst for change, transforming academic processes in the face of a globalized world. Its main advantages or benefits can be grouped into three key areas: equitable cultural internationalization, the development of essential competencies, and pedagogical innovation.

Equitable cultural internationalization enables harmony and respect across diverse cultures. Thus, the COIL project allows for the sharing of interculturality (UNESCO, 2023). Cultural diversity in the use of the COIL methodology offers a window of opportunity for collaborative learning, but above all, it transforms into an opportunity to enrich the personal learning environment, acquire knowledge about other cultures, and develop cultural competencies (Gallego et al., 2023).

The most significant advantage of using the COIL methodology is the capacity to achieve effective and scalable internationalization at home. This democratizes the global experience, making it accessible to a majority of students who, for economic or logistical reasons, cannot participate in physical mobility programs (Succi Junior, 2025). By eliminating high travel costs, COIL ensures that intercultural exposure becomes a fundamental and inclusive educational experience (UCLM, 2025).

The accelerated acquisition and development of essential 21st-century competencies establishes the COIL methodology as an effective vehicle for developing critical skills for the contemporary labor market, including:

Intercultural Competence: By collaborating with peers from other cultures, students are compelled to manage communication differences and develop the empathy and understanding necessary to work in global teams (Ramírez and Bustos-Aguirre, 2022).

Digital Skills (or Digital Literacy): Implementation requires students to master multiple online collaboration tools (asynchronous and synchronous), actively strengthening their digital competencies for their professional future (Universidad Veracruzana, 2022).

Based on studies conducted on the use of the COIL methodology, certain benefits can be encapsulated, such as internationalization at home, which democratizes the global experience, allowing all students to gain an international perspective without incurring the high costs and barriers of physical mobility (Succi Junior, 2025; UCLM, 2025). Likewise, Flexibility and Scalability establish parameters that allow institutions to partner quickly and scale international course offerings to a larger number of students (Ramírez and Bustos-Aguirre, 2022).

The development of global and intercultural competencies has been an essential part of student growth, generating cultural diversity and providing opportunities for growth in acquiring new and better skills focused on an international labor market that increasingly demands new digital and interpersonal tools. Strengthening these skills boosts the handling and mastery of collaborative tools and virtual platforms, enhancing the digital literacy of students and faculty (Universidad Veracruzana, 2022).

Pedagogical innovation and academic quality drive improvements in active learning design, promoting student-centered pedagogies that strengthen critical thinking and problem-solving (Mohamed-Amar et al., 2023). Coupled with technology use and participation between faculty and students, collaborative learning is an active learning method that leads students to develop new ideas and knowledge through the collective construction of shared knowledge, promoting personal, interpersonal, and social competencies (EIA, 2020).

Regarding Professional Faculty Development, using this methodology offers a new window of opportunity for knowledge transmission and acquisition through interaction with other international educational systems. This interaction fosters transnational collaboration among faculties, resulting in new lines of research and the co-creation of innovative teaching materials (Ramírez and Bustos-Aguirre, 2022).

Method

This research work addresses this duality through a qualitative documentary approach, which provides depth of data and interpretative richness in research processes (Hernández et al., 2014), based on the review of specialized literature and recent academic sources. Specifically, we explore how the strategic integration of Universal Design for Learning (UDL) principles and inclusive technological tools can mitigate these obstacles, promoting a more equitable and flexible COIL experience (Condori Mamani et al., 2024; Jiménez Villacis, 2024).

The objective is to offer deep reflection on the need for specialized teacher training and the creation of institutional support frameworks. The article is structured to guide the academic community through the four essential phases of COIL: Design and Planning, Implementation, Assessment, and Trends and Recommendations, in order to transform initial challenges into catalytic elements for sustainable and global educational innovation in the 21st century.

Results

Through a documentary review of the main positive and negative aspects of applying the COIL methodology in the international educational ecosystem, this research demonstrates that key challenges lie in technological factors—including the digital divide and access disparity, platform and tool usage issues, synchronicity management, and data security/privacy—pedagogical factors—including curricular harmonization, management of cultural diversity, inclusive experience design, and assessment challenges—and institutional factors—including lack of institutional support/recognition, specialized faculty training, and sustainability/scalability in standardizing protocols. Addressing and supporting these limitations allows students to immerse themselves in different cultures internationally, fostering growth, socialization, and communication.

This study aims to provide a vision focused on growing internationalization in higher education through COIL implementation, generating diversity in university action plans for the benefit of the educational ecosystem from an international perspective, overcoming student mobility limitations, and producing solutions for COIL's relevance and usage.

Regarding opportunities and benefits, positive aspects motivating its application include equitable cultural internationalization, accelerated acquisition and development of essential competencies (such as intercultural competence and digital skills), and faculty pedagogical innovation, which offers improved knowledge acquisition for professors and direct collaboration with international universities, impacting curriculum improvement, among other aspects.

Discussion

This study adopts a qualitative documentary approach to establish the challenges and opportunities provided by implementing the COIL methodology. Analyzing specialized literature and national/international experts offers a perspective on pros and cons, driving the need for better interfaces to create synergy among universities worldwide. Thus, it can be confirmed that the COIL methodology is an innovative teaching methodology enabling students to improve their professional outlook toward an internationalized and global job market (Crawford et al., 2020; Jung et al., 2022). Defining strategies and setting objectives becomes necessary for HEIs to foster student mobility, prompting decision-making to develop feedback and monitoring proposals in the COIL ecosystem and establish better internal policies benefiting internationalization. Research lines must progressively focus on innovative projects utilizing technology in internationalization to create more inclusive teaching processes adapted to new realities (Arriaga et al., 2022).

Conclusions

Internationalization is a fundamental part of academia, offering students the opportunity to acquire knowledge and experience through exposure to different cultures. The COIL methodology not only provides alternative knowledge acquisition but also offers cost savings compared to physical international mobility. Through this documentary analysis, benefits and challenges have been identified, confirming that COIL effectively supports new digital tools and intercultural development globally. Further in-depth studies are needed to improve conditions across universities internationally and increase student mobility. Identifying these factors is key to advancing from local to global perspectives in higher education, highlighting the necessity of all stakeholders' participation. While challenges like the digital divide exist—especially in Latin America where some universities lack resources—progress is ongoing, and student mobility through HEI internationalization is expected to increase for the benefit of education.

Integrating new teaching and academic practices requires direct action in internationalization to foster educational advancement. The 21st century's digital era mandates implementing new techniques to solve everyday challenges in higher education, breaking traditional barriers to welcome innovative learning methodologies.
