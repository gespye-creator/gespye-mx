Abstract.

A sustainable management model is proposed for B Corp ventures in the service sector, integrating principles of innovation, digitization, profitability, financing, and shared value. A systematic literature review was used as methodological design. The search was conducted using Google Scholar with a search string constructed with Boolean operators and truncators, yielding 477 initial results. After applying inclusion and exclusion criteria, the final sample consisted of twenty-one articles. The information was organized in such a way as to identify conceptual patterns and management axes relevant to the proposed model. The conclusions highlight the need to strengthen coordination between levels (strategic, executive, and operational), promote innovation, digitization, and access to financing, and consolidate a triple-impact business vision aligned with sustainability in the social, environmental, and economic dimensions. The proposed model contributes to more coherent, sustainable decisions oriented toward collective impact.

Keywords:

Sustainable management model; corporate sustainability; organizational strategy; business innovation; organizational performance.

Resumen.

Se propone un modelo de gestión sostenible para emprendimientos B Corp del sector servicios, que integra principios de innovación, digitalización, rentabilidad, financiamiento y valor compartido. Como diseño metodológico se utilizó una revisión sistemática de literatura. La búsqueda fue con Google Académico mediante una cadena de búsqueda construida con operadores booleanos y truncadores, se obtuvieron 477 resultados iniciales. Tras aplicar criterios de inclusión y exclusión, la muestra final fue de 21 artículos. La información se organizó de tal forma que permitió identificar patrones conceptuales y ejes de gestión relevantes para el modelo propuesto. Las conclusiones evidencian la necesidad de fortalecer la articulación entre los niveles (estratégico, ejecutivo y operativo), promover la innovación, digitalización, acceso al financiamiento y consolidar una visión empresarial de triple impacto alineado con la sostenibilidad en la dimensión social, ambiental y económica. El modelo planteado contribuye a decisiones más coherentes, sostenibles y orientadas al impacto colectivo.

Introduction

The participation of ventures in the service sector exceeds that of other key sectors such as commerce, agriculture, manufacturing industries, construction, and mining, indicating a remarkable concentration of entrepreneurial productivity in that field (Coba, 2019). This sector encompasses a wide range of activities such as construction, healthcare, education, transportation, basic service supplies, financial, and professional services, among others (Paredes et al., 2019; Paolini & Odriozola, 2019; Indio & Soriano, 2021). However, as Salgado (2023) identifies, there are many ventures in this sector that are not sustained over time due to limitations in managing four fundamental axes: profitability; financing; innovation; and digital marketing.

Specifically, it is the absence of a management model that integrates financial, advertising, and productivity processes that contributes to these businesses having a short market presence (Loor et al., 2024). Statistics reflect this problem, with 40% of entrepreneurs closing due to lack of profitability, 22% due to insufficient financing, and 20% facing structural problems (Orozco, 2024). These data highlight the need for a holistic and strategic approach in managing such ventures to improve their chances of success.

Additionally, evidence indicates that entrepreneurs do not apply a systematic regimen that integrates productive and administrative elements and activities. This synergy is essential to structure, understand, and represent organizational reality in a simplified manner (Rojas & Chávez, 2024; Meyli et al., 2023). In this context, Julio (2020) proposes three management levels for proper administration: executive, operational, and strategic.

Based on the above, it is suggested to recognize the relevance of organizational management, but also of sustainability criteria to ensure market permanence for ventures. For this purpose, the structure proposed by B Corps is considered, which, unlike traditional companies, not only seek economic success, but also seek to generate a positive impact on society and the environment (Fierro, 2023).

Providing ventures in the service area with a proposed sustainable management model contributes to promoting their long-term permanence, favoring internal processes and providing valuable knowledge in administration, as well as in governance, labor capital, community, customers, and the environment.

Thus, the objective of this article is to propose the design of a sustainable management model for ventures based on the B Corp structure for the service sector, allowing the interrelation of fundamental activities within a venture across three levels (hierarchical, administrative, and operational), taking as reference the processes of innovation, profitability, financing, and digital marketing.

Theoretical Framework

Entrepreneurship is traditionally conceived as the process of creating and developing a business with the objective of offering innovative and value-generating solutions, assuming risks, and leading processes in a competitive environment (Duarte, 2007). Lederman et al. (2015) point out that in Latin America, entrepreneurship is often associated with business creation without necessarily considering innovation, and they identify four main factors influencing this argument: low market competition, the human capital gap, credit access constraints, and the regulatory environment.

According to the Global Entrepreneurship Monitor (2024), entrepreneurship plays a decisive role in the economic development and growth of any society. It allows for economic diversification and reduces reliance on sectors such as non-renewable natural resource extraction by encouraging the creation of new companies based on innovation, knowledge, and sustainability. Furthermore, it fosters job creation, competitiveness, and economic resilience by promoting local solutions to social and environmental challenges, thus contributing to more inclusive and sustainable development. It is through this process that entrepreneurial activity is characteristic of individuals creating businesses to address economic and unemployment challenges (Salgado, 2023).

However, entrepreneurs face a series of challenges and barriers that can hinder the success of their initiatives. One of the main obstacles is the lack of access to financing due to a restrictive financial environment, especially if the entrepreneur is in an early stage; bureaucratic barriers arising from complicated legal frameworks; capacity barriers such as scarce entrepreneurship education; market barriers due to limited market research or difficulty finding new products; business management barriers translating into lack of planning; and personal barriers such as fear of failure (Andrade, 2012; Awatara, 2018; Robichaud et al., 2024).

On the other hand, B Corps are a model applicable to any type of organization, whether a company, micro-enterprise, or startup, in any activity or region of the world (Álvarez et al., 2021). They seek to achieve social and environmental goals while generating profitability, attaining a positive triple impact: economic, social, and environmental (Stubbs, 2017). Furthermore, they align with ESG (Environmental, Social, and Governance) criteria, which are used to evaluate an organization's sustainability and ethical impact.

Environmental criteria examine how a company manages its impact on the environment, including waste management, greenhouse gas emissions, and natural resource usage; social criteria focus on company relationships with employees, suppliers, customers, and communities, covering topics such as working conditions, diversity and inclusion, human rights, and social impact; and governance criteria analyze company leadership and management, evaluating board structure, business ethics, transparency, and anti-corruption policies (De Souza Barbosa et al., 2023).

These criteria provide a comprehensive view of a company's performance and sustainability beyond its financial results (Chen et al., 2023; Correa & Vásquez, 2020; Morales, Santiesteban & Monzón, 2019). Moreover, regarding B Corps, each country's legislation establishes additional requirements to regulate aspects such as mandatory reporting and transparency to guarantee that companies fulfill their social, environmental, and corporate governance objectives. Integrating these elements ensures that B Corps not only pursue economic goals, but also contribute significantly to sustainable development and the creation of value for society and the environment.

In Latin America, B Corps have gained great importance and have been integrated into the legal framework of several countries, such as Ecuador, Peru, Colombia, Brazil, Argentina, Chile, and Uruguay (Fierro, 2023), drawing on the U.S. benefit corporation model (Moscoso et al., 2023). Based on this argument, three components are replicated in Latin American organizations: transparency and mandatory reporting regimes; a purpose of environmental and social benefit alongside standard core business operations; and variations in director liability regimes (Connolly et al., 2020).

An organizational management model is defined as a set of principles, practices, and processes designed to structure and guide business activities and decisions in order to achieve established goals (Huertas et al., 2020). This model acts as a guiding conceptual framework to plan, coordinate, and control organizational resources and efforts. Additionally, it ensures that business areas such as strategic planning, organizational structure, control systems, and operational processes work coherently toward common goals. Within these lie digital marketing, innovation, profitability, financing, and organizational strategy. Together, the model provides a basis for evaluating performance and adapting to the environment through innovation and continuous improvement; essentially, it creates a structured environment that fosters efficiency, effectiveness, and innovation within the business (Cañar & Hidalgo, 2021).

In a stricter sense, Julio (2020) proposes that organizational management is the dynamic through which operational or production processes can be managed and administered, arranged across three levels: operational level, executive level, and strategic level.

The operational level focuses on executing daily tasks and managing the organization's immediate resources. Here, operational processes are carried out where labor is concentrated and previously defined strategies are applied. Employees at this level perform specific, repetitive tasks using administrative tools and information technologies to ensure productivity effectiveness. These processes are essential to maintain continuity and stability in daily business operations, ensuring activities align with established standards and targets are reached (Ronda, 2004).

At the executive level, focus shifts to coordinating and supervising daily operations and implementing strategic decisions formulated by top management (strategic level). Executive managers carry responsibility for planning and organized resource allocation necessary to reach strategic goals. This level acts as a bridge between organizational strategy and operational activities, ensuring established directives translate into concrete and efficient actions. Decision-making processes at this level involve evaluating operational performance, adjusting strategies as needed, and ensuring optimal resource utilization to achieve planned outcomes (Cedeño et al., 2019).

The strategic level is the highest in the organizational hierarchy and focuses on formulating long-term policies and goals for the company. Executives and senior leaders such as presidents, general managers, CEOs, and board members develop the organization's purpose, mission, vision, overarching strategies, and objectives. Decision-making at this level includes analyzing the competitive environment, identifying opportunities and threats, and defining strategic objectives to guide business direction. The organizational structure at this level defines authority delegation and coordination across strategic areas, ensuring the enterprise can adapt to market changes and effectively achieve long-term goals (García et al., 2017).

Regarding the previously defined axes, profitability is a key indicator in evaluating an organization's economic performance, as it identifies the capacity to generate profits relative to capital invested by owners or shareholders (Morillo, 2001). This concept is based on the relationship between net profit obtained and equity, allowing investors to analyze efficiency in using financial resources. Profitability is not merely a tool to measure purely economic success in organizational strategy, but also provides a foundation for informed decision-making on investments, financing, and dividend distribution (Contreras, 2006; Martín, 2024).

Financing refers to the process of providing economic funds or capital to an organization, project, or venture to cover expenses, investments, or operational activities. Various financing sources exist, which can be internal (such as retained earnings) or external (such as bank loans, stock issuances, or bonds) (León & Schreiner, 1998). Financing indicators offer managers, investors, and analysts a clear perspective to evaluate solvency, liquidity, and capital utilization efficiency, facilitating informed decisions regarding operations and financing (Alay, 2020).

Innovation allows companies to adapt to a constantly changing competitive environment and maintain market relevance. Innovation refers to introducing new products, services, processes, or business models that create value, whereas continuous improvement involves constant effort to optimize and refine existing processes to increase efficiency and quality (Carrasco, Peiró & Segarra, 2012). Schumpeter's innovation lifecycle theory highlights how disruptive innovations can transform industries and create new market opportunities (Montoya, 2004).

Finally, digital marketing is defined as the set of strategies, techniques, and methodologies utilizing digital media to promote products and services, interact with customers, and build long-term business relationships. Its evolution has been marked by technological advancement and shifting consumer behavior (Bricio, Calle & Zambrano, 2018); digital metrics and analytics are essential for evaluating marketing campaign performance and understanding customer behavior.

Through web analytics tools such as Google Analytics, organizations can measure key performance indicators (KPIs) like site traffic, conversion rates, etc. These metrics allow specialists to identify effective strategies and those requiring reformulation. Furthermore, real-time data analysis enables organizations to make informed decisions and continuously optimize campaigns, thereby enhancing their ability to achieve commercial goals (Pitre, Builes & Hernández, 2021).

Methodology

The research is qualitative, employing a systematic literature review (SLR) as its methodological design—understood as an observational, retrospective design synthesizing methodologies, results, and conclusions from primary studies (Beltrán, 2005; Manterola et al., 2013). De León-Casillas et al. (2020) specify that this review must follow a transparent, pre-established method encompassing core activities: identifying and searching articles, evaluating evidence quality, and synthesizing findings. Each stage of the process is described below.

The first activity consisted of defining core concepts underpinning the study's theoretical framework: entrepreneurship, service industry, Benefit and Collective Interest Corporations (BIC), and organizational management model. These concepts articulate the conceptual baseline for constructing the search string and selection criteria. Google Scholar was used as a search engine to access various scientific journal databases. The search string was built with Boolean operators (AND) and truncators (*), incorporating central descriptors: [(emprendimiento) "B Corp" (servicios) ("modelo de gestión")]. This strategy yielded 477 initial results. The timeframe was bounded between 2020 and 2024. The lower limit is justified as 2020 was the year Ecuador enacted the Organic Law on Entrepreneurship and Innovation, introducing Benefit and Collective Interest Corporations into the national legal framework for the first time.

To ensure relevance and quality of the analyzed corpus, inclusion and exclusion criteria were established. Inclusion criteria required articles to be peer-reviewed scientific papers in business and administration journals, published between 2020 and 2024, addressing at least one thematic axis: entrepreneurship, profitability, financing, innovation, digital marketing, management model, and/or B Corp / BIC entities.

Exclusion criteria discarded non-journal document types (books, theses, news articles, conference proceedings, websites, blogs, academia.edu papers, research reports, government reports); studies focused outside administration/business (e.g., natural sciences, health, engineering); papers outside 2020–2024; duplicates; paywalled/inaccessible full texts; and articles adding no substantive insight upon full reading.

A preliminary screening of 477 initial results reviewed titles, abstracts, keywords, and fields of study, yielding 47 potentially eligible articles. These 47 articles were read in full by both authors, resulting in a final sample of 21 scientific articles organized around defined axes following Salgado (2023) and Loor et al. (2024).

The 21 selected articles were synthesized into a matrix detailing publication year, title, keywords, core idea, reference, and citation (Annex 1). Content analysis methodology (Fernández, 2002) was applied to systematically categorize findings into defined management axes: profitability, financing, innovation, digital marketing, and organizational strategy, with collective benefit and interest as a transversal axis.

With the gathered information, the proposed management model for B Corp service ventures was drafted, structured across selected axes and hierarchical levels: strategic, executive, and operational.

This proposal integrates key aspects of an organizational management model—such as hierarchies, processes, and activities—while drawing on key insights from analyzed studies regarding defined action axes: profitability, financing, innovation, digital marketing, and organizational strategy, underpinned by a transversal focus on benefit and collective interest.

The model is structured across three management levels for each axis: strategic, executive, and operational. Each axis features clearly defined responsibilities and activities for each management level to ensure alignment with general venture objectives. Furthermore, collective benefit and interest serves as a core transversal element across the strategic level.

The proposed organizational management model for B Corp service ventures offers an integrative approach where business decisions respond equally to economic sustainability and social/environmental commitments. It articulates four primary management axes interacting continuously with stakeholders.

The first axis, profitability, shifts away from traditional capital accumulation toward reinvesting profits into social and environmental goals. It balances economic development with human well-being and environmental preservation, designing strategies that appeal to responsible consumers.

The second axis, financing, strengthens sustainability through diversified funding sources, including state guarantees and collaborative mechanisms such as crowdfunding. Joining official registries and incubator programs opens doors to public and private funds.

The third axis, innovation, incorporates tools and methodologies to enhance customer relations and internal operations. Implementing CRM systems, digital service channels, process automation, and design thinking fosters continuous modernization.

The fourth axis, digital marketing, builds meaningful stakeholder relationships through post-sale follow-ups, experiential marketing campaigns, buyer persona identification, and influencer collaborations.

These four axes operate dynamically across three levels: strategic (defining goals and vision), executive (translating goals into structured workflows), and operational (executing concrete actions).

Finally, all components converge into the transversal axis of benefit and collective interest. This guiding principle ensures that every strategic decision and operational action contributes positively to the community, employees, natural environment, and value chain.

In environmental management, the model emphasizes periodic audits, monitoring greenhouse gas emissions, and implementing circular economy practices (7Rs). Regarding community impact, it promotes purchasing from local or minority suppliers, generating inclusive employment, supporting volunteerism, and closing gender equity gaps. For customers, fair trade practices and transparent pricing build trust, while labor practices focus on fair compensation, professional development, and workplace diversity.

Findings from the 21 reviewed articles engage productively with existing literature on sustainable organizational management, revealing important convergences and research opportunities. Profitability shifts from a sole end goal to a tool for achieving broader societal impact (Stubbs, 2017; De Souza Barbosa et al., 2023). Digitalization acts as a financial performance predictor (Bricio et al., 2018), while alternative financing mechanisms like crowdfunding address traditional funding barriers (Andrade, 2012; Lederman et al., 2015). Innovation in service ventures manifests primarily in process optimization and customer relationship management rather than tangible physical products. Meanwhile, digital marketing serves both commercial positioning and purpose-driven communication functions.

Conclusions

The proposed management model for B Corp service ventures promotes long-term sustainability and economic growth while generating positive impacts across communities, natural environments, and employees. By optimizing resource allocation, adopting digital tools, and automating procedures, service ventures enhance operational efficiency and market resilience.

Implementing CRM systems, design thinking methodologies, and artificial intelligence allows ventures to personalize services, streamline internal communication, and continuously innovate. The transversal axis of collective benefit and interest ensures that economic profitability remains aligned with social equity, environmental stewardship, and sound corporate governance.
