Coexistence of paradigms in public management: case of the State Telecommunications Corporation CNT-EP

Abstract.

The purpose of the writing is to study the coexistence of the paradigms of public management in the case study of the State Telecommunications Corporation CNT-EP. To achieve this objective, a descriptive methodology was applied, based on documentary and bibliographic exploration, as well as the implementation of a survey administered to employees of the public company. The findings, supported by statistical and documentary analysis derived from the survey, confirm the simultaneous coexistence of multiple public management paradigms: New Public Management (managerial), governance-oriented approaches, and the traditional patrimonialist model. This coexistence reflects the presence of organizational subjectivities, historical practices, and institutional dynamics that shape the daily functioning of the entity—knowledge that strengthens the generation of public value in a demanding and constantly evolving environment.

Keywords:

Paradigms, public management, New Public Management, patrimonialism, governance.

Resumen.

El escrito tiene como finalidad estudiar la convivencia de los paradigmas de la gestión pública en el estudio de caso de la Corporación Estatal de Telecomunicaciones CNT-EP. Para cumplir dicho propósito se realizó una metodología descriptiva basada en la exploración documental y bibliográfica, así como el desarrollo de una encuesta aplicada a los colaboradores de la Empresa Pública. Se concluye que sustentado en el análisis estadístico y documental a partir de una encuesta aplicada se confirma la coexistencia y convivencia de múltiples paradigmas de la gestión pública: New Public Management (empresarial), de gobernanza y patrimonialista (Tradicional). Esta convivencia refleja la presencia de subjetividades, prácticas históricas y dinámicas institucionales que influyen en el funcionamiento cotidiano de la entidad, conocimiento que genera valor público en un entorno exigente y en constante transformación.

Palabras clave:

Paradigmas, gestión pública, new public management, patrimonialismo, gobernanza.

Introduction

Organizations, being created by people, bear resemblance to the principles and relationships that exist among those who compose them. This implies agreeing upon and merging principles, methods, beliefs, and values that give meaning to the abstraction of individuals grouped under common objectives and values. This abstraction is not only generated and represented on paper, but acquires a set of qualities that define and project it; in turn, it affects and is affected by the surrounding environment, in what is termed a paradigm.

The word paradigm emerged from Thomas Kuhn's book, The Structure of Scientific Revolutions in 1962. For Kuhn, a paradigm is an accepted model or pattern that functions to "allow the renewal of examples, each of which could serve to replace it," and is "rarely an object of renewal" (Kuhn, 2004, p. 51).

Public management presents theoretical and practical complications due to the incorporation of proposals such as New Public Management (NPM), Governance, and Patrimonialism, whose adoption as public policy depends on the orientation of the government in power. Thus, for NPM, the State can be described as a productive unit, thereby assuming the core mission of promoting broad competition with financial and commercial openness; for Governance, it depends on the level of participation of citizens and actors involved in public processes; and for Patrimonialism, it is reflected in the influence of internal and external power groups on organizational decisions, coexisting with authority directives.

The cited evolutionary path of public administration has enabled us to identify, under paradigmatic logic, the aforementioned models that are currently observed in organizations, either separately or interacting with one another. The object of study is the Public Company of Ecuador, named Corporación Nacional de Telecomunicaciones CNT-EP, regarding how decisions are made based on three public management paradigms: Patrimonialist (traditional), NPM (managerial), and Governance.

Within this framework, the research problem can be formulated as follows: How do patrimonialist, managerial, and governance paradigms coexist, articulate, or come into tension within the institutional management of the Corporación Nacional de Telecomunicaciones CNT-EP, and what implications do these dynamics have for decision-making, organizational efficiency, institutional sustainability, and public value generation in the Ecuadorian telecommunications sector?

This article aims to analyze the coexistence, articulation, and tensions between patrimonialist, managerial, and governance paradigms in the institutional management of the Corporación Nacional de Telecomunicaciones CNT-EP, in order to understand their implications for decision-making, organizational efficiency, institutional sustainability, and the generation of public value in the Ecuadorian telecommunications sector.

Verifying the coexistence of these three outlined public administration paradigms—patrimonialist, managerial, and governance—is justified in the study of the Corporación Nacional de Telecomunicaciones (CNT) because its operation integrates elements characteristic of each approach. As a strategic public enterprise, CNT retains hierarchical structures, formal procedures, and regulatory controls typical of the bureaucratic model; simultaneously, it applies efficiency practices, results-based management, and business criteria associated with New Public Management. Examining this coexistence allows for a precise understanding of the management tensions and institutional challenges facing CNT in delivering telecommunications services within a highly strategic, competitive, and regulated sector.

Management models and organizational challenges in CNT-EP

This section presents a literature review of the main theoretical categories used in the research, as well as a description of the object of study analyzed.

Traditional public and private organizations are characterized by maintaining relations of power and collaboration based on an unquestioned leader who sets the direction and, in some cases, dictates the path. Ladino, in his analysis of patrimonialism in Latin American Administration, reflects on how Weber sought to identify the "motives" behind relations of domination and their permanence in organizations under the categories of "dominants" and "dominated" in social life (Ladino, 2022, p. 2).

The author continues his analysis by explaining that Weber described traditional domination, or so-called "patrimonial bureaucratic administration," as lacking fixed "competence" according to objective rules; lacking a fixed rational hierarchy; lacking professional training (specialization); and not following rules, but rather generating or attributing them to others for personal benefit or for that of followers or affiliates (Ladino, 2022, p. 8).

What has been explained, coupled with the international economic crisis of the 1980s, marked a decisive turning point in rethinking public administration. The traditional bureaucratic model, oriented toward regulatory compliance and structured around rigid hierarchies, demonstrated growing inefficiencies and ineffectiveness due to excessive expansion, high operational costs, and limited adaptability to an increasingly demanding financial and political environment.

In this context, the paradigm of New Public Management emerged as a counterweight to modernize governmental action through managerial criteria inspired by the private sector. As Aguilar notes (Aguilar, 2019, pp. 29-30), this approach focused on two essential goals: "resizing the public sector and [...] restructuring its organization and administration according to a managerial concept and principle that establishes efficiency as the essential value and supreme criterion for the organization, management, and operation of public administrative entities." Thus, NPM sought to overcome the limitations of the bureaucratic model through results orientation, the introduction of evaluation mechanisms, and the strengthening of efficiency as a guiding principle of state functioning.

This conceptual shift paved the way for a broader process of administrative transformation. For Miranda and Soares, this evolution led to a neo-managerialism that deepened and encouraged practices such as privatization of entities, outsourcing of services, and delegated provision of previously state-held activities, consolidating a predominantly regulatory role for government (Miranda & Soares, 2020, p. 450), oriented toward redefining the theoretical and practical foundations of state administration (Bañón & Carrillo, 1997, p. 9).

From the perspective of Governance, public action is conceived as a relational and cooperative process that extends beyond traditional state hierarchy. Fontaine defines governance as a form of regulating relationships among political, social, and economic actors, highlighting exchanges between public and private sectors and strategically oriented political and social change processes (Fontaine, 2015, p. 61).

Under this logic, as Wang points out in a study analyzing comparative perspectives on collaborative and state-centric governance, legitimacy occurs when people accept decisions of a governing body or authority because it promotes collective well-being and social standards (Wang, 2024, p. 72), which must include adopting new technologies and interdisciplinary collaboration to provide a more comprehensive understanding of how subjective aspects and identities influence management (Córdova Montúfar, 2018, pp. 141-190).

This study recognizes the need to incorporate behaviors, subjectivities, and identities into organizational analysis that shape internal institutional life. In this sense, CNT constitutes a relevant case for examining how these dimensions articulate with coexisting management models and influence the organization's capacity to generate public value in a dynamic and strategic sector.

The Corporación Nacional de Telecomunicaciones CNT-EP is governed by the Organic Law of Public Enterprises (Registro Oficial, 2009), aimed at regulating "the establishment, organization, operation, merger, spin-off, and liquidation of non-financial public enterprises acting at international, national, regional, provincial, or local levels; and establishing economic, administrative, financial, and management control mechanisms exerted over them according to the Constitution of the Republic."

This law sought to unify regulations regarding how public enterprises in Ecuador were created, operated, and dissolved, which previously followed diverse mechanisms. In the specific case of public enterprises, Article 315 of the Constitution of the Republic of Ecuador (Asamblea Nacional, 2008, p. 146) established that the State holds the authority to create public enterprises for operations in strategic sectors.

The Corporación Nacional de Telecomunicaciones CNT-EP was created by Executive Decree No. 218 on January 14, 2010, issued by the President of the Republic (Registro Oficial, 2010), as a public law entity with its own assets, endowed with budgetary, financial, economic, administrative, and managerial autonomy, headquartered in Quito, Metropolitan District, Pichincha Province, Ecuador, operating nationwide.

Through Resolution No. DIR-CNT EP-151-2020-565 on December 2, 2020, the Board of Directors of CNT-EP approved the "Project to Amend the Organic Statute by Processes of CNT-EP," which defines three main process groups within the company's Process Map:

  • Strategy, Infrastructure, and Product: development and management of marketing and products, service, resources, and supply chain management.

  • Operations: administration and operation of customer relations, service, resources, suppliers, and business partners.

  • Enterprise Management: Strategy, infrastructure, and product processes, as well as operations processes.

The Statute also establishes that the Organizational Management System of CNT-EP is guided by centralized and decentralized processes depending on the nature of the process, regionalization, customer segmentation, channel development, and product and service convergence.

According to the company's Business Management Report (CNT, 2024, pp. 7-9), staff comprised individuals across different age groups, maintaining a workforce of around 5,800 employees. In Ecuador, voluntary retirement age is 60 with 30 years of service, and mandatory retirement is at 70 with 30 years of service, or after 40 years of service at any age. During the 2020–2024 period, taking 2020 as a baseline, operational revenues decreased by 33.47% (equivalent to USD 252.9 million), whereas payroll expenses increased by 15.51% (equivalent to USD 19.5 million). Applying forecast function analysis under ceteris paribus conditions toward 2030, payroll will grow by 40.8% to USD 176.89 million relative to 2020, representing a shift from 28% to 35% of total revenues, excluding sales costs, administrative expenses, and non-operational costs (CNT, 2024, pp. 10-13).

Human Capital optimization plays a key role in the new organizational concept and efficient management of CNT-EP, reflecting actual operational needs aligned with corporate growth, financial capacity, and securing essential human capital possessing required skills and profiles in line with committed products and services.

Methodology

To understand the research object and analyze the coexistence among public management paradigms, a documentary analysis of current legal frameworks, specialized literature on management models, and systematic information selection was initially conducted.

This process allowed for conceptual delimitation of analytical categories and established the relevance of carrying out a case study—a methodology widely recognized in social sciences for addressing complex phenomena in real-world settings. As noted by Yin (2009) and Stake (1995), case study methodology is ideal when seeking in-depth understanding of organizational processes, considering their interactions, particularities, and internal dynamics.

In line with this perspective, a public institution offering suitable conditions to examine these interactions was selected, viewing the case study as "a research methodology on a specific inter/subject/object with singular functioning, which despite its particular nature must also be explained as an integrated system" (Díaz De Salas, 2011, p. 6). In this sense, the analyzed entity constitutes an empirical unit with specific behaviors embedded within a broader administrative system.

In a second phase, an anonymous survey was administered to 423 individuals to gather employee perceptions and link them to coexisting management models. The survey targeted an institutional universe of approximately 5,800 positions, yielding 390 valid responses. Based on the population size, the minimum sample size requirement is 383, allowing for a 95% confidence level with a 5% margin of error—statistical parameters suitable for organizational studies of this type (Vargas, 2025, p. 8). Empirical data complemented the documentary analysis, contrasting theoretical and normative frameworks with public servants' perceptions regarding predominant paradigms.

Each survey item was structured to gather information regarding company objective, authority type, human talent management, citizen role, transparency level, business innovation, and internal coordination. Items utilized Likert scales with three response options for attitudes, opinions, or perceptions concerning statements tied to Governance, NPM, and Patrimonialism. Survey results indicate a stronger positioning of NPM (managerial), consistent with CNT-EP's nature as a public enterprise, followed by the governance paradigm influence, justified by the process-based management structure with ISO 9001:2015 certification and corporate governance practices. A minor influence of the patrimonialist paradigm is also evident, due to a strong labor union presence influencing internal policy decisions.

This demonstrates that multiple paradigms coexist within the organization; leadership styles and institutional directives cannot be analyzed in isolation, but as a fusion of these approaches. Therefore, CNT-EP must understand how to implement strategies accounting for these subjectivities affecting business outcomes.

The contribution of this analysis is twofold: identifying aspects actively addressed by the company and pointing out areas requiring improvement. Linking NPM and Governance paradigms emphasizes internal and external digital management, promoting innovation and market leadership while fulfilling social mandates.

The case study confirms the simultaneous coexistence of NPM, governance, and patrimonialist paradigms within CNT-EP. Recognizing these dynamics is essential for designing strategies that enhance public value generation. Findings demonstrate that modernization and digital transformation require comprehensive approaches capable of articulating these rationalities beyond dichotomous views.

This study provides relevant empirical evidence for contemporary debates on state capacities in hybrid contexts, reaffirming that sustained public value creation depends on managing these tensions and building adaptive, inclusive governance models.

Conclusions

In conclusion, the following considerations are proposed for similar research inquiries:

Regarding management and organizational culture, internal events should be organized to maximize the practical implementation of hybrid paradigm models, incorporating ethical codes to minimize patrimonialist practices such as nepotism and unjustified discretion.

Concerning internal procedures, decision-making regarding investments, procurement, and human capital should be structured to reduce discretionary actions, establishing clear participation rules and fostering collaborative logic.

For digital governance, implementation of public value indicators—such as efficiency, social responsibility, and budget contribution predictability—should be promoted.

For corporate governance, structured citizen participation mechanisms through binding periodic satisfaction surveys and publication of performance metrics are recommended to increase transparency and trust.
